## 1. How and when was Secunda founded and planned, by whom, and on what principles?

### Takeaway
Secunda is a Sasol company new town: Sasol (Transvaal) Townships was tasked with it in 1974, Swiss architect-planner Max Kirchhofer (planner of Sasolburg) was commissioned the same year, and the first township area was proclaimed on 28 June 1976. The plan is a garden-city / British new-town / Radburn-style layout: residential "quarter cells" bounded by main roads and a continuous network of landscaped green strips, primary schools within 800 m walking distance at each precinct centre, cul-de-sac streets off a circular road, and a compact town centre served by a ring street with parking.

### Cited Findings

**Founding dates and company**
- [CONFIRMED] Sasol's subsidiary Sasol (Transvaal) Townships was tasked with establishing the town in 1974. — [countrytravel.co.za](https://countrytravel.co.za/mpumalanga/secunda)
- [CONFIRMED] The first town area was proclaimed on 28 June 1976. Construction of Sasol Two began in 1976 and the plant was completed in July 1980. — [Sasol heritage page](https://www.sasol.com/celebrating-our-heritage)
- [CONFIRMED] Sasol Three followed in 1982. — [Wikipedia, Secunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunda,_South_Africa)
- [CONFIRMED] The name is from the Latin for "second": the second Sasol coal-to-fuel town after Sasolburg. — [Wikipedia, Secunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunda,_South_Africa)
- [UNCERTAIN, sources conflict] First resident: one source names Mr Lool Odendaal, who moved into the original farmhouse "Tuyshuys" — [Wikipedia mirror](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Secunda_(town)); another names Mr Etienne Smith — [countrytravel.co.za](https://countrytravel.co.za/mpumalanga/secunda).

**Planner**
- [CONFIRMED] Swiss architect and town planner Max Kirchhofer was commissioned by Sasol in 1974 to design the town and envisioned around 13,000 residents; at the time the place had no tar roads or telephones. — [Bloomberg, 19 Jan 2024](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-19/south-african-town-that-rose-with-coal-faces-uncertain-future); syndicated at [BizNews](https://www.biznews.com/news/sasols-secunda-town-built-coal-uncertain-future)
- [CONFIRMED] Kirchhofer's own account is "The planning of Sasolburg and Secunda - achievements and prospects", Town and Regional Planning, 1982 special edition, pp. 1-28. — [UFS journal record](https://journals.ufs.ac.za/index.php/trp/article/view/3171); full-text PDFs at [journals.co.za](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443) and [distantreader.org](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/trp/trp-3171.pdf) (not readable in this session)
- [CONFIRMED] Sasolburg was begun in 1951 on former farmland; a priority there was limiting the disruption caused by motor vehicles. In 1975 responsibility for the "human side of development" passed to the team building the new town of Secunda. — [Kirchhofer 1982, via UFS](https://journals.ufs.ac.za/index.php/trp/article/download/3171/3058/6012)

**Planning principles (from snippets of Kirchhofer 1982 and later studies)**
- [CONFIRMED] Expansibility was a fundamental requirement; each stage of growth was to be "a coherent self-contained entity". — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443)
- [CONFIRMED] "At each stage of the progressive expansion it was possible to maintain a simple coherent layout pattern, contained within a circular road"; a ring road gives access to cul-de-sac streets. — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443)
- [CONFIRMED] Primary schools were placed at the centre of each precinct within a walking distance not exceeding 800 m, so children could reach them without crossing main roads. — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443)
- [CONFIRMED] A continuous system of landscaped green strips runs through the residential areas, rather than isolated parks. — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/trp/trp-3171.pdf)
- [CONFIRMED] The residential areas are called "quarter cells" and are bordered by main roads and green strips. — [Wikipedia, Secunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunda,_South_Africa)
- [CONFIRMED] "Concerted attention was to be given to the creation of a coherent urban scene primarily in the town centre"; the town centre was sized for the town's needs and is fringed by residential areas. — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443)
- [UNCERTAIN, garbled snippet] The town centre measures "700 m x ..." (second dimension cut off) and is served by a ring street that gives access to its parking areas; the paper says this ring street "would also be capable of serving any further industrial expansion to the north-east". — [Kirchhofer 1982](https://journals.co.za/doi/pdf/10.10520/AJA1012280X_443)
- [CONFIRMED] The design drew on garden cities, British new towns and the self-contained neighbourhood concept of Radburn (superblocks separating pedestrian paths from car traffic through interior greenways and cul-de-sacs). — [Geoforum / ScienceDirect, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016718524001866)
- [CONFIRMED] The same 2024 study calls Secunda "perhaps the most pertinent grassroots example" of Davies' (1981) apartheid city model, and says the original spatial structure "remains largely unaltered today". It cites Kirchhofer (1982, p. 22) for a regional layout map. — [ScienceDirect, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016718524001866)
- [CONFIRMED] Secunda (former white town) is separated from eMbalenhle (former black township) by a buffer of about 17 km by the fastest route; the Sasol industrial site, the town of Evander and Winkelhaak Mines form part of that buffer. Secunda is the least dense settlement and eMbalenhle the most dense. — [ScienceDirect, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016718524001866); Wikipedia gives eMbalenhle as 10 km from Secunda — [Wikipedia, Embalenhle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embalenhle) (different measure, not interchangeable)
- [CONFIRMED] A residential area is colloquially named "Die Groenstrook" (The Green Area) and has views of the "Duck Pond" and some of the larger parks of the green network. — [ScienceDirect, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016718524001866)
- [CONFIRMED] A Bloomberg CityLab photo caption (Jan 2024) describes "a tree-lined boulevard" connecting residential neighbourhoods. — [Bloomberg CityLab](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2024-01-19/citylab-daily-a-south-african-coal-town-confronts-a-net-zero-future)
- [CONFIRMED] The plant is "within sight - and often earshot - of the town". — [Wikipedia, Secunda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secunda,_South_Africa)
- [LIKELY] The green strips are still a recognised local feature and a crime concern: Ridge Times headline "Crime spikes in Secunda's green strips", 30 Jan 2025. — [Ridge Times](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-crime-news/2025/01/30/crime-spikes-in-green-strips/)
- [UNCERTAIN, low-quality source] Grokipedia describes the layout as a planned grid with "quarter cells" and linear green belts. — [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/Secunda,_South_Africa)

**Later planning**
- [CONFIRMED] "Secunda New Town" (Secunda Ext 50 and 51): a masterplan for a 1,250-hectare integrated new town between eMbalenhle, Secunda and Evander, identified as a priority urban infill area in Govan Mbeki Local Municipality. — [Urban Scene Architecture](https://www.urbanscene.co.za/projects/p/secunda-new-town-development)
- [LIKELY] Phases one and two were to be at the old mine offices and hostel; the site was described as suited to a government service node, regional retailers and a truck stop. — [Ridge Times, 24 Oct 2019](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2019/10/24/new-town-development-plan-saw-light-years-ago/)

### Inferences
- The level layout can safely use: a compact centre roughly 700 m across in at least one dimension, wrapped by a ring street with surface car parks on the inside edge, with houses starting immediately beyond the ring. This follows from the Kirchhofer snippets, but the exact shape is not confirmed.
- Outside the centre, draw superblock "quarter cells" with looping/cul-de-sac residential streets, a school in the middle of each, and long landscaped green strips threading between and through them carrying footpaths. This is the Radburn pattern the sources say was used.
- Because the same planner applied the same principles as Sasolburg (limiting vehicle disruption, schools reachable without crossing main roads), grade-separated pedestrian crossings and footpaths in the green strips are plausible, but underpasses and cycle paths were NOT confirmed for Secunda in any snippet.
- The mention of industrial expansion "to the north-east" of the centre ring street suggests light-industrial land lies north-east of the CBD; unverified.

### Gaps
- Compass position of the Sasol plant relative to the town, and any prevailing-wind reasoning, were not confirmed by any source reached. (Wikipedia only says the plant is within sight and earshot.) The Kirchhofer PDF almost certainly covers this; it could not be opened.
- Underpasses, cycle paths, pedestrian malls and the exact town-centre dimensions: not confirmed. Check Kirchhofer 1982 pp. 15-28.
- Target population at full build-out, the names and number of original neighbourhoods, and the architects of the first town-centre buildings: not found.
- Whether any road is formally a "ring road" around the whole town (as opposed to the centre's ring street): not confirmed.
- No South African History Online detail beyond a mention of Lake Umuzi was retrieved; no Govan Mbeki IDP/SDF text was retrieved.

## 2. What is in the CBD / town centre today (streets, centres, civic buildings, banks, hotels, taxi rank, fuel)?

### Takeaway
The old CBD is organised around Horwood Street (called "the main street" in a property listing) with Heunis, Nico Diederich(s), Lourens/Louwrens Muller, Walter Sisulu and Steenkamp streets nearby; the municipal head office, library, post office, magistrate's court, Mediclinic and police station are all in or beside it. The newer retail pole is Secunda Mall at the corner of PDP Kruger Street and Oliver Tambo Drive.

## 3. What do the buildings look like (height, materials, era, parking, signage, condition)?

### Takeaway
Almost nothing about physical appearance could be confirmed from text sources in this session. What is confirmed is condition and street life: recurring homelessness in the CBD since at least 2018, potholes and faded markings repaired by private businesses, CCTV and private clean-ups, and theft from parked cars.

## 5. How has the town centre changed since about 2010?

### Takeaway
The dated record shows a new regional mall (Oct 2013) drawing national anchors, a decade of recurring homelessness, potholes and private-sector self-help in the old CBD, and by 2026 an acute municipal water and debt crisis. A causal "mall killed the old centres" story is plausible but was not found stated in any source.

### Cited Findings
- [CONFIRMED] Secunda Mall opened 23 Oct 2013 and by Dec 2013 was reported to have "exceeded all expectations" after 40 days' trading; a listing calls it the town's only regional shopping centre. — [Secunda Mall](https://shopsecundamall.co.za/about-us); [Ridge Times, Dec 2013](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/9095/shops-open-till-late/)
- [CONFIRMED] Potholes were already a resident complaint in Dec 2014. — [Ridge Times, 3 Dec 2014](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2014/12/03/potholes-concern-residents/)
- [CONFIRMED] Timeline of CBD homelessness: removals in 2018, back by 2019-2020, still an issue June 2026 (see section 3). — [Ridge Times, 2020](https://citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2020/03/21/municipality-fails-answer-homeless-people-secunda-cbd); [Ridge Times, 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-news/2026/06/10/concerns-mount-over-vagrant-activity-in-secunda-cbd/)
- [CONFIRMED] 2020-2021: business-led CBD clean-ups, pothole filling, line painting, CCTV. — [Ridge Times, 6 Oct 2020](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2020/10/06/businesses-give-secundas-cbd-a-facelift/); [Ridge Times, 22 Apr 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2021/04/22/imojoe-international-takes-on-clean-up-challenge-secunda-cbd/)
