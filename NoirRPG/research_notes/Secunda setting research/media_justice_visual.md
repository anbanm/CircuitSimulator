## 1. Media: radio, newspapers, and how news travels in Secunda and eMbalenhle

### Takeaway
The one solidly documented local news organ is the Caxton free weekly **Ridge Times** (35 000 copies, Secunda and surrounding towns, online via citizen.co.za/ridge-times). Local radio is thin and poorly documented: there are online or low-profile community stations tied to eMbalenhle and Secunda, but **no currently on-air FM frequency for a Secunda or eMbalenhle community station could be confirmed**; the only FM frequency tied to Secunda in a source is MPowerFM's 105.8.

**How a community station is licensed and funded [SA-GENERAL]**
- A community broadcasting licence lets a **non-profit organisation** serve a community's broadcasting needs. — [gov.za: Apply for a community broadcasting service licence](https://www.gov.za/services/communication/community-broadcasting)
- Licence term conflict: the gov.za service page says a maximum of four years; a 2020 gazette says community sound broadcasting licences are valid seven years. — [gov.za service page](https://www.gov.za/services/communication/community-broadcasting); [Gazette 43077 (2020)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202003/43077gen157.pdf)
- Licence area is the maximum the radio frequency spectrum licence allows and may not exceed a district municipality (here that would be Gert Sibande). Two-phase process: pre-registration, then registration 30 days after the pre-registration decision; incomplete forms are not considered. Evaluation weights include business plan and funding 20%, community participation 20%, technical viability 10%. — [Gazette 42323 (2019)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201903/42323gon439.pdf)
- A station needs two licences: the community sound broadcasting service licence and a radio frequency spectrum licence. In ICASA's most recent round 18 applicants got both. — [ICASA: 18 new community radio stations (2026)](https://www.icasa.org.za/news/2026/icasa-licenses-18-new-community-radio-stations)
- Funding: the MDDA gives grant funding including subsidies to individual community stations plus capacity building; Daily Maverick reports the sector has gone without reliable annual MDDA grants and that ICASA and the MDDA have struggled to function. — [ICASA research report on community broadcasting](https://www.icasa.org.za/uploads/files/Research-Report-on-Regulatory-Framework-for-Community-Broadcasting.pdf); [Daily Maverick: Radio Silence](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/?p=459356)

## 2. Justice institutions: police, courts, mortuary, IPID, private investigators, private security

### Takeaway
Secunda has its own SAPS station and its own magistrate's court; eMbalenhle and Evander each have a SAPS station, and Evander also has a magistrate's court that hears eMbalenhle matters, with serious cases going up to the High Court sitting in Delmas. eMbalenhle SAPS is documented as under-resourced and distrusted, and five of its officers were arrested and bailed in late 2025, which is ready-made material for a police-corruption thread.

**Ranks [SA-GENERAL]**
- Senior management: general, lieutenant-general, major-general, brigadier. Commissioned officers: colonel, lieutenant-colonel, major, captain, lieutenant. Non-commissioned: warrant officer, sergeant, constable, student constable. — [South African Police Service, Wikipedia](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/South_African_Police_Service)
- Major and lieutenant were re-created in 2016. — [Rekord](https://citizen.co.za/rekord/?p=289147)
- Insignia per a 2018 gazette: brigadier three stars in a triangle below a hexagon; colonel two stars below a hexagon; lieutenant-colonel one star below a hexagon; captain three stars; sergeant a shoulder flash with a three-bar chevron below a hexagon and the words "POLICE" and "Sergeant"; warrant officer a shoulder flash with the coat of arms and "POLICE" / "Warrant Officer". — [Gazette 42053 (2018)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201811/42053gon1266.pdf)

**Opening and running a death investigation [SA-GENERAL]**
- Case numbers take the form "CAS 191/09/2021" (sequence / month / year) and are tied to the station where the case was opened. CAS is the Crime Administration System; the newer system is the Integrated Case Docket Management system. — [PMG petition presentation](https://pmg.org.za/files/231128_Presentation_Raseale_Petition_t.pdf); [Daily Maverick, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-03-05-death-is-no-escape-suspects-killed-in-sed-in-shootouts-must-still-be-criminally.md)
- Police frequently open an **inquest docket** first and convert it to a **murder docket** once the post-mortem indicates foul play (Mpumalanga example, Aug 2026). — [EWN, 7 Aug 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/08/07/post-mortem-suggests-st-stithians-pupil-died-of-strangulation-at-mpumalanga-lodge)
- Any unnatural death requires a post-mortem by law; under the Inquests Act the findings may be released only to the SAPS investigating officer. — [Western Cape Government FAQ](https://westerncape.gov.za/general-publication/your-questions)
- Deaths caused by police action or in custody must be reported to IPID; an IPID investigator has testified that where it is clear a person was killed the docket must be registered as murder. — [Daily Maverick, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-03-05-death-is-no-escape-suspects-killed-in-sed-in-shootouts-must-still-be-criminally.md)
- Court sequence seen in the Secunda cases above: first appearance in the magistrate's court, postponement for legal aid, formal bail application on a later date, postponements awaiting DPP instruction, then transfer to the High Court (Delmas). — [Ridge Times, 20 Apr 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-news/2026/04/20/man-appears-in-secunda-court-for-wifes-murder/)

**Forensic pathology and mortuaries**

**Private investigators [SA-GENERAL]**
- Private investigation is regulated as a security service. Working as a PI unregistered with PSIRA is a criminal offence; clients are expected to verify that both the individual and the firm are registered. — [ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/opinion-and-analysis/private-investigations-regulated-as-security-service/)
- Section 15 of the Private Security Industry Regulation Act 56 of 2001 deals specifically with private investigators. — [PSIR Act s15](https://source.acts.co.za/private-security-industry-regulation-act-2001/n305_15__private_investigators.php)
- PIs are bound by the PSIRA Code of Conduct and must act lawfully and respect the Bill of Rights. — [LexBlog](https://www.lexblog.com/?p=3211608)
- In the Netcare matter the High Court in Pretoria held that information gathered by a private investigator was admissible under POPIA where it served the defence of a legal right. — [Moonstone](https://www.moonstone.co.za/netcare-lawsuit-private-investigators-evidence-admissible-under-popia/)

**Firearms for a private person [SA-GENERAL]**
- Firearms Control Act 60 of 2000: section 13 is the "licence to possess firearm for self-defence" (a handgun that is not fully automatic, or a shotgun that is not fully or semi-automatic); section 84 is "carrying of firearm in public place". — [SAFLII consolidated Act](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/consol_act/fca2000192/)
- Secondary summary of section 84: a handgun may be carried in public in a holster attached to the person, in a rucksack or similar holder, or completely covered, with the carrier in effective control; no separate carry permit exists. Applicants must be 21 or older, a citizen or permanent resident, fit and proper, and pass competency tests on the Act plus practical training. — [Firearms regulation in South Africa, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firearms_regulation_in_South_Africa)
- A security business may issue a firearm to a security officer it employs only if both business and officer are PSIRA-registered (among other conditions). Draft amendments tightening the types of firearms and ammunition security firms may hold were published in 2025; final status not confirmed. — [gov.za PSIRA firearm regulations](https://www.gov.za/documents/notices/private-security-industry-regulation-act-regulations-issuing-possession-and-use); [IOL, 11 Apr 2025](https://iol.co.za/news/politics/2025-04-11-psira-clarifies-misconceptions-about-new-firearm-regulations-for-security-firms-in-south-africa/)

**Private security and armed response**
- [SA-GENERAL] PSIRA grades run E (entry, access control and basic patrol) to A (management). Grade D covers gate control, patrolling, searching, incident reports. Grade C covers supervision, armed guarding, control room, alarm monitoring and rapid response, and is the usual minimum for armed response, together with a SAPS firearm competency certificate and a driver's licence. Blog and job-advert sources; they disagree on details. — [Tenders-SA blog](https://www.tenders-sa.org/blog/psira-grading-system-security-service-providers); [Careers24 advert](https://www.careers24.com/jobs/adverts/2070211-armed-reaction-officer-midrand/?jobindex=3)
- [CONFIRMED] Roman Alarms is a security firm based in Trichardt, Secunda, serving domestic and commercial clients. Whether it runs armed response is not stated. — [Procompare: Secunda](https://www.procompare.co.za/armed-response/secunda)
- [CONFIRMED] A security officer appeared in court in the Secunda area in March 2023; gate-motor theft spiked in Secunda in August 2023 (typical suburban crime detail). — [Ridge Times, 9 Mar 2023](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/sports-news/2023/03/09/security-officer-in-court/); [Ridge Times, 24 Aug 2023](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-news/2023/08/24/gate-motor-theft-rises-in-secunda/)

### Inferences
- Jurisdiction split usable in a plot: a body in Secunda town goes to Secunda SAPS and Secunda Magistrate's Court; a body in eMbalenhle goes to eMbalenhle SAPS and (on the bail evidence) Evander Magistrate's Court; the post-mortem most plausibly happens at the Evander forensic pathology unit; a trial goes to the High Court sitting in Delmas. The Evander-mortuary link is an inference from the unit list, not a stated catchment.
- A realistic early beat: the docket is opened as an inquest, a strike or backlog delays the post-mortem, and the conversion to murder comes late. The May 2026 strike makes that timely.
- [UNVERIFIED general knowledge] A PI has no police powers: no warrant powers, no search or seizure, only the citizen's arrest power in the Criminal Procedure Act; may not intercept calls or messages (RICA), may not impersonate police, may not access bank or cell records without consent or a subpoena. Consistent with the sources above but not itself confirmed by a primary text in this session.
- [UNVERIFIED general knowledge] Murder is a Schedule 5 or Schedule 6 bail offence (premeditated murder falls under Schedule 6, where the accused must show exceptional circumstances); detectives are station-based "Detective Service" members who carry the docket; the docket cover is form SAPS 3M. None of this was confirmed by a source here.

### Cited Findings

**Skyline and plant**
- [CONFIRMED] "A defining feature of the landscape around Secunda is the presence of massive cooling towers, tall chimneys, and steel structures" of the oil-from-coal industry; the plants are within view of the town. — [Secunda, Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Secunda_(town))

### Inferences
**Supported by the cited findings**
- Skyline composition: a horizontal band of pipework and steel, a cluster of squat hyperboloid concrete cooling towers with white vapour, and two dominant tall stacks with grey-white plumes that bend with the wind. The plume direction is a storytelling tool: with an easterly wind it lies over eMbalenhle.
- Road texture: patchwork tar, deep potholes on the Evander approach, edge-break and subsidence in Trichardt, fresher black patches where private firms have repaired.
- Underground workers read as white, yellow or orange boiler suits, gumboots, lamp on the helmet, cable to a battery and self-rescuer on the belt.
- Calendar: cosmos in March-May on verges and rail reserves; black burnt strips and smoke columns June-September.

**[UNVERIFIED] Researcher's general knowledge of South African Highveld towns. Not sourced in this session. Verify against Street View and photo libraries before use.**
- *Approaches.* The land is very flat to gently rolling at about 1 600 m, so the plant shows from many kilometres as towers on the horizon before any houses appear. From the N17 (toll road, to the north) it sits to the south across maize fields. From Evander on the R546/R580 the gold-mine headgears and pale tailings come first, then the stacks. From eMbalenhle the plant fills the eastern or north-eastern horizon close up. I recall the main stack being about 301 m, among the tallest structures in Africa; **not confirmed here**.
- *Night.* Thousands of white and sodium-orange work lights on the structures, red aircraft-warning lights on stacks, one or more flares with a flickering orange flame, and lit vapour. Low cloud picks up an orange underglow. No source found describing this.
- *Road furniture.* Left-hand traffic. SADC-style signs: red triangle warnings on white, red-ring circular prohibitions, green direction boards with white text and yellow route numbers (N17, R546, R580) on national and provincial routes, brown tourism signs. Street names on small plates on poles or painted on kerbstones. Traffic lights are called "robots", usually on yellow-and-black or grey poles, often dark or flashing red during power cuts, when the junction is treated as a four-way stop. Four-way stops with "STOP" painted on the tar are very common in the suburbs. Yellow edge lines, white centre lines, speed humps with painted chevrons, minibus taxis stopping on gravel verges.
- *Town plan.* Secunda is a planned 1970s company town: wide streets, greenbelts, single-storey face-brick houses with tiled or corrugated roofs behind walls, a central mall and business district, extensions numbered rather than named. Trichardt is the older farming village beside it; Evander and Kinross are older gold-mining towns.
- *Walls and fences.* Precast concrete panel walls, face-brick walls topped with electric-fence strands and yellow warning plates, steel palisade (often green or black) around schools, substations and businesses, razor wire coils on industrial perimeters, sliding motor gates (note the confirmed gate-motor theft), armed-response signboards on gateposts.
- *Vehicles.* White single-cab and double-cab bakkies (Toyota Hilux, Ford Ranger, Isuzu, Nissan NP200/NP300) dominate; site vehicles carry a tall whip flag with a red or orange pennant, an amber rotating or strobe beacon, red-and-yellow chevron tape on the tail, reflective side strips and a stencilled fleet or call number on the doors. Staff buses in the shift-change convoy. Coal trucks are articulated side-tipper interlinks, typically 34-tonne payload, filthy with black dust, tarpaulined. Taxis are white Quantums or HiAces with a yellow stripe or association sticker. SAPS vehicles are white with blue and yellow markings, a blue light bar and a caged canopy on the back of the van; many are old and dented. Armed-response cars are small hatchbacks or bakkies in company colours with a light bar.
- *Workwear on surface.* Two-piece overalls ("conti suits") in royal blue, navy or orange with silver or yellow reflective bands at the limbs; hard hats white for supervisors and visitors and other colours by role (varies per site, do not fix a code); steel-toe boots; contractor logos on the back.
- *SAPS station.* A low face-brick or plastered building with the SAPS star-and-aloe badge and blue signage, a public counter called the Community Service Centre (older people still say "charge office"), wooden benches, posters, an occurrence book, cells behind, a fenced yard of marked vans and impounded cars, flagpole with the national flag. Uniform is blue shirt and darker blue trousers, with a blue field uniform and cap also common; reflective yellow jackets for roadblocks.
- *eMbalenhle.* Grid of small Sasol-built and state-built ("RDP") block houses, backyard rooms, informal zinc shack sections on the edges, spaza shops trading through a barred hatch in a house or shipping container with soft-drink and cellphone-network branded signboards, taverns and shebeens with beer-brand signage, hair salons, car washes, churches in tents and plain halls, overhead wires and illegal connections, coal smoke from stoves in winter, uncollected refuse and sewage leaks, cattle and goats on verges.
- *Countryside.* Summer (roughly October-March): green maize, sunflowers and soya, tall dark thunderheads building in the afternoon, hail, lightning, red mud. Winter (May-August): frost, straw-blond and brown grass, stubble fields, dry bright days with cold nights, burnt black firebreaks, dust. Constant elements: rows and clumps of blue gum (eucalyptus) on farm boundaries, pans and farm dams, lattice transmission pylons in parallel lines, enclosed overland coal conveyors on trestles crossing farmland, flat-topped grey ash dams and dark coal stockpiles near the plant, pale flat-topped gold tailings and steel or concrete headgears with sheave wheels around Evander and Kinross.
- *Light and air.* A brownish-grey inversion layer on still winter mornings with smoke lying flat; very strong orange and red sunsets through dust and haze; crisp deep-blue sky after summer storms.
