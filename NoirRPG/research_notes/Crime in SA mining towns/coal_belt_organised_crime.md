## 1. Coal theft and coal fraud (trucks, illegal yards, weighbridges, lab results, sabotage, the "cartels", Tegeta/Brakfontein, power-station arrests)

### Takeaway
The best-documented pattern is small and repetitive: contracted truck drivers divert Eskom-bound coal to illegal yards, swap it for discard or rock, and deliver the bad load, often with a supervisor, weighbridge clerk or security guard in on it. Arrests are frequent (2022-2025) but reported convictions are rare; the one heavy sentence found is the Camden sabotage case (20 years effective, January 2026). The "four cartels" story is an unproven private-intelligence claim that the SIU nevertheless said "cannot be ignored".

### Inferences
- The realistic unit of coal crime is the single truckload: a driver, a supervisor, a yard owner, a weighbridge clerk. The "cartel" is the layer that owns the yards, the haulage contracts and the protection, and it is the layer that is never in the dock.
- Detection in the documented cases came from mundane controls: gate security, a quality inspection on delivery, a weighbridge record that did not match gate logs, a tip-off to SARS. A detective plot that turns on a mismatch between two routine records is true to the cases.
- The sabotage motive that has actually been proved in court is commercial, not political: break the plant so the contractor gets the repair work.
- Tax and asset-forfeiture routes (SARS, AFU) appear to move faster than criminal trials.

### Gaps
- Outcomes of the 2022 Kendal, Camden (coal swap) and Matla cases, the 2021 Tutuka fuel-oil case and the 2023 weighbridge cases: not found. Engineering News noted several theft and sabotage cases had been provisionally withdrawn pending more evidence.
- Cost and funder of the intelligence report: not in the sources read. (Background knowledge, unverified in this session: it was privately funded through business donors.)
- No documented case of a bribed coal laboratory resulting in prosecution was found. Brakfontein is the precedent, and it ended in reports and suspensions, not convictions.
- Current status of the Kusile prosecutions was not found. (Background knowledge, unverified here: the case against the former acting CEO was struck off the roll in late 2023 for unreasonable delay.)
- No arrests found at Kriel or Arnot beyond a passing mention in an Eskom roundup.

## 2. Procurement and construction "mafias" and "business forums"

### Takeaway
The 30% demand is a criminal borrowing of a real Treasury rule (30% subcontracting to designated groups on state contracts above R30 million). It began in KwaZulu-Natal around 2014-15, reached mining by 2022, and in Mpumalanga now shows up as small-scale mine extortion arrests (Ermelo 2024, Carolina 2025) and one high-profile case against a taxi boss over "protection fees" from a mine operator (2026). No Mpumalanga conviction was found.

### Cited Findings
- GI-TOC's June 2022 report "Extortion or Transformation? The construction mafia in South Africa" traces the phenomenon to KwaZulu-Natal townships in 2014/15 — [GI-TOC PDF](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/GITOC-Extortion-or-Transformation-The-construction-mafia-in-South-Africa.pdf)
- By 2019 Master Builders South Africa said it had spread to other provinces including Mpumalanga — [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2019-04-12-00-rise-of-the-new-construction-mafia)
- Forums typically demand 30% of contract value for forum members or the forum itself; the figure derives from Treasury's Preferential Procurement framework. Treasury has called the practice illegal, and said the complaints came from the construction sector where contracts exceeded R30 million — [IOL](https://iol.co.za/business-report/economy/2023-06-29-companies-urged-not-to-make-construction-mafia-business-as-usual/); [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/is-sa-inc-fighting-the-construction-mafia-or-adapting-to-incorporate-it-2023-06-27); [Cape Times via PressReader, Aug 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/cape-times/20180808/282239486449244)

## 3. Assassinations and intimidation

### Takeaway
Hired killing is a documented service industry in South Africa, with the taxi industry as its main labour pool and KwaZulu-Natal as the main source of gunmen; Mpumalanga cases repeatedly feature a hitman "from KZN". In the coal belt the clearest case is a Middelburg plant-hire businessman with a mine contract who survived a rifle ambush and a bombing in February 2023 and was shot dead on 2 February 2025; no arrests reported. In provincial government, two officials were arrested in May 2026 for the 2023 killing of an anti-corruption director shot 29 times.

## 7. Mine and plant sale fraud precedents (overstated reserves, competent person's reports, JSE/FSCA, post-acquisition disputes)

This is one side's characterisation, from its own filings — [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001172724/000095012304013549/u48041a5exv99waw38.htm); [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/?p=327559). Note: one search summary misdated this dispute to 2026; the SEC filings fix it at 2004.
- **JSE enforcement against a junior.** In March 2026 the JSE publicly censured a small mining investment company for failing to publish a cautionary announcement about a 2023 acquisition; the Financial Services Tribunal dismissed its reconsideration application; a further censure and suspended R100,000 fine followed in May 2026 for publishing inadequately substantiated allegations of share-price manipulation — [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/jse-censures-mantengu-for-not-disclosing-2023-confidential-information-leak-2026-03-06); [IOL](https://iol.co.za/business/2026-05-29-jse-turns-the-tables-on-mantengu-mining/); [BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/business/876502/r59-million-mining-company-in-hot-water/)

- **Insider trading (coal junior, 2017).** The Financial Services Board (FSCA's predecessor) fined the COO of a listed coal developer for trading after learning on 15 May 2015 that the department had approved a new-order mining right for its flagship project; he resigned — [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/coal-coo-fined-for-insider-trading-in-south-africa-2017-03-16)
- **Post-signing dispute.** A UK court found in Oct 2024 that a South African miner wrongly walked away from a roughly $1 billion Brazilian mine purchase after a pit-wall failure; the seller sought at least $522-600 million — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-10-10-sibanye-loses-uk-suit-over-canceled-1-billion-brazil-mine-deal/); [Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/sibanye-stillwater-liable-to-pay-appian-1-2-billion-for-scrapped-mine-deals/)
- **Mpumalanga sale disputes.** Mpumalanga High Court ruled for the seller in a dispute over transfer of the Lily and Barbrook gold mine assets (July 2019); High Court dismissed a minority shareholder's challenge to the 2018 Maseve mine sale (June 2022) — [Mining Weekly](https://m.miningweekly.com/article/high-court-rules-in-favour-of-vantage-in-mine-sale-dispute-2019-07-18); [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/high-court-dismisses-challenge-against-maseve-mine-sale-2022-06-17)
- Mineral resources and reserves on the JSE must be signed off by a registered "Competent Person" under the SAMREC code — [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Mineral_Reporting_Codes)

### Inferences
- For fiction, the most defensible fraud around a coal asset sale is not a faked drill-hole database but (a) an understated rehabilitation liability or a hollowed-out rehabilitation trust, (b) coal-quality results that flatter the product for an offtake contract (the Brakfontein pattern), and (c) inferred resources presented as reserves (the 2004 dispute).
- A competent person who signs under pressure and later goes quiet is plausible, but I found no real disciplined example to anchor it.

### Gaps
- No JSE or FSCA action over an inaccurate mineral resource statement or CPR was found.
- No SAMREC/SACNASP/ECSA disciplinary case against a competent person was found.
- No post-acquisition lawsuit by a junior coal buyer alleging overstated reserves in Mpumalanga was found. This may exist in arbitration (private) or on SAFLII; not searchable here.

### Inferences
- Secunda's documented crime profile is quieter and more corporate than Witbank's: one dominant employer, a long-running environmental docket, a Hawks commercial-crime office, municipal procurement fraud in the low millions, and violence displaced to the township (eMbalenhle) and the roads (Bethal, N17). Coal-yard and trucking crime sits one district north and west. A Secunda detective story can plausibly draw the Witbank/Middelburg trucking world in through haulage contracts.
- Local community papers (Ridge Times, Highvelder, Middelburg Observer, Witbank News) carry the detail national outlets miss, and their reports are often undated in search extracts.
