## 1. Murder investigation in practice

### Takeaway
On paper a murder runs first responder -> crime-scene manager and Local Criminal Record Centre (LCRC) -> detective -> post-mortem -> forensic lab -> prosecutor -> bail -> trial; in practice each link is overloaded. Nationally only about one murder in nine leads to an identified, arrested suspect (about 11% detection, 2024), detectives carry hundreds of dockets, the DNA and ballistics backlogs run to hundreds of thousands of exhibits and tens of thousands of cases, and trials routinely take years.

### Inferences
- The realistic default for a small-town murder docket is no arrest; an arrest is the exception and a conviction rarer. A protagonist hired by a family is plausible precisely because the Gardee case shows a family doing exactly that in Mpumalanga.
- Because ballistics and DNA results take months to years, cases with an arrested suspect tend to turn on cellphone, vehicle and witness evidence, and they collapse when witnesses disappear (Gardee) rather than on forensic grounds.
- A missing docket is ambiguous in real life: it can be reconstructed, and SAPS keeps no statistics tying missing dockets to failed cases, so corruption and plain disorder are hard to tell apart from outside.

## 2. Police capacity and integrity

### Takeaway
SAPS has thousands of detective vacancies and an oversight body (IPID) with about 170 investigators and a backlog of roughly 14,000-15,000 cases. Since July 2025 the Mkhwanazi allegations, the Madlanga Commission and a parliamentary ad hoc committee have aired sworn evidence of alleged cartel capture of senior police, and public trust in police sits around 27%.

## 3. Commercial crime and corruption investigation

### Takeaway
Fraud runs through a crowded field (station and provincial commercial crime detectives, the Hawks, the SIU, IDAC, the FIC, the AFU, the NPA's Specialised Commercial Crime Unit). ## 4. Private investigators and forensic firms

### Takeaway
Private investigators are regulated as security service providers by PSIRA, have no powers beyond an ordinary citizen's, and work by building a file that is handed to police or prosecutors; courts have accepted that model. Those who investigate corruption (auditors, liquidators, forensic investigators, whistleblowers) have been killed in a visible run of hits from 2021 to 2025, including one forensic investigator in Mpumalanga.

### Cited Findings

**Regulation and lawful scope**
- PSIRA Act 56 of 2001 treats private investigation as a security service; a private investigator is someone who, for reward, investigates another person's identity, actions, character, background or property without that person's consent; investigator and firm must both be registered; s38(3)(g) makes it an offence to hire an unregistered investigator [CONFIRMED-EXTRACT, commentary] — [ProtectionWeb, 15 Dec 2025](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/opinion-and-analysis/private-investigations-regulated-as-security-service/); [Act text, gov.za](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/a56-010.pdf)
- A 2023 government gazette notice on investigation services requires investigation firms to process personal information under POPIA's lawful-processing conditions [CONFIRMED-EXTRACT] — [Government Gazette 49379, GoN 3923](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202309/49379gon3923.pdf)
- RICA s4: a party to a conversation may record it without telling the others (provided it is not for the purpose of committing an offence); "party" can include a silent participant [CONFIRMED-EXTRACT, law-firm alert] — [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, 5 Oct 2020](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2020/Employment/Employment-Alert-5-October-2020-Section-4-of-RICA-The-big-brother-constant-and-the-admissibility-of-secret-recordings.html)
- Recording a conversation one is not party to, including covertly "bugging" an office or meeting room, is generally unlawful interception and an offence unless a party gave prior written consent (s5) [CONFIRMED-EXTRACT, law-firm alert] — [Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Dispute-Resolution/dispute-resolution-alert-24-february-Big-brother-in-the-boardroom-Is-bugging-the-office-lawful-under-RICA)

## 5. Private security in investigations

### Takeaway
Private security staff have only a private person's powers (citizen's arrest under section 42 of the Criminal Procedure Act) yet in practice are first on many scenes and run their own arrests, particularly around mines; deaths in guard-versus-illegal-miner confrontations are documented, and the ISS warned in 2026 of criminal infiltration of the industry.

## 6. Hitmen and contract killing

### Takeaway
Research by the Global Initiative (GI-TOC) describes a professionalised market of violence for hire, rooted in the taxi industry and its KwaZulu-Natal izinkabi, recording 141 targeted killings in 2022 and 131 in 2023. Court-reported fees in recent cases sit in the tens of thousands of rand, and cases are typically cracked when an intermediary or gunman turns state witness under section 204 or takes a section 105A plea.
