## 1. Structure of the criminal economy: diggers, controllers, processing, sale, value

### Takeaway
Research (ENACT/GI-TOC, 2019 and 2022) describes a five-tier pyramid: desperate underground diggers at the bottom, gang bosses above them, then licensed bulk buyers, front-company exporters and international buyers who are largely out of reach of South African police. Gold leaves through scrap-metal and second-hand jewellery dealers and refineries, mostly to Dubai; value estimates range from R7 billion to over R20 billion a year and are not reconciled.

### Cited Findings

**Who the diggers are**
- [C] Zama zamas occupy closed or operational mines to mine gold, coal, iron ore and manganese; sealed shafts are reopened and the workings are unventilated and extremely hot. — [Wikipedia, "Zama zama" (search extract)](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zama_zama)
- [C] A parliamentary committee found most illegal miners were "probably undocumented nationals from Lesotho, Zimbabwe, and Mozambique"; a February 2026 SAPS briefing added Malawi to the cross-border linkages. — [Parliament media statement](https://www.parliament.gov.za/press-releases/media-statement-mineral-and-petroleum-resources-committee-welcomes-natjoints-presantation-coordinated-response-illegal-mining); [Mining Weekly, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/illegal-mining-remains-a-nationally-coordinated-organised-crime-threat-saps-tells-parliamentary-committee-2026-02-24)
- [C] A Chamber of Mines 2016 study (cited August 2017) said 90% of arrested zama zamas were undocumented immigrants. — [News24/Fin24, 13 Aug 2017](https://www.news24.com/fin24/economy/disused-secunda-gold-mines-become-zama-zama-hot-spot-20170813-3)
- [R] "Around 30 000 illegal miners work in and around thousands of disused and active mines"; they are organised by criminal syndicates; the mining is "principally carried out underground in industrial shafts"; diggers face "extortion, murder, forced migration, money laundering, corruption, racketeering, drugs and prostitution". — [ISS Today, Alan Martin, 19 Jun 2019](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/solving-south-africas-violent-and-costly-zama-zama-problem)
- [R] Gold-industry employment fell from 190,000 in 2008 to 95,000 in 2019; underground illegal miners were first reported in 1999 in abandoned shafts at Welkom. — [Daily Maverick, Julian Rademeyer (GI-TOC), 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa)
- [R] Recruitment by deception and force: Mozambican men who surfaced at Stilfontein in November 2024 said they were offered casual work at roadside labour spots in Middelburg (Mpumalanga), Diepsloot and Alexandra by Basotho recruiters in a white minibus, threatened with guns when they asked questions, and ordered underground. One gave his age as 14. A local "cage operator" working for a gang estimated a third of the diggers he saw were coerced, mostly Mozambicans and Zimbabweans. — [GroundUp, Kimon de Greef, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)
- [R] Local participation: the same cage operator grew up in Khuma township (Stilfontein), son of a retrenched Mozambican migrant mineworker; he first dug at the surface, then worked for a Basotho gang lowering men by rope and carrying provisions underground for R8,000 a month. — [GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)
- [R] Lesotho context: 50% poverty and 33% unemployment (2021, African Development Bank); young men from the Mafeteng area follow fathers and grandfathers to the South African reefs; families depend on remittances and often never recover a body. — [Daily Maverick, Hamilton Wende, 26 Mar 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-03-26-the-bloody-threads-that-bind-basotho-gangs-to-zama-zamas-and-famo-musicians-in-remote-mountain-outposts/)
- [C] ENACT argues illegal mining in South Africa should be understood as artisanal mining inside large-scale mines, and that the motives of destitute diggers differ sharply from those higher up. — [Mail & Guardian via PressReader, 12 Aug 2022](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/mail-guardian/20220812/281547999669589)

**Who controls them (tiers)**
- [C] Five-tier hierarchy as summarised by Mail & Guardian: (1) individual miners; (2) gangs and illegal mining bosses; (3) bulk buyers at national/regional level operating as licensed or registered entities; (4) front-company exporters; (5) international (extract truncated at tier 5). — [Mail & Guardian via PressReader, 12 Aug 2022](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/mail-guardian/20220812/281547999669589)
- [C] Tiers four and five "frequently operate from outside South Africa, beyond the reach of law enforcement"; it is people "whose occupations give them the legal right to possess and process gold" who launder illicit gold into the legal supply chain. — [ENACT policy brief "Uncovered: The dark world of the Zama Zamas" (tabled in Parliament, 8 Aug 2022)](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Pages/2022/7-August/8-08-2022/Ministerial_Briefing_Illegal_Mining/Policy%20Brief_Uncovered_The%20dark%20world%20of%20the%20Zama%20Zamas.pdf)
- [R] "The illicit gold trade is loosely organised into a pyramid structure. At the top are white-collar criminals who launder gold from zama zamas into the legal gold market, with a hierarchy of buyers and middlemen beneath them. The miners are at the bottom." Heavily armed gangs from Lesotho muscled in on shafts over two decades and operate as extortion rackets: at Stilfontein, working an abandoned mine became impossible without paying steep fees. Local gold buyers often front those fees for a cut of the gold. — [GroundUp, Kimon de Greef, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)
- [R] Debt bondage: many diggers become trapped in debt underground, worsened by food paid for in gold; some stay months or years and leave with "nothing". Syndicates sell food, gumboots, batteries and shovels underground "at hugely inflated prices". One zama zama said it cost about R10,000 to enter and R25,000 to return. (Anonymous testimony.) — [GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)
- [R] Some Lesotho groups operate as "specialised protection rackets, robbing gold from other illegal miners or working as gunmen for syndicates". Syndicates launder proceeds into "lodges, taxi businesses, liquor stores and guesthouses" (mine security manager, anonymous). — [Daily Maverick, Julian Rademeyer, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa)
- [R] Access to **operating** mines is bought: a whole "line" of six to seven employees (surface security guards, banksmen, winding-engine drivers, miners) is paid off, averaging R65,000-R70,000 per zama zama in the Free State and Gauteng; mineworkers are paid up to R15,000 for use of their access cards. One leading Gauteng mining house apprehends about 200 employees a quarter for aiding illegal miners; security officials believe the true number is at least five times higher. — [Daily Maverick, Julian Rademeyer, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa)
- [C] Hawks cases describe "runners" who recruit foreign nationals as zama zamas and sell gold-bearing material on to illegal gold buyers. — [TimesLIVE, 28 Jan 2022](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2022-01-28-eleven-zama-zamas-arrested-assets-worth-r3m-seized-by-hawks/)
- [C] Police in August 2023 used the term "kingpins" loosely: the police minister said 89 "suspected illegal mining kingpins" had been arrested in the Riverlea operations. — [TimesLIVE, 2 Aug 2023](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2023-08-02-more-than-20-illegal-miners-arrested-in-riverlea-says-bheki-cele/)

**Processing and sale**
- [C] Surface processing equipment seized in raids includes "pendukas" (improvised crushing drums), gas cylinders and generators. — [SAnews, Aug 2023](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/64641)
- [C] Boksburg, 5 July 2023: a toxic gas leak in Angelo informal settlement killed 17 people including three children; the Gauteng premier said the gas was being used by illegal miners to extract gold from soil. Two security sources told Daily Maverick they doubted that explanation; EMS first counted 24 dead before a recount gave 17. — [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/the-death-toll-from-a-south-africa-gas-leak-blamed-on-illegal-gold-processing-rises-to-17); [Daily Maverick, 6 Jul 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-07-06-boksburg-gas-leak-death-toll-rises-to-17/); [Al Jazeera, 10 Jul 2023](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2023/7/10/i-lost-six-people-south-africans-shocked-by-latest-mine-leak)
- [C] Syndicates use scrap-metal and second-hand jewellery dealers and refineries to move gold; it is smuggled mainly via Eswatini and Mozambique to Dubai, Karachi and Mumbai. — [Business Day, 31 Dec 2018, summarising a Global Initiative report](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/national/2018-12-31-great-mine-heist-how-sas-gold-has-been-looted-on-an-industrial-scale/)
- [C] A 2019 Global Initiative report called the local scrap-metal-to-refinery route to Dubai the preferred route for most syndicates; illegal gold is melted with legitimate gold at refineries operating as fronts, making it untraceable. — [Daily Maverick, 16-17 Jan 2021, "All that glitters"](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2021-01-17-all-that-glitters-a-look-into-illicit-gold-networks/)
- [R] Johannesburg is the hub. Gold is laundered by second-hand scrap-metal dealers and jewellers licensed to buy and sell gold; second-hand jewellery and registered precious-metals dealers play a greater role in **financing** the activity than scrap dealers. There are "at least 100 small refineries in Gauteng alone, many of them on the West Rand" (small, not necessarily illegal). Analysts and police say the SA Diamond and Precious Metals Regulator "shies away from its regulatory mandate". — [Daily Maverick, Julian Rademeyer, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa)
- [R] The VAT mechanism: second-hand gold sales are tax-exempt, so newly mined illegal gold is melted to jewellery purity and passed off as recycled gold, and merchants fabricate transactions to claim VAT refunds. Because of this, "much of the gold tends to be laundered into formal supply chains in South Africa rather than being smuggled out". Two companies claimed R24.4 billion in allegedly fraudulent VAT refunds between 2012 and March 2020 (amaBhungane, cited). — [Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa)
- [R] Export: primarily to the UAE, less to India and China. South Africa declared no doré exports between 2007 and 2017, yet UAE import data shows more than 34 tonnes arriving from South Africa in 2012-2016, "probably exported legally after being processed by small refineries in Gauteng". Front export companies linked to Dubai refineries are hard to prosecute because they sit outside South African jurisdiction. — [Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa); [ISS Today, 19 Jun 2019](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/solving-south-africas-violent-and-costly-zama-zama-problem)
- [R] Gold refining inside settlements: at Jumpers (Cleveland, Johannesburg) most illicit activity "was related to refining gold", and one June 2026 massacre victim was found in a dwelling where illicit gold was processed. — [Daily Maverick, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-06-10-fingers-pointed-at-illegal-miners-as-police-hunt-cleveland-mass-shooters/)
- [C] SwissAid (reported 30 May 2024): gold reaches Dubai in hand luggage, with false papers, sometimes by private jet; Dubai has at least 20 refineries and over 7,000 precious-metal traders. — [Mining Weekly, 30 May 2024](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/billions-in-african-gold-smuggled-to-uae-yearly-swissaid-says-2024-05-30)

**Value**
- [C] ENACT/GI: lost gold production "far exceeds R14 billion a year". — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/zama-zamas-try-their-luck-hit-r14bn-a-year-gold-bonanza); [ENACT policy brief](https://www.parliament.gov.za/storage/app/media/Pages/2022/7-August/8-08-2022/Ministerial_Briefing_Illegal_Mining/Policy%20Brief_Uncovered_The%20dark%20world%20of%20the%20Zama%20Zamas.pdf)
- [C] Chamber of Mines / Minerals Council: more than R20-21 billion a year in lost sales, taxes and royalties. — [News24/Fin24, 13 Aug 2017](https://www.news24.com/fin24/economy/disused-secunda-gold-mines-become-zama-zama-hot-spot-20170813-3); [ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/solving-south-africas-violent-and-costly-zama-zama-problem)
- [R] Minerals Council CEO (October 2021): "annual estimated commercial value of illegal mining, and illicit dealings in precious metals and diamonds" is R7 billion to R20 billion, plus R2.5 billion a year in industry security costs. — [Daily Maverick, Ed Stoddard, 7 Oct 2021](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2021-10-07-wild-north-west-six-killed-in-shootout-after-hundreds-of-zama-zamas-forced-to-surface-in-orkney/)
- [R] ISS/ENACT (2019): Minerals Council estimates R21 billion a year in lost sales, taxes and royalties; ENACT puts zama zama output "in excess of R14 billion a year". — [ISS Today, 19 Jun 2019](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/solving-south-africas-violent-and-costly-zama-zama-problem)
- [R] Shaft-level estimate (mine security expert, Orkney, "conservative"): a single shaft could hold 500 men each mining 30 grams a week; at a black-market price of about R1,000 a gram that is upwards of R60 million a month per shaft, and Stilfontein has more than a dozen abandoned shafts. Analysts estimate illegal mining at about a tenth of national gold output, roughly ten tonnes, worth more than R15 billion at late-2024 prices. — [GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)

### Inferences
- The three value figures (R7bn, R14bn, R20-21bn) measure different things (gold output vs. sales+tax+royalties vs. all precious metals) and should not be presented as a trend.
- For fiction: the people a town detective can physically reach are tiers 1-2 (diggers, gang bosses, local buyers, surface processors). Tier 3 is where the story becomes white-collar: a licensed dealer or refinery whose paperwork is in order.
- The debt mechanism (entry fee advanced by a buyer, food sold underground for gold) explains why diggers stay down for months and why "voluntary" and "forced" labour blur.

## 2. Key places and events (dated)

### Takeaway
The best-documented events are Stilfontein (Aug 2024 - Jan 2025, at least 78 bodies recovered in the final rescue, totals of 87-93 reported), Krugersdorp (July-Oct 2022, charges withdrawn against all 14 accused) and Riverlea (July-Aug 2023, five bodies, 194 arrests). For **Evander/Secunda** the sourced record is thin: a 2017 News24 feature, an Embalenhle arrest in April 2025 and local-paper items, with no major documented mass-casualty event found.

### Cited Findings

**Stilfontein / Buffelsfontein (North West), 2024-2025**
- [R] The Stilfontein mine ceased operations in 2013. In Stilfontein, Vala Umgodi "began in August 2024, when the national police started blocking off key shafts and stopping the entry of essential supplies". Shaft 11 is about 2 km deep with 19 levels; some men fell to their deaths trying to climb Shaft 10; the open Margaret Shaft was not reachable from Shafts 10 and 11. Police only collected camera evidence and began two-way notes with miners in November 2024. — [ISS Today, Vanya Gastrow, 16 Jan 2026](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/stilfontein-mine-disaster-the-deadly-cost-of-information-failure); [Wikipedia, "Stilfontein mine deaths"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilfontein_mine_deaths)
- [R] By 28 Nov 2024 more than 1,300 miners had been arrested; more than 1,200 were from Mozambique and Zimbabwe; about 100 were undocumented children. Most came up via the elevator of a nearby functioning shaft. Police argued the men were not trapped and "simply refuse to resurface because they are avoiding arrest". — [GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/)
- [R] In November 2024 community members set up a manual pulley system that retrieved some miners; it was too slow for the hundreds below. A police officer later alleged task-force members removed the community's ropes. — [ISS Today, 16 Jan 2026](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/stilfontein-mine-disaster-the-deadly-cost-of-information-failure); [GroundUp, 18 Jun 2025](https://groundup.org.za/article/these-human-bodies-are-like-pork-zama-zama-miner-tells-how-trapped-miners-in-stilfontein-ate-flesh-to-survive/)
- [U] An Al Jazeera extract dates a court order allowing food to be sent down to October 2024; the full-text sources above place the pulley rescues in November 2024. Exact dates of the 2024 court orders not verified. — [Al Jazeera, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/1/16/did-south-africa-starve-dozens-of-trapped-miners-to-death)

- [R] SAPS testimony to the SAHRC, October 2025: most of those who resurfaced were human-trafficking victims and 27 were children; most bodies were too decomposed to say whether death preceded the police intervention; SAPS said it continued the tactic of closing shafts and stopping food and water "long after Stilfontein". — [ISS Today, 16 Jan 2026](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/stilfontein-mine-disaster-the-deadly-cost-of-information-failure)

### Inferences
- Evander/Secunda illegal gold mining appears, on the sourced record, to be mostly **surface re-digging of old dumps and shallow ground near settlements** (Embalenhle sports field, 2025; "mounds of soil", 2017), policed by small local operations and fines, rather than deep-shaft sieges. A fictional Evander deep-shaft standoff would be invention, borrowing from Stilfontein, Sabie or Sheba.
- Mpumalanga has two distinct economies: gold in the Lowveld escarpment (Barberton, Sabie, Pilgrim's Rest), which is armed and gang-run, and coal on the Highveld (Ermelo, Emalahleni), which is poorer, more local, and kills mainly through drowning, collapse and fire.
- Death tolls in this field are systematically unstable: bodies are brought up by other miners and left at the surface, identification is slow, and community counts exceed police counts.

## 3. Violence patterns

### Takeaway
The documented pattern is factional war between Lesotho-rooted groups (Terene and Seakhi, with splinters), fought both underground and in townships and taverns, plus coercion of the diggers themselves; attribution of specific massacres to named factions is usually disputed. Corruption cases against police and mine security exist but are few relative to the allegations.

### Cited Findings
- [R] The famo gangs' roots are traced to the Marashea ("Russians"), Basotho miners' gangs of the 1940s; the feud turned deadly in the early 2000s after one famo (accordion music) musician allegedly shot another; "some say the two main gangs, Seakhi and Terene, began as funeral societies"; in 20 years dozens of singers, DJs, wives and children have been killed, and the gangs are now "aligned to illegal mining gangs" on the Reef. Named factions include Terene ea Chakela and Terene ea Khosi Mokata, whose leader was reported to have fled to South Africa. A 2018 attack on the Lesotho village of Ha Molungoa killed 15 people, four of them children, after a family feud drew in Seakhi members; the village was largely abandoned. Famo gangsters hand out money at political rallies in Lesotho. A Terene member interviewed is both a zama zama and a recording musician, hides his home address even from fellow gang members, and remits money monthly. One mother's son was abducted, tortured for a week and thrown down an abandoned shaft; police said the body could not be retrieved. — [Daily Maverick, Hamilton Wende, 26 Mar 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-03-26-the-bloody-threads-that-bind-basotho-gangs-to-zama-zamas-and-famo-musicians-in-remote-mountain-outposts/)

### Inferences
- Violence spills from shafts into taverns and informal settlements because that is where faction members sleep and drink; a detective would meet it as a "tavern shooting" docket with a mining motive nobody will state on record.
- Police statements routinely hedge motive ("cannot be ruled out"), and faction leaders publicly deny involvement. Unsolved and disputed attribution is the realistic default.
- Seized weapons in everyday raids are mostly handguns and few in number relative to arrests; rifle fire is reported by residents far more than rifles are recovered.

## 5. State response

### Takeaway

- A realistic local detective is under pressure to produce arrest numbers for weekly Vala Umgodi tallies, most of which become immigration cases or small fines, while murder dockets from the same sites go unsolved.

## 6. Who profits, according to investigators and researchers

### Takeaway
Named reports consistently say the profit sits with licensed or semi-licensed intermediaries (bulk buyers, scrap and second-hand gold dealers, small refineries, exporters) and Dubai-based buyers, enabled by corrupt officials and weak regulation; the extracts retrieved do not name specific South African politicians or large licensed mines as beneficiaries.

### Inferences
- The documented corruption is low- and mid-level (detectives, sergeants, a mine guard, officers who let a suspect walk). Claims about senior police, politicians or major mining houses profiting are, in the material retrieved, structural arguments by researchers rather than proven cases. Fiction that puts a named-rank official at the top is plausible but goes beyond the public record.
- The paper trail that would connect a town-level buyer to an exporter runs through second-hand gold licences, refinery intake records and export permits; this is where investigators say the state does not look.

### Gaps
- No extract named a specific licensed mine, refinery or politician as a proven beneficiary of zama zama gold. I could not read the full reports to check whether they do.
- Bribery of operating-mine employees is now sourced in aggregate (Daily Maverick, Aug 2022: "line" of six to seven staff, R65,000-R70,000; about 200 employees a quarter caught at one mining house), but the only named case retrieved is the Kusasalethu guard (2017).
- No retrieved source quantified how much illicit gold comes from active mines versus abandoned ones.
