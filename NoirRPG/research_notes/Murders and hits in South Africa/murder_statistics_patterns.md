## 1. Totals and rates: 1994 to 2025/26, provinces, international comparison

### Takeaway
The murder rate fell by more than half from about 67 per 100,000 in 1994/95 to a low of about 30 in 2011/12, climbed for a decade to about 45 in 2022/23 (27,000+ murders a year), and has since fallen for three straight years to 36.6 per 100,000 in 2025/26 (roughly 23,100 murders, about 63 a day). Eastern Cape and Western Cape have the worst rates (about 60 per 100,000), Limpopo the lowest (about 12); Mpumalanga sits below the national rate (20.7 in 2025/26, after a 2023/24 spike to 31.4).

### Inferences
- Summing the four quarterly counts gives about 23,096 murders for 2025/26 (my arithmetic, not an official total; quarterly figures are later revised). This squares with ISS's "16% fewer than 2023/24" (27,621 x 0.84 = about 23,200) and with 36.6 per 100,000.
- At 20.7 per 100,000 and roughly 5 million people, Mpumalanga's 2025/26 toll would be on the order of 1,000 to 1,100 murders; the 2023/24 spike (31.4) would have been around 1,500 to 1,600. These are back-of-envelope estimates.
- For a story set in Mpumalanga around 2023/24, the province was at its worst murder rate since 2004; by 2025/26 it was back near its 2014–2019 level.

### Gaps
- No official single-number 2025/26 annual murder count was found (only the rate and the four quarters).

- Counts for 1994/95–2010/11 and 2019/20–2020/21 were not individually sourced; use the rate table.

## 2. Circumstances: motives, places, days and times, alcohol

### Takeaway
In every recent SAPS quarterly "causative factor" sample, arguments (including road rage and provocation) are the largest identified motive by a wide margin, followed in shifting order by robbery, vigilantism/mob justice, retaliation/revenge and gang killings (almost entirely Western Cape); taxi and hijacking murders are in the dozens per quarter. Roughly half of murders happen in public places, about three in ten in a home, a few percent at liquor outlets; they cluster on weekend nights and are heavily tied to alcohol.

### Inferences
- The identified-motive categories add up to well under half of all murders each quarter; the remainder are undetermined. Any "X% of murders are arguments" claim should be phrased as a share of the sample or of known motives (arguments were about 21% of all Oct–Dec 2024 murders and 17% of Jan–Mar 2026 murders by my arithmetic).
- A realistic "typical" South African murder is a weekend-night stabbing or shooting between acquaintances near a tavern or in a yard, not a planned hit.

### Gaps
- SAPS does not publish a murder-specific alcohol share in the material I could read; the 7,267 figure is a combined four-crime count.
- "Domestic violence" and "contract killing" murder counts by quarter were not found in readable sources (SAPS presentations list domestic-violence-related murders, but I could not open them).
- Time-of-day data newer than 2018/19 was not found.

## 3. Weapons: firearms versus knives, and where illegal guns come from

### Takeaway
Guns are now the main murder weapon: about 40–44% of murders in 2023/24 to 2025/26 (9,398 firearm murders in 2025/26), with knives a distant second at about 15–17%. Almost all gun murders are committed with unlicensed weapons, fed by theft and loss from civilians, private security and the state, including police evidence stores.

## 4. Victims and perpetrators: sex and age, femicide, children, police, farms, councillors

### Takeaway
Victims and perpetrators are overwhelmingly young men; about 2,400 women are killed a year (roughly seven a day), about 60% of them by intimate partners, and in 44% of femicide cases no perpetrator is ever identified. Farm murders number in the tens per year (under 1% of murders) and are driven mainly by robbery; the "white genocide" framing is rejected by fact-checkers. Political assassinations hit a record in 2026.

### Cited Findings
**Sex and age:**
- "A lot of the victims are young men, anywhere between 17 and about 35 or 40 years old" (David Bruce). [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/three-lives-lost-every-hour-despite-murder-drop/)
- 90% of firearm homicides globally are committed by men. [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/opinionista/2025-02-25-unpacking-the-deadly-mix-behind-south-africas-violent-gun-driven-crime-epidemic/)
- High-murder precincts have disproportionately more male residents and more people in their twenties. [FULL] — [ISS, First in line for murder](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/first-in-line-for-murder)

**Femicide (SAMRC mortuary-based national surveys):**

[EXTRACT] — [PLOS Medicine 2024 via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10796052), [SAMRC brief](https://www.samrc.ac.za/sites/default/files/attachments/2022-09/DecreaseFemicideSouthAfricaResearchBrief.pdf)

## 5. Geography: precinct concentration, top stations, mining and industrial towns, Mpumalanga

### Takeaway
Murder is extremely concentrated: half of all murders occur in fewer than 170 of about 1,175 station areas, overwhelmingly dense urban townships and informal settlements in Cape Town, eThekwini, Gqeberha and Gauteng. No Mpumalanga station is currently in the national murder top tier; the province's violence shows up instead in Lowveld township precincts (Calcutta, Pienaar, KaBokweni, Masoyi, Tonga), the Highveld coal and petrochemical belt (eMbalenhle, Vosman/Witbank, Ermelo) and illegal-mining shootouts on the escarpment.

Multiple-victim shootings grew sharply after 2019/20 and are now tracked as a distinct organised-crime phenomenon: GI-TOC counted 45 mass killings (three or more dead) with at least 210 deaths in the first nine months of 2026 alone. Taverns are the recurring venue; explanations offered are gang and taxi turf, illegal mining, extortion and revenge, enabled by rifles and weak crime intelligence.

- Explanations: control of criminal markets and territory; reprisal attacks where bystanders are caught; local police outgunned and sometimes on syndicate payrolls ("they use police vehicles to ferry gold ore"); mass arrests of low-level workers leave financiers untouched; disarray in crime intelligence (Julian Rademeyer, GI-TOC). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-09-27-17-shot-in-joburg-10-in-cape-town-2026-mass-killings-toll-now-196/)
- Wikipedia summary: mass shootings are mostly by gangs over turf and resources, illegal mining and taxi conflicts, often as revenge, and taverns are often targeted. [FULL] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mass_shootings_in_South_Africa)

### Inferences
- Definitions differ (SAPS "multiple murder" = two or more; GI-TOC mass killing = three or more dead; GFSA = four or more), so counts cannot be compared across sources or years.
- Many victims are incidental: a hit on one person in a crowded tavern produces a massacre. This is a plausible structure for a fictional case in which the true target is obscured.

### Gaps
- No consistent annual count of mass shootings for 2022, 2023 and 2024 on one definition was found; GFSA's "more than 80 in 2024" is [EXTRACT] only.
- The Soweto death toll appears as 16 (ISS, Wikipedia) and 15 (The Citizen); 16 is the better-sourced figure.

## 7. Justice outcomes: detection, conviction, delays, capacity

### Takeaway
About nine in ten murders go unpunished: the SAPS murder detection rate is about 11%, and convictions measured against reported murders run at roughly 10–15%, even though the NPA's in-court conviction rate looks high because it enrols only winnable cases. Explanations centre on too few and overloaded detectives, collapsed crime intelligence, forensic backlogs and court delays.

### Cited Findings
- Detection rate for murder (suspect arrested and charged) "a meagre 11%"; conviction rate lower; SAPS has about 18,000 detectives with 4,000 more being recruited; some carry 200 or more dockets; 16 specialised detective units in 2024. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/detective-task-teams-a-stopgap-response-to-sa-s-crime-crisis)
- Detection 11% in 2024; murder conviction rate 13% over the past five years (Gareth Newham). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/)
- "Only around 12% of cases lead to convictions"; the NPA's own rate is high because it only enrols cases with a prospect of success. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/three-lives-lost-every-hour-despite-murder-drop/)
- "Only 15% of murder cases are solved" (ISS, 2023). [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/reducing-murder-must-be-a-top-sa-government-priority)
- As of 2020 fewer than 20% of murder investigations resulted in a trial date, down from 31% in 2010/11. [FULL, Wikipedia] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)
- Station level: SAPS proposed a murder detection target of 11.33% in April 2025; among 35 high-profile stations, 27 were under 10%; Nyanga 1.4%, Jeppe 1.4%, Johannesburg Central 1.8%, Hillbrow 2.5% (Hillbrow's in-court conviction rate 16%); Temba 18%; Inanda 11.3%; Thohoyandou fell from 35.1% to 19.25% and Mankweng from 32.5% to 17.7% as budgets were cut about 25%. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/heres-how-many-murders-successfully-investigated-police/)
- Oct–Dec 2025: "more than 550 murderers" sentenced to prison in a quarter with 6,351 murders. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/murder-continues-to-decrease-but-crime-stats-still-unacceptably-high/)
- Forensics: nearly 250,000 DNA exhibits overdue; only 1.5% of 2025/26 DNA profiles were finalised within the statutory 30 days; SAPS reports against a 90-day target instead; about 166,000 reference samples sit in storage; cases are struck off the roll when reports are missing. [FULL] — [Daily Maverick, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-15-only-15-of-saps-dna-samples-are-processed-within-target-time/)
- Toxicology backlog about 40,000 cases. [EXTRACT] — [AOL/AFP](https://www.aol.com/articles/unbearably-long-wait-justice-leaves-000251941.html)
- Courts: 248 judges in 2022/23, only 2% more than 2011/12 despite about 20% population growth; a trial of 17 police officers for a 2018 death in custody was set down only for June 2025. [FULL] — [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/our-justice-system-is-grinding-to-a-halt/)
- Some stations have more than 300 open dockets per detective (DA claim). [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/murder-convictions-in-sa-remain-low-as-poor-policing-leaves-cold-cases/)
- Femicide: no perpetrator identified in 44% of cases (2020/21). [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/whos-killing-sa-women-femicide-rates/)
- Extortion: 6,056 cases in five years to March 2024, 2,389 arrests, 178 convictions. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/opinion/sa-crime-shifts-kidnappings-and-extortion-skyrocket/)
- Police integrity: the Madlanga Commission and a parliamentary ad hoc committee are examining claims that a drug cartel infiltrated policing and politics, after KZN commissioner Mkhwanazi's July 2025 allegations. [FULL] — [Daily Maverick, 28 Nov 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-11-28-murders-in-sa-drop-slightly-police-relee-release-crime-figures-amid-drug-cartel/)
- Recommendations: focus on the people, places and behaviours tied to lethal violence, with station-level murder-reduction plans (ISS); rebuild the general detective service instead of multiplying task teams (ISS); fund family-violence prevention and parenting programmes (ISS); recover illegal firearms and tighten the Firearms Control Act (GFSA); prosecution-led investigations and conviction-based performance measures (DA); follow the money, faster prosecutions, witness protection, professionalised police (minister). [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/reducing-murder-must-be-a-top-sa-government-priority), [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/detective-task-teams-a-stopgap-response-to-sa-s-crime-crisis), [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis), [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-11-28-murders-in-sa-drop-slightly-police-relee-release-crime-figures-amid-drug-cartel/)

### Inferences
- 550 imprisoned murderers against 6,351 murders in the same quarter is about 9%, in line with the 10–13% conviction estimates (different cohorts, so indicative only).
- A fictional small-town detective plausibly carries 100–300 dockets, waits months for DNA and ballistics, and loses cases to missing lab reports. An arrest is the exception.

### Gaps
- No figures were found on inquest dockets (how many unnatural deaths are opened as inquests instead of murder, or reclassified), nor a measured median time from murder to trial or verdict. I found no reliable source on either.
- The 11%, 12%, 13% and 15% figures use different definitions and years; none could be traced to a readable SAPS or NPA table.

## 8. Vigilante and mob killings

### Takeaway
Mob justice is one of the top three to five identified murder motives: SAPS attributed 849 murders to it in 2017/18, rising to 2,124 in 2022/23, and still about 300–390 per quarter since. Triggers are suspected theft, robbery, rape, witchcraft accusations and suspicion of foreigners, in places where people do not expect police to act.

### Cited Findings
- Murders ascribed to vigilantism/mob justice: 849 (2017/18), 789 (2018/19), 1,202 (2020/21), 1,353 (2021/22), 2,124 (2022/23; quarters 472, 528, 536, 588). [FULL, Wikipedia citing SAPS quarterly reports and Kemp, Why We Kill, 2024] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)
- Quarterly: 389 (Oct–Dec 2024); 299 (Jan–Mar 2026). [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/saps-crime-statistics-leading-causes-of-murder/), [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/)
- Where: Gauteng 90, KZN 80, Western Cape 75 in Oct–Dec 2024. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/saps-crime-statistics-leading-causes-of-murder/)
- ISS (2019): at least two people a day die in vigilante or group attacks; 846 of 20,336 murders in 2017/18 were tied to mob justice and the true figure is "probably far higher"; 66% of people mostly distrust the police and 46% the courts (Afrobarometer 2018); 3.5% of households belong to self-help groups (Stats SA Victims of Crime Survey 2018). Case: Thoriso Themane, 28, beaten and stoned by a group including school pupils in Polokwane. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/is-mob-violence-out-of-control-in-south-africa)
- Typical cases (2021): a 58-year-old woman set alight in Soweto after a witchcraft accusation; an 83-year-old woman burnt in her home in Majuba village, Eastern Cape; a man stoned and burnt in Bismarck village, Limpopo; in Mpumalanga, a man beaten to death by a crowd at Magudu Trust near Tonga after allegedly assaulting a woman, and four arrested for killing a youth accused of theft. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/mob-justice-fed-up-communities-dont-trust-police-to-protect-them/)
- Women accused of witchcraft are murdered at regular intervals; drug (tik) users who steal are killed in township mob attacks. [FULL, Wikipedia] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)
- Those targeted are often not the people responsible (Gareth Newham). [EXTRACT] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/mob-justice-fed-up-communities-dont-trust-police-to-protect-them)

### Inferences
- The 2022/23 total implies about six mob-justice murders a day at the peak; the 2026 run-rate is roughly half that.
- Mob killings are hard to prosecute (many participants, community silence), which adds to the unsolved share.

### Gaps
- Annual totals for 2019/20, 2023/24, 2024/25 and 2025/26 were not found.
- No Mpumalanga-specific mob-justice count was found.

## 9. Kidnapping and extortion as they relate to murder

### Takeaway
Kidnapping rose 264% in a decade to about 17,000 cases a year, but two-thirds are short "express" abductions during hijackings and robberies; under 5% involve ransom, trafficking or extortion. Extortion is heavily under-reported and almost never ends in conviction, and it now appears as a motive behind targeted hits and mass shootings.

### Cited Findings
- Kidnappings: 4,692 (2014/15) to 17,061 (2023/24), +264%; about 50 a day in Jul–Sep 2024; Gauteng 51% of cases, KZN 20%, Western Cape and Mpumalanga 6% each, Eastern Cape 5%. Motives in a 14,646-case sample for 2023/24: 66% during aggravated robbery (44% hijacking, 22% other robbery), 10% sexual offence, 7% retaliation or revenge, about 4% ransom, under 1% extortion or trafficking. Robbery-linked share: Mpumalanga 63%, KZN 55%, North West 55%. Carjackings 22,735 in 2023/24 (+78% in a decade); truck hijackings 1,976. [FULL] — [ISS, Dec 2024](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/south-africa-s-armed-robbery-problem-drives-kidnapping)
- 2024/25: 16,901 kidnappings. [EXTRACT, single opinion source] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/opinion/sa-crime-shifts-kidnappings-and-extortion-skyrocket)
- Jan–Mar 2025: kidnappings up 6.8% to 4,571 while murder fell 12.2%. [FULL] — [ISS in Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-28-crime-rates-show-hopeful-decline-is-south-africa-turning-the-corner-on-violence/)
- Ehlanzeni (Mpumalanga): 121 kidnappings in Oct–Dec 2023, up 65.8%. [FULL] — [Lowvelder](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2024/03/15/216-murders-in-ehlanzeni-in-three-months/)
- Extortion: 6,056 cases, 2,389 arrests and 178 convictions in five years to March 2024; "construction mafias" posing as business forums demand a 30% cut of projects. [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/opinion/sa-crime-shifts-kidnappings-and-extortion-skyrocket/)
- Gauteng led organised-crime killings in 2023, some linked to the "Boko Haram" extortion gang (Tshwane). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-05-21-sas-elections-stand-out-globally-for-very-real-threat-of-violence-assassination-report/)
- Hypothesis (James Myburgh): a cashless economy has cut robbery yields, so robbers shift to kidnapping; house robberies, business robberies and hijackings each fell about 18–22% in Jan–Mar 2026. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)

### Inferences
- Falling robbery and hijacking volumes are a plausible partial cause of falling murder (robbery murders dropped from about 403 to 235 per quarter between late 2024 and early 2026).

### Gaps
- [UNCERTAIN] The Jan–Mar kidnapping figures conflict: ISS (June 2026) says kidnappings rose "from 4,478 in the first quarter of 2025 to 4,571" in 2026, while ISS (May 2025) gives 4,571 for Jan–Mar 2025. One of the two is misreported; I could not check SAPS.
- No count of murders committed in the course of kidnapping or extortion was found.
- The News24 article on 17,000 kidnappings could not be fetched.

## 10. What researchers say drives it, including COVID and July 2021

### Takeaway
There is no single accepted cause. Researchers point to a mix of normalised interpersonal violence fuelled by alcohol, easy access to illegal guns, organised crime embedded in local economies, a weakened police and justice system, and deep inequality in dense urban settlements. The 2020 lockdown produced a dip (33 per 100,000), followed by a surge in 2021/22 and 2022/23; the cause of the fall since 2023 is openly disputed.

### Cited Findings
- Governance thesis (Lancaster and Gould, ISS): the 1994–2011 fall followed the end of political conflict; the low coincided with the 2010 World Cup; the rise from 2011 tracked the weakening of state institutions during State Capture, with corruption and criminal groups embedding locally, "leading to more illicit firearms, drugs, robberies, extortion and kidnapping". [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-28-crime-rates-show-hopeful-decline-is-south-africa-turning-the-corner-on-violence/)
- COVID and unrest: the rate fell to 33.5 in 2020/21, jumped to 41.8 in 2021/22 and 45.2 in 2022/23; the July 2021 unrest contributed 350 of 2021/22's 25,181 murders. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis). The unrest ran 9–18 July 2021 in KZN and Gauteng, sparked by Jacob Zuma's jailing and fuelled by layoffs and inequality worsened by pandemic policies. [FULL] — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_unrest)
- The 2024–26 fall is contested: Jonny Steinberg doubts the data because "nothing significant has changed"; Gareth Newham credits hotspot policing and prevention programmes; Jean Redpath finds it tracks the end of load-shedding; James Myburgh sees a return to the pre-COVID level and falling robbery. ISS concludes several factors are probably at work. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)
- Guy Lamb links the decline to Operation Shanela's high-density operations (from 2023); David Bruce says "we do not have instruments sensitive enough" and trends differ by province; Simon Howell warns against reading causality into small fluctuations. [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/)
- Firearms: "the availability of illegal guns in the hands of criminals fuelled violence" (ISS 2024 policy brief). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-28-crime-rates-show-hopeful-decline-is-south-africa-turning-the-corner-on-violence/)
- Alcohol: "The more alcohol we consume, the more violence we will suffer" (Acting Minister Cachalia, May 2026). [FULL] — [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/murder-down-almost-10-majority-cases-reported-these-four-provinces/)
- Organised crime: South Africa's mass killings come from "a wide ecosystem of competing criminal enterprises" (gangs, illegal mining, protection rackets, extortion, taxi violence), unlike Latin America's single dominant drug trade; the state is "hamstrung" by corruption, lack of resources and disarray in crime intelligence (Rademeyer). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-09-27-17-shot-in-joburg-10-in-cape-town-2026-mass-killings-toll-now-196/)
- State capture "continues in different forms"; criminal networks infiltrate parties, departments and procurement (Cachalia, Nov 2025). [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-11-28-murders-in-sa-drop-slightly-police-relee-release-crime-figures-amid-drug-cartel/)
- Inequality and urban form: high-murder precincts are urban, informal, young and male-skewed (ISS census analysis); gang areas feature poor lighting, unemployment, substance abuse and crowding. [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/first-in-line-for-murder), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)
- Trust: in 27 years of surveys, never more than half of adults said they trusted the police. [FULL] — [Daily Maverick](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/)
- Family violence as the root: with at least 14,270 home assaults a quarter, much of it witnessed by children, "cycles of violence prove so difficult to break". [FULL] — [ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)

### Inferences
- The post-2020 surge was strongest in KZN (40.9 to 57.8) and Gauteng (27.5 to 41.8), the two unrest provinces, and in Eastern Cape (52.2 to 72.3); Western Cape barely moved, which fits a province-specific gang dynamic.
- The provinces now falling fastest (KZN, Mpumalanga, North West) are those that spiked after 2020; Western Cape's gang violence is not following the national decline.

### Gaps
- No readable quantitative study isolating the effect of inequality, lockdown alcohol bans or the July 2021 unrest on murder was obtained; the above are expert interpretations.
- Stats SA's Victims of Crime (GPSJS) survey and mortality reports were not accessed, so there is no independent check here on SAPS murder counts.
