# SECUNDA (working title) — Game Design, v4

A detective story set in Secunda, Mpumalanga, over five days. Isometric, rotatable camera. Single player, one fixed main character, three to four hours.

Status: design draft, no code. This version moves v3 from an invented gold town to the real town of Secunda. Character and company names are placeholders.

Ground rules for using a real place:

- **The town is real; the people and companies are invented.** The colliery, the security firm, the guesthouse, the radio station and every named person are fictional. The synthetic-fuel plant is the skyline and the reason the town exists, and is never named as a party to the story or shown doing wrong. No real person appears.
- **Grounded, present day.** Phones, cameras, drones, radios. No implants, no abilities.
- **Single player.** Co-op is parked.
- **Accuracy needs local eyes.** What is written here about Secunda comes from published sources and memory, not from having been there. Anyone who knows the town should correct it.

---

## 1. The game in one paragraph

Ruth Mahlangu, a private investigator, drives into Secunda on a Monday evening to meet a client. He is shot before she finds him. His father owns a family colliery outside town, and three years ago brought in hard people to break a strike there and never got rid of them. Four of them now feed on the mine and the people around it. Over five days Ruth finds out who killed her client, learns what he was hiding, and decides what to tell whom. On Friday the colliery is to be sold, and what things look like that morning is the ending.

## 2. Source

The structure is taken from two Dashiell Hammett novels that are in the US public domain.

- *Red Harvest* (1929): an investigator arrives in a mining town, the man who sent for him is already dead, and he breaks the town's gangs by turning them on each other. The cost of doing that is the point of the book.
- *The Maltese Falcon* (1930): one object, and everyone who wants it tells a different story about what it is.

Only the structure is used. No text, names, scenes or dialogue are carried over. Hammett remains in copyright in the UK and EU until 2032, which is another reason to keep it to structure. Get a rights opinion before release.

## 3. Pillars

1. **What you tell people is the main verb.** The town changes because of who you informed, not who you shot.
2. **The player solves it.** The game hands out facts. Conclusions are the player's, and wrong ones play out.
3. **Violence is short, final and remembered.** Nobody is a bullet sponge, including Ruth. Everyone who dies stays dead and is counted.
4. **Five days, one place.** A few small areas known thoroughly, with a clock that does not wait.
5. **The basics only.** No levels, no stats, no upgrades, no loot.

## 4. Structure

Four acts across five days. Each day has three blocks: morning, afternoon, night. A block ends when the player moves time on (sleeping, waiting, travelling out to the mine) or when a scripted event closes it.

| Act | Days | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Arrival | Monday | 40 min | The client is dead. Ruth meets the people around him, takes a room, and is summoned to the farm, where the old man hires her to find his son's killer. |
| II. The four | Tuesday, Wednesday | 90 min | The murder is solved, rightly or wrongly. The old man tries to pay Ruth to leave. She learns about the core box and starts feeding the four powers what they want to hear. |
| III. Harvest | Thursday | 60 min | It comes apart. Ruth's best source is killed and Ruth is blamed. Steyn's men are looking for her. She finds the box and reaches the old man. The heaviest stealth and action. |
| IV. Epilogue | Friday | 20 min | The morning of the sale. Ruth decides what happens to the box, then walks through what is left. No combat. |

**Leaving.** Ruth's car is parked outside the guesthouse. From Tuesday onward the player can drive out at any time, and the epilogue plays for the town as it stands. Taking the old man's money on Wednesday is a complete, short, bad ending.

## 5. The place

**Secunda** was laid out in the mid-1970s as a company town for a coal-to-liquids plant, the second such town in the country, which is where the name comes from. About forty thousand people live in the town itself and many more in eMbalenhle to the west. It sits on flat Highveld grassland and maize country over the Highveld coalfield. It is a planned town of its period: wide roads, face-brick, parking lots, suburbs behind precast walls. The plant is visible from everywhere, with its cooling towers, steam and flares, and a single concrete stack about three hundred metres tall. Coal reaches it on conveyors that cross the veld from the collieries. Evander's gold headgears stand on the horizon to the north-west. In autumn the road verges are full of pink and white cosmos.

Coverage in recent years describes a town that rose with coal and does not know what comes after it. That uncertainty is the mood of the game. The story's colliery having eighteen months left is a small version of the same question.

**When.** A week in late March, cosmos season.

**The map.** Secunda is too spread out to walk, so the game is four small areas joined by short drives in Ruth's car.

| Area | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Town centre** | The guesthouse where Ruth stays (her room, the board, the save point), the community radio station in a row of shops, the police station, a shopping strip, the lake at the edge of the centre |
| **Brandtskraal Colliery** (fictional) | A small family mine east of town: the gate, the incline shaft, the office, the lab, the core shed, the conveyor to the rail loadout, the contractor camp |
| **eMbalenhle** | One neighbourhood: Joseph's house, a tavern, a taxi stop, Mokoena's truck yard on the edge |
| **The Brandt farm** | The original farmhouse among bluegums, older than the town, with the maize and the colliery's new block beyond it |

**Driving** is the travel interlude: the road, the plant on the skyline, and the car radio. After Monday, Daniel's station plays his old programmes on repeat, and what is on the radio changes with what Ruth has done.

The town runs on a clock. Shifts change at the mine and the plant, the roads fill and empty, and named people have places to be at each block.

## 6. People

**Ruth Mahlangu**, 44. Ten years in police commercial crime, now at a two-person firm in Johannesburg. Careful, tired, good at paperwork. Carries a licensed pistol she has drawn twice in her life.

**Daniel Brandt**, 36. The client. The owner's son, who ran a community radio station in town and had started broadcasting about the colliery. Shot outside the station on Monday evening.

**Hendrik Brandt**, 78. His family farmed here before the town existed. Owns Brandtskraal Colliery, which sells its coal under contract. Brought in Steyn to end the strike. Wants the sale to go through on Friday and wants to know who killed his son, in that order.

The four powers:

| Who | Holds | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| **Marius Steyn**, security contractor | The colliery gate, the contractor camp, forty armed men | To own Brandt after the sale as he does now |
| **Abel Mokoena**, haulier, head of a local business forum | The trucks, and the right to decide who gets a contract at the mine and who does not | The new coal block he believes the drilling proved |
| **Grace Tau**, guesthouse owner and lender | Rooms for contractors, half her guests' bank cards, every rumour | Her loans repaid before the mine changes hands |
| **Captain Sizwe Khoza**, station commander | The docket, the cells, the official version | To reach his pension without anything being written down |

Others: **Anneline Petersen**, who runs the colliery lab, sells information to all four and knows what the core says. **Joseph Sithole**, former shaft steward, sacked after the strike, lives in eMbalenhle, Ruth's first honest source. **Sam Nel**, technician at the radio station.

## 7. The story

**Hidden truth.** Brandtskraal's current workings are nearly mined out. Its future, and its sale price, rest on a new block under the farm, drilled last year. The boreholes found the seam there cooked by a dolerite sill: burnt, devolatilised coal that no one will buy. This is a real and common problem on this coalfield. The mine has about eighteen months left. Brandt had the logs and lab results replaced, paid Anneline to sign them, and is selling on Friday. Daniel found the real core, tray 114, and its lab sheet, hid them, and hired Ruth to confirm what he had before he broadcast it.

**The object.** Nobody Ruth meets describes the box the same way.

| Who | What they say it is |
|---|---|
| Hendrik Brandt | Stolen company property, worthless to anyone else |
| Steyn | Evidence. Whoever holds it holds Brandt |
| Mokoena | Twenty years of coal that somebody is hiding |
| Grace Tau | Collateral. Daniel pledged it against a loan |
| Khoza | There is no box |
| Anneline | A year and a half, then nothing |

**Who killed Daniel.** Sam Nel. He owed Grace Tau more than he could pay and had been selling her what Daniel was working on. Daniel found out on Monday afternoon. It was Daniel's own pistol, taken from the station desk. It had nothing to do with the mine. By Tuesday morning Steyn's men have dressed the scene to point at Mokoena's drivers, because Steyn wants the forum off his gate.

**Act by act.**

- **Monday.** Ruth arrives at dusk. Daniel is not at the guesthouse. She finds the crowd outside the radio station. Khoza gives her the official version; Joseph, who was Daniel's contact, gives her the background; Grace gives her a room. At night she can go back into the station, which is the stealth tutorial. A car from the farm fetches her.
- **Tuesday.** The murder. Three questions on the board: who was at the station, whose gun, who gains. By nightfall Ruth names someone to Brandt and Khoza. If she names Mokoena's people, Steyn raids the truck yard that night with her finding as his warrant.
- **Wednesday.** Brandt thanks her, pays her, and tells her to go. Every one of the four separately asks whether she has "it". She meets Anneline. The Tell mechanic opens fully, and the first night of violence follows from what she said.
- **Thursday.** Anneline is found dead in the lab, and Ruth was the last person seen with her. Steyn's vehicles are at every junction. Ruth moves as a wanted person, finds tray 114 in the core shed hidden among thousands like it, and drives out to the farm. How many people are there, and on whose side, depends on Wednesday.
- **Friday.** The buyers' cars arrive at the colliery office at 10:00. Ruth chooses what happens to the box: to the buyers, to Brandt, to Joseph and the workers, on air from Daniel's station, or into the slimes dam. None of these saves anyone. The sale collapsing closes the mine now. The sale going through closes it in eighteen months with the money gone. Then she drives out past the plant.

**The epilogue** is playable. The town centre and the colliery gate reflect who is alive, what burned, what Ruth chose. Survivors will talk. The last screen gives the number of dead and the names.

## 8. Investigation

The case board is the wall of Ruth's room and a full-screen view anywhere else. There is no quest log and no objective marker.

- **Clue:** one fact, marked as *seen* (Ruth observed it), *said* (someone told her), or *written* (a document or recording, which can be false).
- **Lead:** an open question a clue raises. Leads are the to-do list and softly mark the people and places they touch.
- **Insight:** two or three clues the player links on the board. A valid link produces a new card at once.
- **Question:** each act poses two or three. Each takes one answer.
- **Confrontation:** naming an answer to someone with power. Only then does the game respond to whether it was right.

Rules:

1. Every insight needed for the truth can be reached at least three ways: looking, talking, or getting into somewhere Ruth should not be.
2. Linking is free and immediate. Answers are never checked until they are spoken to someone.
3. Each act has a planted answer with real support, and one *seen* clue that breaks it.
4. A wrong answer moves the story on along a worse road. There is no fail screen for being wrong.
5. Time removes evidence: scenes are cleaned, people leave on the morning bus, documents are shredded. The last route to a required insight is never removed.

**Objects.** There are no quest items. Every object has what it does, what it shows, and who reacts to it. Daniel's pistol is a weapon, the murder weapon, and the thing Sam cannot look at. Tray 114 is a wooden tray of drill core, the proof, and something each of the four will kill for a different reason.

## 9. Conversations

Named characters are spoken to face to face: the view cuts from the isometric town to a first-person frame, a large portrait against the room Ruth is standing in, in the manner of Dune (1992). A short list of commands sits at the bottom right.

- **Ask about…** topics, which exist because of clues and leads Ruth holds.
- **Show…** any object or clue card.
- **Press** when what they said conflicts with something *seen*.
- **Tell…** give them a clue or an answer. Ruth chooses which, and can leave part of it out. She cannot invent facts, but she can tell different people different true things.
- **Offer…** money, silence, a lift out of town.
- **Leave.**

**Night resolves the town.** Each of the four acts overnight on what they currently believe: who has the box, who killed Daniel, who is talking to Ruth. The morning shows the result, such as a burned scrap yard, a missing guard, a new man on the gate, or a body. This is the engine of acts II and III, and the reason the body count is the player's.

## 10. Stealth

Used for places Ruth has no right to be (the station at night, the colliery lab, the contractor camp, the farmhouse, Mokoena's truck yard) and for all of Thursday.

- Guards see in cones, hear footsteps on gravel and iron sheeting, and follow routes tied to the shift clock.
- Darkness hides; a torch reveals both ways.
- Distractions are ordinary: a thrown stone, a car alarm, a phone left ringing.
- Ruth can hold someone from behind and put them down without killing them. They wake later and remember her.
- Being seen is not instant failure. People ask what she is doing, and in daylight a good answer often works.
- A drawn weapon changes every conversation in town.

## 11. Weapons, armour, health

No upgrades, no attachments, no ammunition types, no experience.

- **Weapons:** Ruth's 9 mm pistol with two magazines. Found during play: a revolver, a pump shotgun, a hunting rifle. Improvised: a torch, a tyre iron. She carries one long gun and one handgun. Ammunition is counted in single rounds and there is little of it.
- **Armour:** one covert vest, obtainable on Wednesday. It stops pistol rounds a few times, slows her slightly, and is noticed in conversation.
- **Health:** three states: fine, hurt, badly hurt. A first-aid kit takes her up one state. The clinic treats her fully but costs a block and the nurse asks questions. Wounds carry over to the next day.
- **Enemies** are as fragile as she is. Two or three hits ends anyone. A fight with more than four opponents is one to avoid or to arrange allies for.
- Death reloads the start of the current block.

## 12. Camera and controls

- 3D town, fixed-pitch isometric camera, free rotation, three zoom steps. Walls and roofs between camera and Ruth fade.
- WASD or click to move, mouse to aim, one interact key with a small radial (Look, Take, Use, Talk).
- **Focus** (hold): the view desaturates and anything examinable nearby is outlined.
- Crouch, lean at corners, and a noise ring under Ruth's feet when moving.

## 13. First build

Monday only: arrival, the crowd at the station, three conversations, the room and the board, the night entry into the station, the drive to the farm. About forty minutes. It exercises movement, Focus, conversation, clue capture, linking, stealth and one possible gunfight.

Engine is still open. With co-op gone, a browser build (Three.js) remains the fastest route to something playable and testable here; Unity or Godot are equally reasonable for the full game.
