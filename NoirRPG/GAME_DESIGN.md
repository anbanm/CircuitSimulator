# SECUNDA (working title) — Game Design, v5

A detective story set in Secunda, Mpumalanga, over five days. Isometric, rotatable camera. Single player, one fixed main character, three to four hours.

Status: design draft, no code. This version revises v4 using the research in `reports/` (the Secunda setting report, and the crime research notes in `research_notes/`). Character and company names are placeholders.

Ground rules for using a real place:

- **The town is real; the people and companies are invented.** The colliery, the security firm, the guesthouse, the radio station and every named person are fictional. The synthetic-fuel plant is the skyline and the reason the town exists, and is never named as a party to the story or shown doing wrong. No real person appears.
- **Grounded, present day.** Phones, cameras, drones, radios. No implants, no abilities.
- **Single player.** Co-op is parked.
- **Real institutions, invented office-holders.** The police station, courts and municipality are real institutions; every officer, official and employee shown is invented, and none is modelled on a real person.
- **Accuracy needs local eyes.** What is written here about Secunda comes from published sources, mostly read as search extracts, not from having been there. Almost nothing about how the town looks is confirmed yet. Anyone who knows the town should correct it.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

Ruth Mahlangu, a private investigator, drives into Secunda on a Monday evening to meet a client. He is shot before she finds him. His father owns a family colliery outside town, and three years ago brought in hard people to break a strike there and never got rid of them. Four of them now feed on the mine and the people around it. Over five days Ruth finds out who killed her client, learns what he was hiding, and decides what to tell whom. On Friday the colliery is to be sold, and what things look like that morning is the ending.

## 2. Source

The structure is taken from two Dashiell Hammett novels that are in the US public domain.

- *Red Harvest* (1929): an investigator arrives in a mining town, the man who sent for him is already dead, and he breaks the town's gangs by turning them on each other. The cost of doing that is the point of the book.
- *The Maltese Falcon* (1930): one object, and everyone who wants it tells a different story about what it is.

Only the structure is used. No text, names, scenes or dialogue are carried over. Hammett remains in copyright in the UK and EU until 2032, which is another reason to keep it to structure. Get a rights opinion before release.

## 3. Pillars

1. **What you tell people is the main verb.** The town changes because of who you informed, not who you shot.
2. **The player solves it.** The game hands out facts. Conclusions are the player's, and wrong ones play out.
3. **Violence is short, final and remembered.** Nobody is a bullet sponge, including Ruth. Everyone who dies stays dead and is counted.
4. **Five days, one place.** A few small areas known thoroughly, with a clock that does not wait.
5. **The basics only.** No levels, no stats, no upgrades, no loot.

## 4. Structure

Four acts across five days. Each day has three blocks: morning, afternoon, night. A block ends when the player moves time on (sleeping, waiting, travelling out to the mine) or when a scripted event closes it.

| Act | Days | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Arrival | Monday | 40 min | The client is dead. Ruth meets the people around him, takes a room, and is summoned to the farm, where the old man hires her to find his son's killer. |
| II. The four | Tuesday, Wednesday | 90 min | The murder is solved, rightly or wrongly. The old man tries to pay Ruth to leave. She learns about the crate and starts feeding the four powers what they want to hear. |
| III. Harvest | Thursday | 60 min | It comes apart. Ruth's best source is killed and Ruth is blamed. Steyn's men are looking for her. She finds the crate and reaches the old man. The heaviest stealth and action. |
| IV. Epilogue | Friday | 20 min | The morning of the sale. Ruth decides what happens to the crate, then walks through what is left. No combat. |

**Leaving.** Ruth's car is parked outside the guesthouse. From Tuesday onward the player can drive out at any time, and the epilogue plays for the town as it stands. Taking the old man's money on Wednesday is a complete, short, bad ending.

## 5. The place

**Secunda** was planned from 1974 by Max Kirchhofer as a company town for a coal-to-liquids plant, the second such town in the country, which is where the name comes from. The plan is neighbourhood cells of numbered extensions separated by landscaped green strips, each with a primary school within walking distance, around a centre inside a ring street. About forty thousand people lived in the town at the 2011 census, about 70% of them Afrikaans-speaking. eMbalenhle, established in 1978 as a dormitory town for the plant's workers, had about 119,000, half of them isiZulu-speaking, and lies beyond the plant, 10 to 17 km away by road depending on the source. The plant sits about 3 km south-south-west of town and is visible from everywhere: cooling towers, steam, flares, and a 301 m concrete stack. Coal reaches it on conveyors over 20 km long. Evander, with its gold shafts, the second magistrate's court and the mortuary, is about 8 km to the north-west.

**How it is now.** The municipality has been under provincial intervention since 2018 and owes the power utility billions. In 2026 parts of the town went more than two weeks without water. Private firms patched the main roads themselves. The town centre has had a three-day power cut blamed on cable theft, people living in tents near the taxi rank, and regular theft from parked cars, some of it by remote jamming. Residents describe a rotten-egg smell when the wind is wrong. eMbalenhle has burned its municipal offices twice and has sewage running in some streets; its police station has no holding cells, fewer than half its vehicles running, and detectives carrying about 150 dockets each.

Most of the town depends on one employer, which has put its own estimate of jobs at risk from the move away from coal at nearly 25,000. That uncertainty is the mood of the game. The story's colliery running out is a small version of the same question.

**When.** A week in September, during the plant's annual maintenance shutdown, when up to twenty thousand contractors arrive and every guesthouse is full. A town full of strangers explains why nobody looks twice at Ruth, or at Steyn's extra men. (Cosmos flowers from mid-March to May, so the earlier autumn setting and its flowers are dropped.)

**The map.** Secunda is too spread out to walk, so the game is four small areas joined by short drives in Ruth's car.

| Area | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Town centre** | The guesthouse where Ruth stays (her room, the board, the save point), the community radio station in a row of shops, the police station, a shopping strip, the lake at the edge of the centre |
| **Brandtskraal Colliery** (fictional) | A small family mine outside town that trucks its coal out by road: the gate and weighbridge, the shaft, the office, the lab and its sample store, the stockpile and loading area, the contractor camp. No real independent colliery was found near Secunda, so this is pure invention, and what a small colliery looks like on the surface still needs reference |
| **eMbalenhle** | One neighbourhood: Joseph's house, a tavern, a taxi stop, Mokoena's truck yard on the edge |
| **The Brandt farm** | The original farmhouse among bluegums, older than the town, with maize beyond it |

### The town in detail: business and residential

This is the first area to be designed and drawn. Street-level facts here are thin: published sources describe Secunda's centre and suburbs only in general terms, so every location below needs checking against the real town.

**Business area.** A low-rise centre of face-brick shopping centres and offices set among large parking lots, serving the town and the smaller towns around it.

| Location | Use in the story |
|---|---|
| Community radio station (fictional), a unit in a shop row | Daniel's station. Murder scene on Monday; night entry is the stealth tutorial; the broadcast desk is one of Friday's choices |
| Shop row and parking lot | The crowd on Monday. Car guards and shop staff are the first witnesses |
| Police station (the real one is on Steenkamp Street) | Captain Khoza, the docket, the cells. Ruth is brought here on Thursday if caught |
| Bank and ATM row | Where Grace Tau's borrowers draw their wages with cards she holds. ATM slips tie Sam Nel to her |
| Municipal offices (the real head office is on Horwood Street, the main street) | Rates and land records for the farm and the colliery: *written* clues, some of them altered. The water is off and the queue is long |
| 24-hour fuel station | Open when nothing else is. Night meetings, a camera that saw a car on Monday |
| The lake | Where Anneline will talk, after dark, away from the mine |

**Residential area.** Suburbs of single-storey houses on generous plots behind walls, with lawns, carports and street trees, and walled townhouse complexes among them.

| Location | Use in the story |
|---|---|
| Grace Tau's guesthouse (fictional), a converted house with rooms built on behind | Ruth's room, the board, the save point. Contractors' vehicles in the yard, and Grace always at the kitchen door |
| Daniel Brandt's house | Searched on Tuesday. He lived in town, not on the farm, and the house says why |
| Sam Nel's flat, in a block of walk-ups | Entered on Tuesday. The debt, the pawn slips, and what he was selling |
| Anneline Petersen's townhouse, in a complex with a boom gate | Wednesday's meeting. Thursday's reason for running |
| Marius Steyn's house, the highest wall on the street | Optional entry on Wednesday night. What Steyn believes about the crate |
| Green strips between the neighbourhoods, part of the original town plan | On Thursday the roads are blockaded or watched. These are how Ruth crosses town on foot. Whether they carry continuous footpaths today is not confirmed |

**Driving** is the travel interlude: the road, the plant on the skyline, and the car radio. After Monday, Daniel's station plays his old programmes on repeat, and what is on the radio changes with what Ruth has done.

The town runs on a clock. Shifts change at the mine and the plant, the roads fill and empty, and named people have places to be at each block.

## 6. People

**Ruth Mahlangu**, 44. Ten years in a police commercial crime unit, now a registered private investigator at a two-person firm in Johannesburg. Careful, tired, good at paperwork. She left a unit where cases reported outnumbered arrests eight to one. Carries a licensed pistol, concealed as the law requires, which she has drawn twice in her life.

**Daniel Brandt**, 36. The client. The owner's son, who ran a small community station in town, streaming online while its FM licence was pending, and had started broadcasting about the colliery. Shot outside the station on Monday evening.

**Hendrik Brandt**, 78. His family farmed here before the town existed. Owns Brandtskraal Colliery, which sells its coal by the truckload to traders and industrial buyers. Brought in Steyn to end the strike. Wants the sale to go through on Friday and wants to know who killed his son, in that order.

The four powers:

| Who | Holds | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| **Marius Steyn**, owner of an armed-response firm | The colliery gate and weighbridge, the contractor camp, forty armed men, and a seat on the local policing forum | To own Brandt after the sale as he does now |
| **Abel Mokoena**, haulier, head of a local business forum | The trucks, a coal yard, and the right to decide who gets a contract at the mine and who does not | To prove the stone in the loads is the mine's and not his drivers', and to keep his share when the mine changes hands |
| **Grace Tau**, guesthouse owner and lender | Rooms for contractors, half her guests' bank cards, every rumour | Her loans repaid before the mine changes hands |
| **Captain Sizwe Khoza**, detective | The docket, the cells, the official version | To close this one as a robbery. He is not bought; he has a hundred and fifty other dockets and two working cars |

Others: **Anneline Petersen**, who runs the colliery lab, signs the certificates, sells information to all four and knows what the tins hold. **Joseph Sithole**, former shaft steward, one of the workers dismissed after the strike, whose unfair-dismissal case is still in the labour courts three years on. He lives in eMbalenhle and is Ruth's first honest source. **Sam Nel**, technician at the radio station.

**What is invented and what is borrowed.** Steyn's firm is drawn from the real pattern here of armed-response companies and policing forums working together, including joining vehicle chases. Mokoena's forum is a provincial and national pattern; no such incident was found at Secunda itself. Lenders who hold borrowers' bank cards are documented elsewhere in Mpumalanga, not here. All three are plausible inventions and should be written as that.

**Language.** In town, conversation defaults to Afrikaans and English; in eMbalenhle, to isiZulu. Ruth moves between them. Underground workers wear one-piece boiler suits in white, yellow or orange, not blue two-piece overalls.

## 7. The story

**Hidden truth.** Brandtskraal is selling rock. Its remaining coal is thin and full of stone, and for about a year the quality certificates that go out with each consignment have been false. When coal is sampled for sale, part of every sample is sealed and kept back in case of a dispute. Those retained samples, three months of them in sealed tins with the lab's own day book, show what the mine really dug. The colliery is to be sold on Friday on the strength of its sales contracts, and one honest test of those tins would end the contracts and the sale. Brandt paid Anneline to sign the certificates. Daniel took the crate of tins and the day book, hid them, and hired Ruth to confirm what he had before he broadcast it.

This follows documented coal-quality frauds in Mpumalanga: rival laboratories disagreeing, samples taken with no observer, false weighbridge tickets, loads swapped at coal yards. The sampling and laboratory detail needs review by someone who has worked in a coal lab.

**The object.** Nobody Ruth meets describes the crate the same way.

| Who | What they say it is |
|---|---|
| Hendrik Brandt | Old lab rejects that should have been thrown out |
| Steyn | Evidence. Whoever holds it holds Brandt |
| Mokoena | Proof that his drivers never touched the loads. Customers have been docking his haulage for delivering stone |
| Grace Tau | Collateral. Daniel pledged it against a loan |
| Khoza | There is no crate. There is a robbery |
| Anneline | Three months of what we really dug |

**Who killed Daniel.** Sam Nel. He owed Grace Tau more than he could pay and had been selling her what Daniel was working on. Daniel found out on Monday afternoon. It was Daniel's own pistol, taken from the station desk. It had nothing to do with the mine. This matches the local record: most murders here are personal, and are solved when someone confesses or runs.

**The first theory.** Police open an inquest docket and call it a robbery: the town centre is a known spot for theft from parked cars. By Tuesday morning Steyn's men have dressed the scene to point at Mokoena's drivers, who are already suspected of swapping loads, because Steyn wants the forum off his gate.

**The second death.** On Thursday Anneline is shot in her car at the gate of her complex, and her laptop and bag are taken. This one is a hit, and unlike Daniel's death it is the kind that usually goes unsolved. Who ordered it is the game's second mystery, and will be designed from the research on contract killings now under way.

**Act by act.**

- **Monday.** Ruth arrives at dusk. Daniel is not at the guesthouse. She finds the crowd outside the radio station. Khoza gives her the official version; Joseph, who was Daniel's contact, gives her the background; Grace gives her the last free room in town. At night she can go back into the station, which is the stealth tutorial. A car from the farm fetches her.
- **Tuesday.** The murder. Three questions on the board: who was at the station, whose gun, who gains. The body is at the mortuary in Evander. By nightfall Ruth names someone to Brandt and Khoza. If she names Mokoena's people, Steyn raids the truck yard that night with her finding as his warrant.
- **Wednesday.** Brandt thanks her, pays her, and tells her to go. Every one of the four separately asks whether she has "it". She meets Anneline at the lake. The Tell mechanic opens fully, and the first night of violence follows from what she said.
- **Thursday.** Anneline is killed, and Ruth was the last person seen with her. Mokoena's forum blockades the roads with burning tyres, as has happened here before, and Steyn's vehicles are at the junctions that are left. Ruth moves as a wanted person, finds the crate where Daniel hid it, and drives out to the farm. How many people are there, and on whose side, depends on Wednesday.
- **Friday.** The buyers' cars arrive at the colliery office at 10:00. Ruth chooses what happens to the crate: to the buyers, to Brandt, to Joseph and the dismissed workers, on air from Daniel's station, or into the slimes dam. None of these saves anyone. The sale collapsing closes the mine now. The sale going through closes it within the year with the money gone. Then she drives out past the plant.

**The epilogue** is playable. The town centre and the colliery gate reflect who is alive, what burned, what Ruth chose. Survivors will talk. The last screen gives the number of dead and the names.

**What the research says about endings.** In the real cases, the person who pulled the trigger is sometimes convicted and the person who paid almost never is. The national detection rate for murder is about one in nine. The epilogue should be honest about that: Ruth can know who ordered Anneline's death and still watch nobody be charged.

## 8. Investigation

The case board is the wall of Ruth's room and a full-screen view anywhere else. There is no quest log and no objective marker.

- **Clue:** one fact, marked as *seen* (Ruth observed it), *said* (someone told her), or *written* (a document or recording, which can be false).
- **Lead:** an open question a clue raises. Leads are the to-do list and softly mark the people and places they touch.
- **Insight:** two or three clues the player links on the board. A valid link produces a new card at once.
- **Question:** each act poses two or three. Each takes one answer.
- **Confrontation:** naming an answer to someone with power. Only then does the game respond to whether it was right.

Rules:

1. Every insight needed for the truth can be reached at least three ways: looking, talking, or getting into somewhere Ruth should not be.
2. Linking is free and immediate. Answers are never checked until they are spoken to someone.
3. Each act has a planted answer with real support, and one *seen* clue that breaks it.
4. A wrong answer moves the story on along a worse road. There is no fail screen for being wrong.
5. Time removes evidence: scenes are cleaned, people leave on the morning bus, documents are shredded. The last route to a required insight is never removed.

**Objects.** There are no quest items. Every object has what it does, what it shows, and who reacts to it. Daniel's pistol is a weapon, the murder weapon, and the thing Sam cannot look at. The crate is forty sealed tins of crushed coal and a day book, the proof, and something each of the four wants for a different reason.

## 9. Conversations

Named characters are spoken to face to face: the view cuts from the isometric town to a first-person frame, a large portrait against the room Ruth is standing in, in the manner of Dune (1992). A short list of commands sits at the bottom right.

- **Ask about…** topics, which exist because of clues and leads Ruth holds.
- **Show…** any object or clue card.
- **Press** when what they said conflicts with something *seen*.
- **Tell…** give them a clue or an answer. Ruth chooses which, and can leave part of it out. She cannot invent facts, but she can tell different people different true things.
- **Offer…** money, silence, a lift out of town.
- **Leave.**

**Night resolves the town.** Each of the four acts overnight on what they currently believe: who has the crate, who killed Daniel, who is talking to Ruth. The morning shows the result, such as a burned scrap yard, a missing guard, a new man on the gate, or a body. This is the engine of acts II and III, and the reason the body count is the player's.

## 10. Stealth

Used for places Ruth has no right to be (the station at night, the colliery lab, the contractor camp, the farmhouse, Mokoena's truck yard) and for all of Thursday.

- Guards see in cones, hear footsteps on gravel and iron sheeting, and follow routes tied to the shift clock.
- Darkness hides; a torch reveals both ways.
- Distractions are ordinary: a thrown stone, a car alarm, a phone left ringing.
- Ruth can hold someone from behind and put them down without killing them. They wake later and remember her.
- Being seen is not instant failure. People ask what she is doing, and in daylight a good answer often works.
- A drawn weapon changes every conversation in town.

## 11. Weapons, armour, health

No upgrades, no attachments, no ammunition types, no experience.

- **Weapons:** Ruth's 9 mm pistol with two magazines. Found during play: a revolver, a pump shotgun, a hunting rifle. Improvised: a torch, a tyre iron. She carries one long gun and one handgun. Ammunition is counted in single rounds and there is little of it.
- **Armour:** one covert vest, obtainable on Wednesday. It stops pistol rounds a few times, slows her slightly, and is noticed in conversation.
- **Health:** three states: fine, hurt, badly hurt. A first-aid kit takes her up one state. The clinic treats her fully but costs a block and the nurse asks questions. Wounds carry over to the next day.
- **Enemies** are as fragile as she is. Two or three hits ends anyone. A fight with more than four opponents is one to avoid or to arrange allies for.
- Death reloads the start of the current block.

## 12. Camera and controls

- 3D town, fixed-pitch isometric camera, free rotation, three zoom steps. Walls and roofs between camera and Ruth fade.
- WASD or click to move, mouse to aim, one interact key with a small radial (Look, Take, Use, Talk).
- **Focus** (hold): the view desaturates and anything examinable nearby is outlined.
- Crouch, lean at corners, and a noise ring under Ruth's feet when moving.

## 13. First build

Monday only: arrival, the crowd at the station, three conversations, the room and the board, the night entry into the station, the drive to the farm. About forty minutes. It exercises movement, Focus, conversation, clue capture, linking, stealth and one possible gunfight.

Engine is still open. With co-op gone, a browser build (Three.js) remains the fastest route to something playable and testable here; Unity or Godot are equally reasonable for the full game.
