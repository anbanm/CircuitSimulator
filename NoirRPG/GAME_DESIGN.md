# SECUNDA (working title) — Game Design, v7

A detective story set in Secunda, Mpumalanga, over two weeks. Isometric, rotatable camera. Single player, one fixed main character, about five and a half hours.

Status: design draft, no code. This version revises v4 using the research in `reports/` (the Secunda setting report, and the crime research notes in `research_notes/`). Character and company names are placeholders.

Ground rules for using a real place:

- **The town is real; the people and companies are invented.** The colliery, the security firm, the guesthouse, the radio station and every named person are fictional. The synthetic-fuel plant is the skyline and the reason the town exists, and is never named as a party to the story or shown doing wrong. No real person appears.
- **Grounded, present day.** Phones, cameras, drones, radios. No implants, no abilities.
- **Single player.** Co-op is parked.
- **Real institutions, invented office-holders.** The police station, courts and municipality are real institutions; every officer, official and employee shown is invented, and none is modelled on a real person.
- **Accuracy needs local eyes.** What is written here about Secunda comes from published sources, mostly read as search extracts, not from having been there. Almost nothing about how the town looks is confirmed yet. Anyone who knows the town should correct it.

## 1. The game in one paragraph

Ruth Mahlangu, a private investigator, drives into Secunda on a Monday evening to meet a client. He is shot before she finds him. His father owns a family colliery outside town, and three years ago brought in hard people to break a strike there and never got rid of them. Four of them now feed on the mine and the people around it. Over two weeks Ruth finds out who killed her client, learns what he was hiding, and decides what to tell whom. On the second Friday the colliery is to be sold, and what things look like that morning is the ending.

## 2. Source

The structure is taken from two Dashiell Hammett novels that are in the US public domain.

- *Red Harvest* (1929): an investigator arrives in a mining town, the man who sent for him is already dead, and he breaks the town's gangs by turning them on each other. The cost of doing that is the point of the book.
- *The Maltese Falcon* (1930): one object, and everyone who wants it tells a different story about what it is.

Only the structure is used. No text, names, scenes or dialogue are carried over. Hammett remains in copyright in the UK and EU until 2032, which is another reason to keep it to structure. Get a rights opinion before release.

## 3. Pillars

1. **What you tell people is the main verb.** The town changes because of who you informed, not who you shot.
2. **The player solves it.** The game hands out facts. Conclusions are the player's, and wrong ones play out.
3. **Violence is short, final and remembered.** Nobody is a bullet sponge, including Ruth. Everyone who dies stays dead and is counted.
4. **Two weeks, one place.** A few small areas known thoroughly, and a calendar of fixed events that does not wait for the player.
5. **The basics only.** No levels, no stats, no upgrades, no loot.

## 4. Structure

Four acts across two weeks, fourteen days from a Monday to the Sunday after next. Each day has three blocks: morning, afternoon, night. The player does not play every block. Ruth can let time pass (sleeping, waiting for a records request, healing, waiting for money to come through), and whole days can go by in a minute. What she cannot do is stop the calendar.

| Act | Days | Length | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| I. Arrival | 1–2 | 45 min | The client is dead. Ruth meets the people around him, takes a room, and is summoned to the farm, where the old man hires her to find his son's killer. |
| II. The murder | 3–7 | 100 min | The first week. Daniel's murder is solved, rightly or wrongly. The funeral puts everyone in one place. The old man pays Ruth off, and from then her only backing is a detective with two cars. |
| III. The fraud | 8–11 | 130 min | The second week. The buyers arrive to test the coal. Ruth finds a witness and decides who to tell. The witness is killed, Ruth is blamed, the roads are blockaded. It ends at 21:00 on day 11 in a five-storey building with a rehearsal on the fourth floor. |
| IV. Epilogue | 12–14 | 30 min | The sale, the choice, and what is left. Ruth makes her statement and drives out. No combat. |

About five and a half hours in all, which is longer than first planned.

Other things run on their own clocks. A records request takes three working days. Money comes through each morning. Wounds carry from day to day. Rumours Grace sells cost more each day.

**Leaving.** Ruth's car is parked outside the guesthouse. From day 2 onward the player can drive out at any time, and the epilogue plays for the town as it stands. Taking the old man's money on day 6 and going is a complete, short, bad ending.

## 5. The place

**Secunda** was planned from 1974 by Max Kirchhofer as a company town for a coal-to-liquids plant, the second such town in the country, which is where the name comes from. The plan is neighbourhood cells of numbered extensions separated by landscaped green strips, each with a primary school within walking distance, around a centre inside a ring street. About forty thousand people lived in the town at the 2011 census, about 70% of them Afrikaans-speaking. eMbalenhle, established in 1978 as a dormitory town for the plant's workers, had about 119,000, half of them isiZulu-speaking, and lies beyond the plant, 10 to 17 km away by road depending on the source. The plant sits about 3 km south-south-west of town and is visible from everywhere: cooling towers, steam, flares, and a 301 m concrete stack. Coal reaches it on conveyors over 20 km long. Evander, with its gold shafts, the second magistrate's court and the mortuary, is about 8 km to the north-west.

**How it is now.** The municipality has been under provincial intervention since 2018 and owes the power utility billions. In 2026 parts of the town went more than two weeks without water. Private firms patched the main roads themselves. The town centre has had a three-day power cut blamed on cable theft, people living in tents near the taxi rank, and regular theft from parked cars, some of it by remote jamming. Residents describe a rotten-egg smell when the wind is wrong. eMbalenhle has burned its municipal offices twice and has sewage running in some streets; its police station has no holding cells, fewer than half its vehicles running, and detectives carrying about 150 dockets each.

Most of the town depends on one employer, which has put its own estimate of jobs at risk from the move away from coal at nearly 25,000. That uncertainty is the mood of the game. The story's colliery running out is a small version of the same question.

**When.** Two weeks in September, during the plant's annual maintenance shutdown, when up to twenty thousand contractors arrive and every guesthouse is full. A town full of strangers explains why nobody looks twice at Ruth, or at Steyn's extra men. (Cosmos flowers from mid-March to May, so the earlier autumn setting and its flowers are dropped.)

**The map.** Secunda is too spread out to walk, so the game is four small areas joined by short drives in Ruth's car.

| Area | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Town centre** | The guesthouse where Ruth stays (her room, the board, the save point), the community radio station in a row of shops, the police station, a shopping strip, the lake at the edge of the centre |
| **Brandtskraal Colliery** (fictional) | A small family mine outside town that trucks its coal out by road: the gate and weighbridge, the shaft, the office, the lab and its sample store, the stockpile and loading area, the contractor camp. No real independent colliery was found near Secunda, so this is pure invention, and what a small colliery looks like on the surface still needs reference |
| **eMbalenhle** | One neighbourhood: Joseph's house, a tavern, a taxi stop, Mokoena's truck yard on the edge |
| **The Brandt farm** | The original farmhouse among bluegums, older than the town, with maize beyond it |

### The town in detail: business and residential

This is the first area to be designed and drawn. Street-level facts here are thin: published sources describe Secunda's centre and suburbs only in general terms, so every location below needs checking against the real town.

**Business area.** A low-rise centre of face-brick shopping centres and offices set among large parking lots, serving the town and the smaller towns around it.

| Location | Use in the story |
|---|---|
| Community radio station (fictional), on the fifth floor of an office block in the centre | Daniel's station. Murder scene on day 1; night entry is the stealth tutorial; the broadcast desk is one of the final choices |
| Shop row and parking lot | The crowd on day 1. Car guards and shop staff are the first witnesses |
| Police station (the real one is on Steenkamp Street) | Captain Khoza, the docket, the cells. Ruth is brought here on day 11 if caught |
| Bank and ATM row | Where Grace Tau's borrowers draw their wages with cards she holds. ATM slips tie Sam Nel to her |
| Municipal offices (the real head office is on Horwood Street, the main street) | Rates and land records for the farm and the colliery: *written* clues, some of them altered. The water is off and the queue is long |
| 24-hour fuel station | Open when nothing else is. Night meetings, a camera that saw a car on the first night |
| The lake | Where Anneline will talk, after dark, away from the mine |

**Residential area.** Suburbs of single-storey houses on generous plots behind walls, with lawns, carports and street trees, and walled townhouse complexes among them.

| Location | Use in the story |
|---|---|
| Grace Tau's guesthouse (fictional), a converted house with rooms built on behind | Ruth's room, the board, the save point. Contractors' vehicles in the yard, and Grace always at the kitchen door |
| Daniel Brandt's house | Searched in the first week. He lived in town, not on the farm, and the house says why |
| Sam Nel's flat, in a block of walk-ups | Entered in the first week. The debt, the pawn slips, and what he was selling |
| Anneline Petersen's townhouse, in a complex with a boom gate | Where she lives, and where she is shot on day 11 |
| Marius Steyn's house, the highest wall on the street | Optional entry in the second week. What Steyn believes about the crate |
| Green strips between the neighbourhoods, part of the original town plan | On day 11 the roads are blockaded or watched. These are how Ruth crosses town on foot. Whether they carry continuous footpaths today is not confirmed |

**Driving** is the travel interlude: the road, the plant on the skyline, and the car radio. After the first night, Daniel's station plays his old programmes on repeat, and what is on the radio changes with what Ruth has done.

The town runs on a clock. Shifts change at the mine and the plant, the roads fill and empty, and named people have places to be at each block.

## 6. People

**Ruth Mahlangu**, 44. Ten years in a police commercial crime unit, now a registered private investigator at a two-person firm in Johannesburg. Careful, tired, good at paperwork. She left a unit where cases reported outnumbered arrests eight to one. Carries a licensed pistol, concealed as the law requires, which she has drawn twice in her life.

**Daniel Brandt**, 36. The client. The owner's son, who ran a small community station in town, streaming online while its FM licence was pending, and had started broadcasting about the colliery. Shot outside the station on Monday evening.

**Hendrik Brandt**, 78. His family farmed here before the town existed. Owns Brandtskraal Colliery, which sells its coal by the truckload to traders and industrial buyers. Brought in Steyn to end the strike three years ago, when the crews refused to load waste rock with the coal. Wants the sale to go through on Friday and wants to know who killed his son, in that order.

The four powers. Each has money, men or leverage, and no legitimate claim to what they hold:

| Who | Holds | Wants |
|---|---|---|
| **Marius Steyn**, owner of an armed-response firm | The colliery gate and weighbridge, the contractor camp, forty armed men, and a seat on the local policing forum | To own Brandt after the sale as he does now |
| **Abel Mokoena**, haulier, head of a local business forum | The trucks, a coal yard, and the right to decide who gets a contract at the mine and who does not | To prove the stone in the loads is the mine's and not his drivers', and to keep his share when the mine changes hands |
| **Grace Tau**, guesthouse owner and lender | Rooms for contractors, the dismissed men's bank cards, every rumour, and she sells all three | Her loans repaid before the mine changes hands |

Others: **Anneline Petersen**, who runs the colliery lab, signs the certificates, sells information to all four and knows what the tins hold. **Joseph Sithole**, former shaft steward, one of the workers dismissed after the strike, whose unfair-dismissal case is still in the labour courts three years on. He lives in eMbalenhle, is Ruth's first honest source, and is keeping a box for Daniel without knowing what is in it. **Sam Nel**, technician at the radio station, who stood surety for his brother's loan from Grace Tau.

### The police

The police are the opposite of the four powers. The four have money, men and reach and no right to any of it. The police have the right and almost nothing else.

- **Captain Sizwe Khoza**, detective. About a hundred and fifty open dockets, two working cars, a commander who wants numbers. He calls Daniel's death a robbery on the first night because a robbery can be closed. He is honest, and once Ruth shows him something he cannot ignore he is on her side.
- **Constable Lerato Nkuna**, three years in, drives the car that works. She is the one who actually comes when called.
- **The mortuary at Evander**, where a forensic officer will tell Ruth more than the report says, if she is asked properly.
- **The officer who moonlights for Steyn.** Not a villain: a sergeant with a second job on a security company's books, who mentions things. The station is not corrupt. It is porous.

**Funding.** Money is the game's one resource, and from the end of the first week it comes from the police. Until then Ruth is spending Brandt's retainer. When he pays her off and tells her to leave, Khoza signs her on as a registered informer so that he can draw from the station's informer fund. It is small, it is slow, and every rand has a form behind it.

| What the money is for | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Information** | The main use. Car guards, gate guards, clerks, a mortuary porter, a truck driver, and Grace Tau, who sells rumours at a price that goes up each day. Paying is one way to open a topic; Show and Press are the others |
| **Ammunition** | Bought over the counter at the gun shop in town against Ruth's licence. Counted by the round, and there is never enough |
| **A second weapon** | No upgrades and no attachments, as before. What money buys is one used long gun, or Khoza quietly lends a shotgun from the station safe, which is irregular and goes on his account |
| **Armour** | One vest. Khoza signs a station vest out to her, or she buys a worn one from a security guard for more than it is worth |
| **Getting by** | The room, fuel, airtime, the clinic |

**Level of support.** How much comes through each morning depends on how far Khoza can justify her to his commander. It rises when she adds something solid to the docket and falls when she is wrong or when someone she named turns up dead. At its lowest she gets nothing and is on her own. At its highest it is still less than Steyn spends on diesel in a day, and the player should be able to see that.

Whether the police can fund an informer this way, and what a private person may be lent, needs checking before it is final.

**Favours.** Alongside the money, Khoza can give a short, visible list of favours, and each is used up when spent:

| Favour | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| The docket | Ruth reads what the police have and adds to it. Her clues become *written* and count later | Khoza's name is on the access register |
| A statement taken | Something said to Ruth becomes evidence | An appointment in a book other people can read |
| A records request | Phone or bank records for one person | It takes three working days. Ask too late and it arrives after the sale |
| The car | Nkuna comes to one place, once | It is the only car. On day 11 it is behind a blockade |
| An arrest | Khoza will pick up one person on Ruth's word | If she is wrong, he has nothing left to give |

There are five favours and more than five uses for them. Between the money and the favours, the player feels exactly how little the law can do, and that the people in it are trying.

Khoza's own moves are small and real: sending an exhibit to a second laboratory when the first report leaves something out, holding someone on an unrelated charge so the others are not warned, and trying to get the docket to an outside unit before it is taken from him.

**The deaths nobody investigates.** In the same two weeks the station also gets a stabbing outside a tavern, a woman killed by her partner, and a man beaten to death by a crowd as a suspected thief. They are folders on Khoza's desk and items on the radio. Nobody is sent. The game should let the player notice which deaths get a detective.

**What it costs them.** The epilogue shows it. Khoza loses the case. Nkuna is still driving the same car.

**What is invented and what is borrowed.** Steyn's firm is drawn from the real pattern here of armed-response companies and policing forums working together, including joining vehicle chases. Mokoena's forum is a provincial and national pattern; no such incident was found at Secunda itself. Lenders who hold borrowers' bank cards are documented elsewhere in Mpumalanga, not here. All three are plausible inventions and should be written as that.

**Language.** In town, conversation defaults to Afrikaans and English; in eMbalenhle, to isiZulu. Ruth moves between them. Underground workers wear one-piece boiler suits in white, yellow or orange, not blue two-piece overalls.

## 7. The story

### The two killings

The game has two murders and each looks like the other kind.

- **Daniel Brandt, day 1.** A broadcaster exposing a mine is shot outside his station. The town assumes a hit. The police call it a robbery, because his phone and laptop are missing and a robbery can be closed. Both readings suit someone: the station is spared an unsolvable hit, and the people behind the fraud are content to be suspected of a killing they did not commit so long as nobody looks at the books. It is neither: it is personal, done by someone one step from him, which is what most murders here are.
- **Anneline Petersen, day 11.** A lab manager is shot in her car at the gate of her complex and her laptop and bag are taken. It looks like a hijacking gone wrong. It is a paid killing, the rare kind that is rising and that almost never reaches the person who paid.

### The storylines

Six threads. None is a side story: each supplies a cause, a clue or a cost for another. No single person planned it all. They connect through debts, mistakes and self-interest.

| Thread | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The strike** | Three years ago the underground crews refused an instruction to load waste rock in with the coal. Brandt called it an unprotected strike, brought in Steyn, and dismissed them. Their case is still in the labour courts |
| **The fraud** | Since then the colliery has sold stone as coal on false certificates. The proof is the crate: sealed retained samples and the lab's day book, which goes back to the week of the strike |
| **The debts** | The dismissed men borrowed from Grace Tau to live. Others stood surety for them. She holds their cards |
| **Daniel's murder** | Day 1 |
| **The hit** | Day 11 |
| **The shutdown** | Twenty thousand strangers in town, every bed taken, the water off, and a power cut in the centre |

How they lock together:

1. **The strike made the debts.** Dismissed crews borrowed from Grace. Sam Nel, Daniel's technician, stood surety for his brother, one of them.
2. **The debts killed Daniel.** Sam could not pay, so he sold Grace what Daniel was working on. Daniel found out. The chain that ends in Daniel's death starts with his father's decision three years ago, and neither man knows it.
3. **Daniel's murder told everyone about the crate.** Grace sold what Sam told her to Steyn and to Mokoena. By the funeral all four are asking Ruth for "it", each believing it is something different.
4. **The crate is two things.** The tins prove the fraud and matter to the buyers. The day book proves why the crews refused to load, and matters to Joseph and the dismissed men. At the sale Ruth can send them to different people.
5. **The crate needs Anneline.** Tins are only coal unless the person who sealed them says so under oath. She agrees to make a statement. That makes her the most valuable person in town and the most exposed.
6. **Protecting her exposes her.** If Ruth asks Captain Khoza to take the statement, the appointment is seen at the station by an officer who moonlights for Steyn. If Ruth tells any of the four instead, it travels the same way. Every route to keeping Anneline safe is a route by which Steyn learns she will talk.
7. **Everyone has a hand in the hit and nobody planned it alone.** Steyn takes the news to Brandt as leverage. Brandt pays. Steyn arranges it and so owns Brandt, which is all he ever wanted. The gunman is hired from outside the province through one of Mokoena's drivers, without Mokoena knowing. The pistol is one "lost" from Steyn's own armoury. The gunman takes a bed in the only place with a bed left, Grace Tau's guesthouse, down the corridor from Ruth.
8. **The shutdown hides it.** One more stranger in a hired bakkie is invisible. The power cut killed the cameras in the centre on the first night. On day 11 Mokoena's forum blockades the roads over docked haulage and Tuesday's raid, and the one police car that works cannot get through to Anneline's complex.
9. **The hit reaches back to the strike.** The pistol was used before: a shaft steward was shot and wounded during the strike, a docket that has sat in Khoza's pile for three years. The cartridge cases match. Proving it takes a ballistics laboratory with a backlog of tens of thousands of cases. The result arrives long after Friday.

### The hidden truth, plainly

- **The fraud.** Brandtskraal's remaining coal is thin and full of stone. Certificates have been false for three years. Brandt paid Anneline to sign them. The colliery is to be sold on the second Friday on the strength of its sales contracts. This follows documented coal-quality frauds in Mpumalanga; the sampling and laboratory detail needs review by someone who has worked in a coal lab.
- **Who killed Daniel.** Sam Nel, with Daniel's own pistol from the station desk, on the afternoon of day 1 when Daniel confronted him.
- **Who dressed the scene.** Steyn's men, by the next morning, to point at Mokoena's drivers, who are already suspected of swapping loads. Steyn wants the forum off his gate.
- **Who killed Anneline.** A hired man who does not know who paid. Brandt paid. Steyn arranged it.
- **Where the crate is.** Daniel left it with the one person nobody would search: it is in Joseph Sithole's back room in eMbalenhle, and Joseph has not opened it.

### The object

Nobody Ruth meets describes the crate the same way.

| Who | What they say it is |
|---|---|
| Hendrik Brandt | Old lab rejects that should have been thrown out |
| Steyn | Evidence. Whoever holds it holds Brandt |
| Mokoena | Proof that his drivers never touched the loads. Customers have been docking his haulage for delivering stone |
| Grace Tau | Collateral. Daniel pledged it against a loan for the station's licence |
| Khoza | There is no crate. There is a robbery |
| Anneline | Three months of what we really dug, and a book that says why the men stopped |
| Joseph | A box Daniel asked him to keep. He never looked |

### Clues that break each false story

Taken from how real staged scenes were undone.

- **Day 1 is not a robbery:** Daniel's phone and laptop bag are gone, which is why the docket says robbery, but his wallet, watch and car are not. **And not a hit:** one shot, close, from a pistol kept in the station desk; no car waiting; the emergency call came twenty minutes late and from Sam's phone.
- **Sam:** his phone was switched off for the hour that matters. A loan settlement at Grace's is dated that same morning, before Daniel died. He had already listed the station's mixing desk for sale.
- **The frame:** the cartridge case Steyn's men "found" the next day is the wrong calibre for the wound the forensic officer at the Evander mortuary describes.
- **Day 11 is not a hijacking:** her car was left; only the work laptop and bag went. The gunman's bakkie was stolen in another province four months earlier. It happened an hour before she was due to make a statement. The gate motor had been disabled the night before. The guesthouse register has a guest who paid cash, gave a contractor's name that no contractor recognises, and left at four in the morning.

### Act by act

- **Act I, days 1–2.** Ruth arrives at dusk. Daniel is not at the guesthouse. She finds the crowd outside the radio station, in the dark, because the power in the centre is out. Khoza gives her the official version; Joseph, who was Daniel's contact, gives her the background and says nothing about the box in his back room; Grace gives her the last free room in town. At night Ruth can go back into the station, which is the stealth tutorial, and see the scene before it is dressed. A car from the farm fetches her, and Brandt hires her to find who killed his son. Next morning, the post-mortem.
- **Act II, days 3–7.** The murder. Three questions on the board: was it a robbery, whose gun, who gains. Sam's flat, Daniel's house, Grace's loan book, the last broadcast, instalment night, the bus, payday. Ruth names someone to Brandt and Khoza when she is ready, and no later than the funeral. If she names Mokoena's drivers, Steyn raids the truck yard that night with her finding as his warrant. At the funeral every one of the four separately asks whether she has "it". That afternoon Brandt thanks her, pays her, and tells her to go. On Sunday evening, Anneline.
- **Act III, days 8–11.** The fraud. The buyers come to test the coal and Ruth can watch the test being rigged. She has a witness, a detective with five favours, and a little money each morning. She works out what the crate is and that it is two things. She decides who to trust with Anneline's statement. A stranger takes the last bed. On day 11 the roads close, Anneline is killed on her way to the station, and Ruth, the last person seen with her, crosses town on foot to Joseph's house. That evening she learns where the gunman is to be paid, and what is on the floor above. The finale follows.
- **Act IV, days 12–14.** Before dawn on Friday Ruth drives out to the farm and sits across from Brandt one last time. There is no fight; he is an old man in a chair. The buyers' cars arrive at the colliery office at 10:00 on Friday. Ruth chooses what happens to the tins and to the day book, together or apart: the buyers, Brandt, Joseph and the dismissed men's lawyer, Khoza's docket, the air from Daniel's station, or the slimes dam. That night the plant restarts. On Saturday she makes her statement. On Sunday she drives out past the plant.

### The finale: the music school, day 11, 21:00

The last playable action in the game, and the one place where it can get heavy. It is the hit's own aftermath arriving somewhere it was never meant to be.

**The building.** A five-storey 1980s office block in the town centre, half let (fictional; whether the real centre has a block this tall is not confirmed). Steyn's firm holds its security contract. The player has been here since day 1 and knows it floor by floor before the finale starts.

| Floor | What is there | When the player learns it |
|---|---|---|
| Roof | Daniel's antenna mast and the plant room | Day 1 |
| 5 | Daniel's radio station | Day 1, the murder scene and the stealth tutorial |
| 4 | A community music school: a rehearsal hall, six practice rooms, an instrument store. Youth orchestra and adult choir | Optional visits on day 4 and day 9, when Ruth can sit in on a rehearsal and meet the director |
| 3 | A labour broker's office, the front for the driver who hired the gunman, and where shutdown contractors buy drugs | Day 8 onward, if Ruth follows the driver |
| 2 | Empty offices, glass partitions, stacked furniture | |
| 1 | Steyn's control room for this building: cameras, door locks, the lift | Day 2, if she asks to see Monday's footage and is told there is none |
| Ground | Lobby and guard desk, Grace Tau's cash-loan shop, a shuttered pharmacy | Day 1 |
| Basement | Parking, the generator, the service lift, the loading door | |

**What is happening.** The gunman is owed the second half of his fee. The driver is to pay him at 21:00 on the third floor, in cash and product, in a building that should be empty. Brandt and Steyn have decided otherwise: a four-man team from Steyn's firm will kill both men at the handover and leave it looking like a drug deal that went wrong. It is the same logic as the morning's killing, applied to the people who did it.

It goes wrong because of the blockade. The rehearsal for the Heritage Day concert, which should have finished at half past eight, started late because nobody could get across town, and at 21:00 there are forty people on the fourth floor, half of them under eighteen. The driver, who has been expecting something like this, runs upstairs into the rehearsal with the cash. Steyn's team now has two targets in a room full of witnesses and a guard force on the door that takes its orders from them.

**Who is there with Ruth.** The blockade has the station's other officers on the wrong side of town. She has Captain Khoza, whose daughter plays in the orchestra, Constable Nkuna, and whatever she has built over ten days.

**The objective.** Get from the street to the fourth floor and get forty people out before the team goes up. Everything else is optional: the driver alive, the gunman alive, Steyn's men alive, the day book that Joseph, who sings bass, has brought with him to hand over.

**The clock.** The player arrives at about 20:50. The handover is at 21:00. The team needs a few minutes on the third floor and then starts up the stairs. Being early changes everything; so does being late.

**Ways in.**

| Route | What it takes | What it risks |
|---|---|---|
| The lobby | Khoza's badge. The guards stall and radio upstairs | The team knows police are in the building |
| Talking the guards round | A guard Ruth has spoken to before, and something to Show them: this is not a job they were told about | Works only if she spent time on them earlier |
| The basement ramp | An access card lifted from a guard, or the loading door forced | Noise. Leads to the generator and the service lift |
| Grace Tau's shop | Grace, if she is still on speaking terms, has a back door into the service corridor | Owing Grace |
| The roof | Across from the next building and down through the station, which Ruth knows in the dark | Slow. Arrives above the fourth floor, not below it |
| The fire stair | An alarmed external door | The alarm, which is also a tool |

**Ways up.** The main stair, which the team will use. The fire stair. The service lift from the basement. The passenger lift, which runs only if the control room lets it. A service riser with a ladder between floors 1 and 5.

**The building as a set of switches.** This is the Crusader part: the level is a machine and the player can work it.

- **The control room.** Take it and Ruth sees every floor, locks and unlocks the fire doors, and holds the lift. Lose it and the team sees her.
- **The power.** The distribution board is in the basement. Cut it and the building goes dark: good for stealth, bad for forty frightened people, and the lift stops where it is. The generator can bring back the emergency lights only.
- **The fire alarm.** Empties the fourth floor down the fire stair without anyone having to be told why. Also releases every magnetic lock in the building, for both sides.
- **The sprinklers and extinguishers.** Cover, not weapons.
- **Glass and furniture on the second floor.** It breaks, and it can be stacked.
- **The station's desk on the fifth floor.** It still drives a speaker in every corridor. Ruth can talk to the whole building, or put Daniel's signal back on the air and tell the town what is happening. People come. Four men with rifles do not want a crowd.

**The people as options.** This is the Deus Ex part: nearly everyone in the building can be turned, used, or left alone, depending on what Ruth knows and what she did earlier.

- **The guards on the door** earn very little and were told to keep people out. Some will walk away if they understand what is upstairs.
- **The music director** will move forty people quietly if asked the right way, and can keep the orchestra playing while they go, one desk at a time, so the floor below hears no change. The music carries on through the whole sequence until the hall is empty.
- **The gunman** is about to be killed by the people who hired him. Warned, he is a second armed man on the third floor who wants out. He is also the man who shot Anneline.
- **The driver** is the only person who met both sides. If he walks out alive, the ending is different.
- **Mokoena** can lift the blockade with a phone call, which brings the rest of the station and an ambulance, and puts Ruth in his debt in front of the police.
- **Khoza and Nkuna** do what the player asks. Each can hold a stair, run the evacuation, or come up with Ruth. Either can be hurt.

**Ways it can go.**

| Approach | Shape |
|---|---|
| Quiet | In by the basement or the roof, control room first, fire doors locked on the team, hall emptied down the fire stair to music. Possibly no shot fired |
| Loud | Up the main stair behind Khoza's badge. Short, close, final. Everyone is as fragile as Ruth is |
| Sideways | The alarm, the speakers, the crowd, the blockade lifted. Ruth barely enters the building and the team leaves on its own, with both its targets still alive |
| Late | The handover has happened. Two men are dead on the third floor and the team is on the stairs |

**What the game counts.** How many of the forty were hurt, and it should be possible, and hard, for that to be none. Who of the driver, the gunman and Steyn's four is alive. Whether Khoza and Nkuna are. Whether the day book came out. How many people Ruth killed. All of it goes on the last screen.

**Why it belongs.** No attack like this is documented in Secunda; it is invention. It is built from documented parts: a hit cleaned up by killing the hired men, a drug sale as the cover, a security firm that controls a building, and a police station with two officers free. It also puts the cost where the research says it falls, on people who were only in the room.

### How it ends

Ruth gets out. That is the only thing the player can be sure of.

- The sale collapsing closes the mine now. The sale going through closes it within the year with the money gone. Neither saves a job.
- Sam Nel can be convicted, if Ruth named him. It is the one clean result available and it is the least important.
- The gunman, if he lived through the finale, pleads, takes fifteen to twenty years, and says truthfully that he does not know who paid. The driver, the one person who met both sides, decides everything: dead, the chain ends with him; alive, he can name Steyn, and whether he does depends on what protection Khoza can offer, which is very little.
- Brandt is seventy-eight and will not live to stand trial. Steyn is charged only if the driver lives and talks, and even then the trial is years away. Without Anneline the tins are disputed for years.
- The day book can win the dismissed men their case. The judgment comes after the mine has closed.
- Khoza is taken off the docket, which goes to a provincial unit and stops moving. The ballistics match on the strike shooting comes back the following winter.

The epilogue is playable. The town centre and the colliery gate reflect who is alive, what burned, what Ruth chose. Survivors will talk. The last screen gives the number of dead, the names, and what became of each case, dated, in the flat language of a court roll.

## 8. Investigation

The case board is the wall of Ruth's room and a full-screen view anywhere else. There is no quest log and no objective marker.

- **Clue:** one fact, marked as *seen* (Ruth observed it), *said* (someone told her), or *written* (a document or recording, which can be false).
- **Lead:** an open question a clue raises. Leads are the to-do list and softly mark the people and places they touch.
- **Insight:** two or three clues the player links on the board. A valid link produces a new card at once.
- **Question:** each act poses two or three. Each takes one answer.
- **Confrontation:** naming an answer to someone with power. Only then does the game respond to whether it was right.

**What Ruth can lawfully reach.** She has a citizen's powers and no more. She cannot get phone or bank records herself; those come only through Khoza's records request. What she can work is what solved the real cases: security-company control-room logs, private cameras, vehicle-tracker reports held by owners and insurers, policy and loan paperwork, copies the victim left elsewhere (a message to a friend, a draft sent to a private address, a recording on the station computer), and people. Which language a person will talk in, and to whom, decides what she learns.

Rules:

1. Every insight needed for the truth can be reached at least three ways: looking, talking, or getting into somewhere Ruth should not be.
2. Linking is free and immediate. Answers are never checked until they are spoken to someone.
3. Each act has a planted answer with real support, and one *seen* clue that breaks it.
4. A wrong answer moves the story on along a worse road. There is no fail screen for being wrong.
5. Time removes evidence: scenes are cleaned, people leave on the morning bus, documents are shredded. The last route to a required insight is never removed.

**Objects.** There are no quest items. Every object has what it does, what it shows, and who reacts to it. Daniel's pistol is a weapon, the murder weapon, and the thing Sam cannot look at. The crate is forty sealed tins of crushed coal and a day book, the proof, and something each of the four wants for a different reason.

## 9. Conversations

Named characters are spoken to face to face: the view cuts from the isometric town to a first-person frame, a large portrait against the room Ruth is standing in, in the manner of Dune (1992). A short list of commands sits at the bottom right.

- **Ask about…** topics, which exist because of clues and leads Ruth holds.
- **Show…** any object or clue card.
- **Press** when what they said conflicts with something *seen*.
- **Tell…** give them a clue or an answer. Ruth chooses which, and can leave part of it out. She cannot invent facts, but she can tell different people different true things.
- **Offer…** money from what Ruth has left, silence, a lift out of town.
- **Leave.**

**Night resolves the town.** Each of the four acts overnight on what they currently believe: who has the crate, who killed Daniel, who is talking to Ruth. The morning shows the result, such as a burned scrap yard, a missing guard, a new man on the gate, or a body. This is the engine of acts II and III, and the reason the body count is the player's.

## 10. Stealth

Used for places Ruth has no right to be (the station at night, the colliery lab, the contractor camp, the farmhouse, Mokoena's truck yard) and for all of day 11.

- Guards see in cones, hear footsteps on gravel and iron sheeting, and follow routes tied to the shift clock.
- Darkness hides; a torch reveals both ways.
- Distractions are ordinary: a thrown stone, a car alarm, a phone left ringing.
- Ruth can hold someone from behind and put them down without killing them. They wake later and remember her.
- Being seen is not instant failure. People ask what she is doing, and in daylight a good answer often works.
- A drawn weapon changes every conversation in town.

## 11. Weapons, armour, health

No upgrades, no attachments, no ammunition types, no experience.

- **Weapons:** Ruth's 9 mm pistol with two magazines. Found during play: a revolver, a pump shotgun, a hunting rifle. Improvised: a torch, a tyre iron. She carries one long gun and one handgun. Ammunition is counted in single rounds and there is little of it.
- **Armour:** one covert vest, obtainable in the second week through Khoza or bought second-hand. It stops pistol rounds a few times, slows her slightly, and is noticed in conversation.
- **Health:** three states: fine, hurt, badly hurt. A first-aid kit takes her up one state. The clinic treats her fully but costs a block and the nurse asks questions. Wounds carry over to the next day.
- **Enemies** are as fragile as she is. Two or three hits ends anyone. A fight with more than four opponents is one to avoid or to arrange allies for.
- Death reloads the start of the current block.

## 12. Camera and controls

- 3D town, fixed-pitch isometric camera, free rotation, three zoom steps. Walls and roofs between camera and Ruth fade.
- WASD or click to move, mouse to aim, one interact key with a small radial (Look, Take, Use, Talk).
- **Focus** (hold): the view desaturates and anything examinable nearby is outlined.
- Crouch, lean at corners, and a noise ring under Ruth's feet when moving.

## 13. First build

Day 1 only: arrival, the crowd at the station, three conversations, the room and the board, the night entry into the station, the drive to the farm. About forty minutes. It exercises movement, Focus, conversation, clue capture, linking, stealth and one possible gunfight.

Engine is still open. With co-op gone, a browser build (Three.js) remains the fastest route to something playable and testable here; Unity or Godot are equally reasonable for the full game.

## 14. What the research says to leave out

No serial killer. No occult angle for colour. No murdered woman's body as spectacle. No hostel or informal settlement as scenery. No foreign crime boss. No single corrupt official who explains everything. No quick forensic breakthrough. Ruth is not a drinker with a past in the police; she is a file-builder.
