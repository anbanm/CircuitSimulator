# South Africa jails gunmen, not paymasters

Most South African murders are weekend arguments between men who know each other, about one in nine leads to an arrest, and the stranger with a balaclava is the explanation that most often turns out to be false. Contract killing is a real and cheap market (court-reported fees run from R5,000 for a domestic job to R120,000 split among a crew for a councillor), staffed from the taxi industry, hostels, gangs and serving or former police, and its settled outcome is that the gunmen are sometimes convicted while the person who paid is not: in April 2026 Daily Maverick counted at least ten professionals killed in three years with no mastermind arrested. Hits are usually recognised within hours because nothing is taken; the commoner error is a wrong motive, with the public fixing on the victim's most famous enemy while the evidence later points somewhere smaller and closer. In the famous "murder mysteries" the hidden killer was almost always a husband, wife, child or neighbour, the cover story was a hijacking, robbery, farm attack or suicide, and it was undone by dull records (phone towers, insurance paperwork, a late emergency call) and not by a laboratory. For a story set in Secunda this means the two killings the plot needs are each well precedented, the weak but honest detective is the statistical norm, a private investigator has documented real-world counterparts, and an ending in which the payer is known but uncharged is the realistic one and not a noir affectation. It also means the setting is open ground: no crime novel, series or film set in the Highveld coal towns was found. Roughly a third of what follows rests on search extracts only, several of the central cases are still before the courts, and both limits are marked throughout.

## How to read this report: two grades of source and three grades of claim

Five research files underlie this report. Unlike the two earlier reports in this series (on Secunda as a setting and on crime in mining towns), most of the pages behind this one were **read in full**: the Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime (GI-TOC) reports, Institute for Security Studies (ISS) analyses, Daily Maverick, GroundUp, The Citizen and its local titles, and Wikipedia articles. In this report **a citation with no tag was read in full**. A citation marked **"extract"** rests only on a search-result summary; the page could not be opened (News24, EWN, TimesLIVE, IOL, Mail & Guardian, SABC, the police and prosecution sites and the court-judgment site SAFLII were all unreachable), so its dates, figures and wording are provisional. This is the reverse of the tagging used in the mining-towns report, where extracts were the majority. Wikipedia is labelled as such; it is a tertiary source and several of the case articles are thin or out of date. **No court judgment and no police statistical release was read first-hand**: every police figure is second-hand through the press or ISS, and every judicial finding comes from press reports.

Claims come in three grades. A **conviction**, **acquittal** or **sentence** is stated as such. An **arrest**, **charge** or pending **trial** means only that, and everyone so described is presumed innocent. An **allegation** covers commission testimony that has not been tested in a criminal court, a journalist's reconstruction, a party's claim or an anonymous source, and is labelled in the sentence. Victims, convicted people and office-holders are named where the sources name them; people who are only accused are described by role wherever the point can be made without the name. All statuses are as of the latest source found, with a research date of 22 November 2026; a verdict delivered in the days before that could have been missed.

## The typical murder is a weekend argument, and nine in ten go unpunished

### The rate halved, climbed for a decade and is falling again

South Africa's murder rate fell from about **67 per 100,000 in 1994/95** to a low of about **30 in 2011/12**, rose to about **45 in 2022/23**, and has since fallen for three years to **36.6 in 2025/26** ([Wikipedia, Crime in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa); [GroundUp, 29 May 2026](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/)). In counts, that is 15,554 murders in 2011/12 ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/reducing-murder-must-be-a-top-sa-government-priority)), a peak of 27,621 in 2023/24 and 24,692 in 2024/25 ([Daily Maverick, 6 Dec 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/)). No official 2025/26 total was found; the four quarterly figures sum to about 23,100, which is the researchers' arithmetic and matches the ISS statement that the year saw 16% fewer murders than 2023/24 ([ISS, 10 June 2026](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)). Even at 36.6 the rate is about three times the African average and more than six times the world average ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/)).

Why it is falling is openly disputed. ISS researchers tie the 2011 to 2023 rise to the weakening of state institutions and the local embedding of criminal groups ([Daily Maverick, 28 May 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-28-crime-rates-show-hopeful-decline-is-south-africa-turning-the-corner-on-violence/)); for the fall, named analysts variously credit hotspot policing, the end of load-shedding and falling robbery, and one doubts the data because "nothing significant has changed" ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)). A story should not have a character explain the trend with confidence.

### Mpumalanga sits below the national rate, with a 2023 spike and a 2026 uptick

The provinces differ by a factor of five. In 2025/26 the Eastern Cape recorded 61.3 murders per 100,000 and the Western Cape 58.3; KwaZulu-Natal 38.8 and Gauteng 35.0; **Mpumalanga 20.7**; Limpopo 12.1 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)). Mpumalanga's recent history is a sharp rise and fall: 19.5 in 2020/21, **31.4 in 2023/24** (its worst since 2004), back to 20.7 two years later. Quarterly counts were 285 in October to December 2025 and 253 in January to March 2026 ([Lowvelder, 10 Mar 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2026/03/10/third-quarter-statistics-show-decrease-in-general-crime-in-mpumalanga/); [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/)). In April to June 2026, however, Mpumalanga and North West were the only provinces where murder rose ([Daily Maverick, 28 Aug 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-08-28-murder-drops-5-9-but-political-killings-spike-ahead-of-local-government-elections/)), in Mpumalanga by 14 cases ([The Citizen, Sept 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/mpumalanga-crime-stats-robbery-plunges-murder-and-rape-surge/)).

Murder is extremely concentrated: half of all murders occur in fewer than 170 of 1,175 station areas, mostly dense townships and informal settlements in the four big metros ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/reducing-murder-must-be-a-top-sa-government-priority); [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/murder-down-almost-10-majority-cases-reported-these-four-provinces/)). No Mpumalanga station is in the current national top tier. The province's violence shows up in Lowveld township precincts (the Ehlanzeni district alone held 44% of the province's murders in late 2023) ([Lowvelder, 15 Mar 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2024/03/15/216-murders-in-ehlanzeni-in-three-months/)), in illegal-mining shootouts on the escarpment, and on the Highveld in eMbalenhle, the township beside Secunda's plant, which leads crime in the Gert Sibande district with murder, house robbery and gender-based violence as its main problems ([Ridge Times, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/12/09/embalenhle-struggles-with-highest-crime-rate-in-district/)). eMbalenhle's 85 murders in 2016/17 made it the only Mpumalanga station in the national top 30 that year ([Lowvelder, extract](https://citizen.co.za/lowvelder/?p=503555)). No current station-level murder ranking for the province was found. The earlier mining-towns report gives the local picture in detail; the short version is that Secunda town records theft and eMbalenhle records roughly a murder a week.

### Arguments, public places, weekend nights and illegal guns

The police publish a "causative factor" analysis each quarter from a sample of dockets. Motive is established for well under half of all murders, so every share below is a share of known motives, not of all murders.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct–Dec 2024 | 6,953 | 1,453 | 403 | 389 | 217 | 294 | [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/saps-crime-statistics-leading-causes-of-murder/) |
| Jan–Mar 2026 | 5,181 | 898 | 235 | 299 | 251 | 242 | [GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/) |

**Arguments are the largest identified motive by a wide margin.** Taxi and hijacking murders run to dozens a quarter (57 and 48 in late 2024). An older docket study of 2,912 murders from 2015 is more detailed: a motive could be established in only 60% of dockets; of those, 51.5% arose from an argument, 20% during another crime such as robbery and 10% from vigilantism; where a relationship could be established, **70% of offenders were known to the victim**; and 96% of identified perpetrators were men ([ISS Today, 7 Nov 2017](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/what-do-we-know-about-murder-in-south-africa)). ISS's 2026 reading is the same: the biggest driver is "arguments between people who know each other", not robbery networks ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/debate-about-sa-s-falling-murder-levels-masks-a-deeper-crisis)).

About half of murders happen in public places and about three in ten in a home: in January to March 2026, 2,587 in public places, 1,523 at the home of victim or perpetrator and 160 at liquor outlets ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/sa-murder-rate-edges-down/)). Half occur over weekends and many involve alcohol or drugs ([Daily Maverick, 28 May 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-05-28-crime-rates-show-hopeful-decline-is-south-africa-turning-the-corner-on-violence/)); a 2018/19 police analysis put the worst window between 21:00 on Saturday and 03:00 on Sunday ([IOL, extract](https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/western-cape/2019-09-12-crime-stats-youre-most-likely-to-be-murdered-between-9pm-and-3am-on-a-weekend/)). Victims are overwhelmingly men between about 17 and 40 ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/three-lives-lost-every-hour-despite-murder-drop/)).

**Guns are the main weapon: 42% of murders in late 2024**, against 17% for knives ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/saps-crime-statistics-leading-causes-of-murder/)). Parliamentary replies, as read by a political party and a lobby group, give 9,398 firearm murders in 2025/26, of which 99.2% were committed with illegal guns and 75 by the legal owner of a licensed one ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/99-of-firearm-murders-in-south-africa-committed-with-illegal-guns/)). "Illegal" there includes stolen licensed guns. The supply is domestic leakage: state bodies lose an estimated 1,800 firearms a year, at least 357 vanished from police evidence stores between 2020 and 2023 ([Daily Maverick, 18 Oct 2024](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-10-18-firearm-fiasco-government-loses-1800-firearms-annually-to-criminals/)), civilians reported about 33,000 lost or stolen over five years, and ballistic testing shows **20% of recovered guns had already been used in a murder** ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/inside-the-collapse-of-police-gun-control/)). Close to Secunda, a police R5 rifle allegedly stolen by a constable was linked to the 2025 murder of a Kinross taxi boss ([Ridge Times, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/12/09/embalenhle-struggles-with-highest-crime-rate-in-district/)).

### Women are killed by partners; crowds kill suspected thieves

About **2,400 women are murdered a year**, a rate of 10.6 per 100,000 women, and nearly 60% are killed by an intimate partner ([Bhekisisa in Daily Maverick, 14 Oct 2024](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-10-13-heres-where-women-in-south-africa-are-most-likely-to-be-killed/)). The share of femicide cases in which no perpetrator is ever identified rose from 30% in 2017 to **44% in 2020/21** ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/whos-killing-sa-women-femicide-rates/)), and fewer than one in five intimate-partner femicides ends in a conviction ([SAMRC, extract](https://www.samrc.ac.za/press-releases/south-africas-femicide-crisis-persists-new-report-highlights-increase-intimate)). An advocacy op-ed adds that women are most at risk from a licensed gun owned by a partner ([Daily Maverick, 25 Feb 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/opinionista/2025-02-25-unpacking-the-deadly-mix-behind-south-africas-violent-gun-driven-crime-epidemic/)).

Mob justice is one of the top five identified motives. Police attributed 849 murders to it in 2017/18 and **2,124 in 2022/23** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_South_Africa)), and about 300 to 390 a quarter since. The triggers are suspected theft, robbery, rape and witchcraft accusations in places where people do not expect the police to act; two-thirds of people told a 2018 survey they mostly distrust the police ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/is-mob-violence-out-of-control-in-south-africa)). Those targeted are often not the people responsible ([The Citizen, extract](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/mob-justice-fed-up-communities-dont-trust-police-to-protect-them)). No Mpumalanga-specific count was found. By contrast farm murders, which dominate public argument, number in the tens a year and under 1% of murders, and the 2003 police inquiry found most were for material gain ([Wikipedia, South African farm attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_farm_attacks)).

### An arrest is the exception

The police's murder detection rate, meaning a suspect arrested and charged, is **about 11%**, and the conviction rate measured against murders is about 12 to 13% ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/detective-task-teams-a-stopgap-response-to-sa-s-crime-crisis); [Daily Maverick, 6 Dec 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/)). In the 2015 docket study it was 24% ([ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/what-do-we-know-about-murder-in-south-africa)), so it has halved in a decade. The prosecution's own conviction rate looks high because it enrols only cases it expects to win ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/three-lives-lost-every-hour-despite-murder-drop/)). Among 35 high-profile stations, 27 detect under 10% of murders ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/heres-how-many-murders-successfully-investigated-police/)). The reasons given are consistent: about 18,000 detectives, some carrying 200 or more dockets ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/detective-task-teams-a-stopgap-response-to-sa-s-crime-crisis)); nearly 250,000 overdue DNA exhibits, with **only 1.5% of 2025/26 DNA profiles finalised within the statutory 30 days** and cases struck off the roll when reports are missing ([Daily Maverick, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-15-only-15-of-saps-dna-samples-are-processed-within-target-time/)); and courts with 2% more judges than in 2011/12 ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/our-justice-system-is-grinding-to-a-halt/)). These four detection and conviction figures use different definitions and years, and none could be traced to a readable police or prosecution table.

## Hired killing is cheap, staffed from taxis and police, and stops at the gunman

### Two or three recorded hits a week, and an undercount by design

GI-TOC defines a hit as a killing with a specific target and an arrangement between the person who ordered it and the person who did it, for money or other gain, and sorts cases into four motives: political, taxi, organised crime and personal ([GI-TOC, Ballots and Bullets, Oct 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). The police publish no such category. GI-TOC's press-derived database holds more than 2,500 incidents since 2000: roughly 50 to 90 a year until 2014, a peak of **211 in 2018**, then 124 to 145 a year, with 130 in 2024 and 137 in 2025. The first nine months of 2026 produced **205 incidents**, but GI-TOC itself warns that more intensive monitoring inflates the 2026 figure and that only the political series is comparable across years (same report). That series reached a record **45 incidents with at least 43 dead** by 30 September, against 23 in the same period of 2025, ahead of the November 2026 local elections. Political killings cluster in municipal election years because a councillor's seat brings salary, influence over jobs and housing lists, and proximity to procurement.

The mix has shifted. Taxi conflict supplied 46% of hits from 2000 to 2022 ([GI-TOC, The Business of Killing, 2023](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Rumbi-Matamba-The-business-of-killing-Assassinations-in-SA.-April23-web.pdf)); in 2026 organised crime is the largest category at 78 incidents (36%), with political and taxi at 45 each, only 7 classed as personal, and 44 with no established motive ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). The count is low by construction. It is built from press reports and misses rural cases, gang hits, and **hits disguised as robberies, hijackings or accidents**, some of which are recognised only years later in court ([GI-TOC, 2023](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Rumbi-Matamba-The-business-of-killing-Assassinations-in-SA.-April23-web.pdf)). The personal category is the one most likely to be undercounted, and GI-TOC notes that it changes as cases that "appear at face value to have other motives" are reclassified ([GI-TOC, 2 Oct 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/analysis/targeted-killings-south-africa-2026-local-government-elections/)).

### The men, the fees and the guns

Hitmen are "often recruited from armed gangs in the Western Cape and the minibus-taxi industry, particularly in KZN and the Eastern Cape. And, in some cases, perpetrators are hired from law enforcement agencies", and KwaZulu-Natal is "notorious for supplying hitmen to various other provinces" ([GI-TOC, 2023](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Rumbi-Matamba-The-business-of-killing-Assassinations-in-SA.-April23-web.pdf)). The research describes hired killing as "moonlighting" by a pool of trained muscle. The six men who pleaded guilty to killing Babita Deokaran had taxi-industry links, at most grade 11 schooling and monthly incomes of R1,500 to R20,000, and did not know who she was ([Daily Maverick, 22 Aug 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-08-22-babita-deokaran-killers-plead-guilty-and-get-jail-terms-but-the-shooter-and-assassination-mastermind-remain-at-large/)). The same men take political and domestic jobs: Sabelo Phewa, sentenced to life in September 2025 for killing a municipal manager who was probing tenders, had a month earlier killed a man whose partner paid R5,000 ([News24, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/crime-and-courts/life-sentence-for-hitman-who-killed-kzn-municipal-manager-probing-corruption-20250923-0680)). Police and soldiers recur. The National Commissioner told the Madlanga Commission that the vehicle and firearm used in the 2017 shooting of Sindiso Magaqa came from police Crime Intelligence and that 34 police officers had been charged over alleged roles in political killings ([Daily Maverick, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-25-political-killings-task-team-probe-leads-to-hitmans-conviction-while-madlanga-commission-continues/)). A GI-TOC analyst draws the consequence: "Where law enforcement is compromised, there are going to be efforts to sabotage investigations" ([Daily Maverick, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-15-10-professionals-assassinated-in-three-years-zero-masterminds-arrested/)).

Vehicles are stolen or borrowed: the Mercedes used to kill a Piet Retief councillor in November 2022 had been stolen in Brakpan months earlier, carried plates that appeared invalid and was abandoned beside a dam ([The Citizen / LNN, 5 Nov 2022](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/mpumalanga-councillor-fatally-shot-at-filling-station/)).

### Who orders, and how the job is done

Clients are insiders: taxi rivals, party comrades competing for seats, officials and contractors threatened by audits, and family members after insurance money. The Moerane Commission in KwaZulu-Natal found "overwhelming evidence ... that access to resources through the tender system is the main root cause of the murder of politicians", as quoted by GI-TOC ([GI-TOC, Murder by Contract, 2021](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/GITOC-Murder-by-Contract-Targeted-Killings-in-eastern-and-southern-Africa-.pdf)). The 2026 record adds finance officials, public-accounts committee members, school staff killed over posts, traditional leaders and state witnesses ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). The personal category covers love triangles, life-insurance payouts and children arranging parents' deaths; a Mpumalanga example tried at Mbombela in August 2026 is a 62-year-old man killed in 2020 and laid on railway tracks over a R40,000 policy that paid double for accidental death, for which a daughter pleaded guilty (same report).

The method is consistent. Firearms were used in 95% of hits from 2015 to 2020; 39% took place at the victim's home, 12% just outside it and 27% were drive-bys; victims were "typically targeted when they were either arriving at their homes or leaving them"; and **87% of recorded hits killed the target**, though only 77% of personal ones did, some plots failing through police stings or because the hired man warned the target ([GI-TOC, 2021](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/GITOC-Murder-by-Contract-Targeted-Killings-in-eastern-and-southern-Africa-.pdf)). GI-TOC grades a "probable hit" on four marks: a designated victim, shooters who arrive and leave quickly, **nothing stolen**, and earlier threats ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). Prior watching and repeat attempts are common: several 2026 victims were killed days or months after surviving a first attack. Bystanders die often, though no source gives a rate.

Disguise is documented and matters most for this story. Perpetrators "have often ... masked [hits] as failed hijackings or robberies" ([GI-TOC, The Rule of the Gun, 2018](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/The-rule-of-the-gun_Assassination-Witness.pdf)). On 2 April 2024 near Komatipoort an ANC councillor was shot in his driveway by four men in balaclavas who assaulted his wife and took cellphones and a Toyota bakkie; police opened murder and robbery dockets with motive unknown ([News24, 3 Apr 2024](https://www.news24.com/politics/mpumalanga-anc-councillor-gunned-down-at-his-house-his-wife-assaulted-20240403)). No source measures how often hits are staged as robberies.

### What solves a hit, and why the payer stays out of the dock

Solved cases turn on three things: one firearm matched by ballistics to several scenes, phone and vehicle location data, and an arrested participant who talks. The Political Killings Task Team (PKTT) caught Phewa in 2018 because a gun in his possession was linked to multiple murders, and by July 2024 had recovered 53 firearms linked to 59 political cases ([Daily Maverick, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-25-political-killings-task-team-probe-leads-to-hitmans-conviction-while-madlanga-commission-continues/); [The Citizen, 19 Jul 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/kzn-plagued-by-mass-murders-political-killings-mkhwanazi-notes-successful-convictions)). The legal instruments are the section 105A plea-and-sentence agreement and the section 204 accomplice witness who testifies in return for possible indemnity.

How far up convictions reach has no number. Arrests or charges were reported in at least 12 of 45 political incidents in the first nine months of 2026 and in fewer than 10 of 78 organised-crime incidents, and "even when gunmen are arrested, the people who ordered the killings are rarely identified" ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). Orderers are sometimes convicted: two ANC councillors for a 2014 killing, a taxi boss in the Bozwana case below, and the PKTT claims "more than 30" life sentences "for several hitmen and hit orderers" without giving a split ([Daily Maverick, 25 Sep 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-25-political-killings-task-team-probe-leads-to-hitmans-conviction-while-madlanga-commission-continues/)). Analysts explain the gap structurally: "often, the hitmen themselves don't know who the mastermind is", payment is informal, and arrests are "often limited to gunmen, drivers or facilitators" ([Daily Maverick, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-15-10-professionals-assassinated-in-three-years-zero-masterminds-arrested/)).

### The task team, the commission and the Mpumalanga clusters

The PKTT began work in 2018 after the Moerane Commission. Its record exists only as official claims that do not agree with one another year to year: in July 2024 the KwaZulu-Natal commissioner gave about 330 cases since 2018, 369 suspects charged, and 110 accused found guilty in 99 cases with 32 acquitted ([The Citizen, 19 Jul 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/kzn-plagued-by-mass-murders-political-killings-mkhwanazi-notes-successful-convictions)); ISS gives 333 cases, 191 finalised and 106 convictions ([ISS](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/detective-task-teams-a-stopgap-response-to-sa-s-crime-crisis)). On 31 December 2024 the Police Minister signed a directive to disestablish the team as "not adding any value"; the National Commissioner later called that claim "completely false" ([Daily Maverick, 22 Sep 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-22-encroachment-masemola-on-mchunus-killings-task-team-disbandment/)). On 6 July 2025 the provincial commissioner publicly alleged ministerial interference, and the President appointed the Madlanga Commission, whose hearings began on 17 September 2025 and whose deadline was extended to 16 November 2026 ([Wikipedia, Madlanga Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madlanga_Commission)). Its testimony, which is sworn but untested in criminal courts, includes claims of a Gauteng cartel involved in drugs, tender fraud and contract killings, and it has had consequences: a general was dismissed in June 2026 for misconduct including "associating himself with a known criminal" ([Daily Maverick, 5 Jun 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-06-05-madlanga-commission-fallout-deepens-as-shibiri-dismissed-nine-officers-suspended/)), and one of its witnesses was murdered. The team survived, was funded for 2025/26 and in 2026 worked cases in Gauteng, Mpumalanga and Cape Town ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). Whether the commission delivered a final report by its deadline was not found.

Mpumalanga is a low-count province in GI-TOC's data (two political incidents in the first nine months of 2026) with a long reputation and a poor record: Wikipedia, on sourcing from about 2011, cites 14 documented assassinations and one conviction ([Wikipedia, Political assassinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_assassinations_in_post-apartheid_South_Africa)). Its pattern is clusters. The first is Mbombela around the 2010 World Cup stadium tenders, where the municipal speaker Jimmy Mohlala was shot in January 2009 and arrests were later withdrawn ([Wikipedia, Jimmy Mohlala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Mohlala)). The second is ten taxi hits in Nkangala in 2021 after four in the previous five years, cause unknown ([GI-TOC, 2023](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Rumbi-Matamba-The-business-of-killing-Assassinations-in-SA.-April23-web.pdf)). The third is in **Gert Sibande, Secunda's own district**: Mkhondo councillor Muzi Manyathi was shot at a Piet Retief filling station on 4 November 2022, days after speaking publicly about threats, and a second councillor and two party members were shot there on 13 January 2023, with one arrested suspect from uMbumbulu in KwaZulu-Natal ([The Citizen / LNN, 5 Nov 2022](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/mpumalanga-councillor-fatally-shot-at-filling-station/); [The Citizen / LNN, 19 Jan 2023](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/anc-trio-murdered-in-mpumalanga-third-suspect-arrested/)). The fourth is organised-crime killings around Bushbuckridge in 2023 and 2026. The outcomes of nearly all of these are unknown. In 2026 a prosecutor in a Kwaggafontein extortion case turned back on the way to court after death threats, and the matter was struck from the roll and later re-enrolled ([GI-TOC, 2026](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Julian-Rademeyer-Ballots-and-bullets-Targeted-killings-and-South-Africas-2026-local-government-elections-GI-TOC-October-2026.pdf)). No study of the province's hit economy exists.

At Secunda itself the record is thin but not empty. The mining-towns report found one killing with the marks of a hit, the December 2023 shooting of an eMbalenhle ward councillor, with no arrests reported. This research adds a second, on an extract only: an eMbalenhle taxi boss shot at his gate in 2011, for which **his widow and two hitmen were convicted at Delmas eleven years later** ([IOL, extract](https://iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2022-09-18-11-years-later-widow-and-two-hitmen-convicted-for-murder-of-her-taxi-boss-husband/)). That qualifies the earlier report's statement that no local contract killing with a proven paymaster was found. A Wikipedia list entry for a Secunda deputy mayor killed in 2007 could not be corroborated and should not be used.

### The first explanation is usually "hit"; the first error is usually the motive

In the modern professional cases police call it a hit within hours, because "usually, nothing is taken during these shootings, pointing away from opportunistic crime" ([Daily Maverick, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-15-10-professionals-assassinated-in-three-years-zero-masterminds-arrested/)). An ordinary-crime first reading that later proved wrong appears in about three of the files, by the researchers' own count: Meyiwa ("robbery"), the police-watchdog investigator Mandla Mahlangu, who died in an "alleged 'botched' break-in" days before he was to testify ([Daily Maverick, 3 Jan 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-01-03-cellphone-towers-dont-lie-the-24-hour-potential-killer-in-your-pocket-could-also-help-solve-your-murder-heres-how/)), and the informant Jaco Hanekom, whose 2023 death was "initially reported as a hijacking and drive-by shooting" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaco_Hanekom)). These are the older, domestic-setting or low-visibility cases.

The commoner error is a wrong motive. The public tied the Murrays to their most notorious liquidations; Deokaran to a personal-protective-equipment scandal when her own messages pointed to payments at one hospital; Magaqa to party factions when the State's case is a municipal tender ([Wikipedia, Deokaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Babita_Deokaran); [Wikipedia, Magaqa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindiso_Magaqa)). In each the corrected motive was smaller and closer than the first. Two further misreadings recur. Officials minimise: after a municipal finance official was shot on CCTV near a police station in March 2026, the municipality "was quick to deny claims that [she] was a whistleblower". And an alternative motive is planted from the victim's past: an internal police note in the Rhadebe matter played up his taxi-industry history ([Daily Maverick / amaBhungane](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-05-09-murder-of-bazooka-radebe-in-xolobeni-hawks-general-accused-of-shutting-down-investigation/)). For comparison, real robbery killings look different: a community-radio music manager shot in Alexandra in July 2022 had his phone and shoes taken, and four others died in similar robberies nearby the same night ([Daily Maverick, 15 Jul 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-07-15-alex-fm-music-manager-among-at-least-5-people-killed-overnight-in-alexandra/)). No case was found in which a court established that a journalist or community broadcaster was killed by hired gunmen for their reporting.

### The gun, the phone and the junior man who talks

The decisive object is the firearm, because the same weapons are reused and rented across jobs. Guns seized after the Armand Swart shooting were linked to about 30 serious cases, including the killings of two DJs and the wounding of an actress ([Daily Maverick, 2 Jun 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-06-02-the-cop-who-allegedly-buried-the-bullets-forensic-analyst-faces-second-tampering-charge/)). Ballistics is also where cases are sabotaged: in that case the first report omitted 15 cartridges, investigators took the exhibits to a second laboratory in another province, and a police forensic analyst was arrested in May 2026 as an alleged accessory, which he denies (same source). Phone location data placed an accused near the AKA scene three minutes before the shooting ([The Citizen, 27 Mar 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/courts/cctv-cellphone-records-link-accused-to-aka-tibz-murder-state-argues-in-bail-case/)); vehicle-tracker data allegedly placed a policeman's vehicle at the alleged orderer's house and then at the target's workplace on five successive days ([Daily Maverick, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-10-22-armand-swart-killing-suspect-kt-molefes-nephew-on-the-run-since-2024/)). CCTV and neighbours find the car. Bank records and the victim's files explain the motive.

The decisive person is rarely an outside eyewitness. It is a survivor in the car, or a junior participant who pleads. Confessions are fragile: the confessions in the Meyiwa trial were admitted only after a trial-within-a-trial of about five months ([Sunday World, extract](https://sundayworld.co.za/news/confessions-in-meyiwa-murder-case-admissible-court-rules/)), a Deokaran confession naming a senior politician was retracted and he denied involvement, and accused in two current trials allege assault after arrest. Evidence fails in a characteristic order: the scene first (in Meyiwa the forensic officer arrived almost four hours late, took no swabs from the door handle and tested nobody for gunshot residue; in Rhadebe the hijacked car was returned to its owners within two days unexamined), then the laboratory, then the witnesses.

### What protects the person who ordered it

Five protections recur. The first is the **cut-out who dies**: the one person who met both sides. In Deokaran the alleged gunman and middleman was released by a detective and murdered four months after the killing, and the journalist Jeff Wicks concludes "evidence has been destroyed" ([Daily Maverick, 31 Aug 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-08-31-babita-deokaran-murder-probe-reveals-police-failure-fading-hope-for-justice/)). In the van der Merwe case the alleged link man died by suicide after an attempt on his own life, and the accused then blamed him; the magistrate called it "a convenient shift of blame on a dead person". The second is the **bargain struck at the wrong level**: in Kebble the men who fired and the man who hired them were indemnified, and the only accused walked because the shooter could not link him to the instruction. In Deokaran the judge accepted a plea deal that did not identify the orderer. The third is **friends inside**: detectives reportedly removed just before arrests, a docket closed as an inquest, a watchdog report classified top secret for two years, investigators photographed, followed and nearly run off the road until three of five withdrew, and alleged talk of "three envelopes" for a magistrate, a prosecutor and the investigators ([Daily Maverick, 20 Oct 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-10-20-envelopes-and-missing-evidence-madlanga-commission-hears-allegations-of-murder-cover-up/)). All of that is allegation or reporting, not finding. The fourth is **payment that leaves no trail**: cash, a tender, public money. The fifth is **time**: a trial date three years and three months after the killing, with a judge warning against "another Senzo Meyiwa" ([TimesLIVE, extract](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-10-26-judge-warns-against-another-senzo-meyiwa-delay-in-katiso-molefe-case/)). The acting Police Minister said it in one sentence in August 2026: "Part of the reason we don't have justice today is that those people are in positions of power and able to protect themselves" ([The Citizen, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/babita-deokarans-ghost-still-haunts-corrupt-kathrada-foundation-demands-mastermind-be-jailed/)).

### Witnesses and investigators are the next targets

Witnesses are killed at predictable moments: the week after testimony (van der Merwe, shot about three weeks after his commission evidence, with surveillance allegedly starting once he had testified), the days after implicated people get bail (Hanekom), the days before a hearing (Mahlangu), and the period after a report is handed over (Mafole). A second investigator from Machaba's Mpumalanga unit was shot at home near Mbombela, survived, and was shot dead after leaving hospital ([Lowvelder, 28 May 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2026/05/28/mpumalanga-government-official-arrested-on-murder-charges/)). GI-TOC recorded eight witnesses killed in the first nine months of 2026. Protection is thin and reactive: Deokaran had none after a year on special leave; Kinnear's guard was posted after a grenade attempt and withdrawn nine months before he was shot; van der Merwe declined protection because he ran his own security company. "Declined" should be read with care, since protection means leaving home, business and income. Investigators are intimidated, removed or, like Kinnear, killed; two prosecutors were shot dead in the Eastern Cape in 2025 ([Daily Maverick, 7 Sep 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-09-07-rising-death-toll-of-sa-corruption-busters-like-bouwer-van-niekerk-highlights-cost-of-accountability/)). Families do the pushing: Deokaran's sisters post a daily count of days without justice, and one victim's mother reached the investigating officer only through the estate's executor.

### The victim's records explain why, and almost never who paid

The victim's paper trail survives better than the docket. Deokaran's WhatsApp messages to her supervisor ("Our lives could be in danger") and the payments she had flagged became the basis of findings of more than R2 billion in losses and, in July 2026, a court-confirmed forfeiture of R326 million in assets from a businessman's syndicate. That was civil forfeiture, not a murder charge ([Daily Maverick, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-07-20-babitas-revenge-state-seizes-r326m-empire-of-tembisa-hospital-tender-kingpin/)). Mafole's audit report outlived him as a document the opposition was still demanding be tabled months later. Mahlangu had recorded a call offering him promotion to implicate colleagues. Killers know the value of records: Machaba's attackers took his work laptop and investigation documents, and in the Swart matter the honest supplier had been deleted from the procurement system. Official records are meanwhile lost or locked: case documents "damaged in a flood", a ballistic report withdrawn.

Two cautions follow. No case was found in which a victim's records were relied on to convict an orderer; prosecutors have preferred fraud charges and forfeiture, which documents can prove. And the records cut both ways: Kebble's post-mortem audits exposed his own share dealings, and van der Merwe was reportedly about to be charged himself ([IOL, extract](https://iol.co.za/news/2026-02-12-slain-witness-d-faced-charges-in-mbense-murder-case/)).

## In the mystery cases the killer was inside the house and the intruder was invented

### Who it turns out to be

About thirty prominent cases from 2004 to 2026 were reviewed. Where a court made a finding, the hidden killer was a husband or male partner (Jayde Panayiotou's husband, Gill Packham's, Susan Rohde's, Tshegofatso Pule's boyfriend), a wife or widow (the wife of acting judge Patrick Maqubela; a Boschkop widow), a child of the family (Henri van Breda; the Griekwastad teenager; a constable's 15-year-old daughter at Mmakau), wider kin (the police sergeant Rosemary Ndlovu, who killed a partner and five relatives for insurance), or neighbours and fellow congregants (the Krugersdorp group). Paid strangers appear only as instruments. This matches the national data: partners in 60% of femicides, known offenders in 70% of murders where a relationship could be established.

Respectability is the usual cover: a police sergeant, a schoolteacher in a prayer group, a businessman who said the couple had been planning to renew their vows. Secunda has its own example in the mining-towns report: a man who in April 2026 stabbed his wife of two months, cleaned the townhouse with pine gel, hid the body in a sports bag in the outside room and fled to Mozambique; he took a plea agreement for 40 years ([Ridge Times, 6 Aug 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-crime-news/2026/08/06/high-court-sends-secunda-wife-killer-to-jail-for-40-years/)).

### Five cover stories and what broke them

The cover stories are hijacking, an intruder or farm attack, suicide, "she left and never arrived", and theft by the victim. They work because they name what a town already fears. Henri van Breda described "an axe-wielding black man, wearing dark clothes, gloves and a balaclava-type mask" ([Wikipedia, Van Breda murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Breda_murders)); the Griekwastad boy blamed a farm attack.

What broke them was ordinary. In Van Breda: a guarded estate with no breach, weapons from the house, superficial wounds, and an emergency call made almost three hours after he phoned his girlfriend. In Griekwastad the judge said "The dogs would have barked" ([M&G, extract](https://mg.co.za/article/2014-03-27-teen-found-guilty-on-all-griekwastad-charges/)). In Packham: a work phone switched off for two hours and eight minutes, tower data for a second phone, a neighbour's camera, blood in the garage, and a colleague asked to lie ([News24, extract](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/rob-packhams-phone-was-turned-off-the-morning-his-wife-disappeared-expert-20190325)). In Maqubela, where decomposition hid the cause of death: a query about a R20 million life policy weeks earlier, a forged will, a flight booked before the time she claimed to have been stood up, and base-station records showing the dead man's phone travelling with hers ([Daily Maverick, 8 Nov 2013](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2013-11-08-the-black-widow-how-thandi-maqubela-spun-her-web-of-lies)). At Boschkop a widow said three men posing as clients robbed and killed her husband; she was unhurt, the blood pattern did not fit, and she pleaded guilty ([Rekord, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/rekord/news-headlines/local-news/2025/10/16/widow-sentenced-after-staging-robbery-to-cover-husbands-murder/)). At a Piet Retief farm, guards testified they were told to put two sheep in the victims' vehicle to stage a stock theft ([Middelburg Observer, 12 Dec 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/middelburg-observer/news/2025/12/12/eight-accused-convicted-of-murdering-two-men-in-piet-retief/)).

The recurring tells, in rough order of frequency, are a time gap the suspect cannot explain, the survivor's light or absent injuries, no sign of entry at a place that is fenced, gated or dog-guarded, paperwork done too early, and the weakest accomplice talking. Where the spouse hired someone, the break came from the hired end: Panayiotou's middleman was turned and put in a bugged car ([News24, extract](https://www.news24.com/SouthAfrica/News/panayiotou-video-recording-is-admissible-20170612)); Pule's killer pleaded and testified against her boyfriend ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Tshegofatso_Pule)); Ndlovu's would-be hitman went to the police, and a colleague had already noticed a "bundle" of insurance policies ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosemary_Ndlovu)).

### The cases that failed, failed on handling

The acquittals and open cases mostly failed on forensic handling or witness reliability, not for lack of suspects. In the Inge Lotz case a police expert misrepresented an overseas expert's opinion about a shoe mark, a fingerprint lift was disputed, and the boyfriend was acquitted in 2007; the case is unsolved ([Wikipedia, Minister van Polisie v Van der Vyver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_van_Polisie_v_Van_der_Vyver)). At Coligny two farm managers were convicted on one eyewitness and acquitted on appeal because investigators had collected no blood from the vehicle or scenes and found no cartridge cases ([Wikipedia, Coligny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coligny,_South_Africa)). In the Brendin Horner case farmers stormed a court to reach two accused whose DNA, thirteen months later, did not match; both were acquitted ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Brendin_Horner)). Shrien Dewani was discharged when the hired men's evidence collapsed in contradiction ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Anni_Dewani)). Claims that statements were beaten out of suspects attach to several convictions and are tested in trials-within-trials.

Who is suspected first follows the town's existing lines. In the racially charged rural cases each side read the same body as proof of its own story, and lobby groups, parties and neighbourhood watches arrived before any forensic report. A documentary maker's remark on the Lotz case states the class point: asked what would have happened had the accused been a black man, "across the board everyone says he would have ended up in jail" ([Daily Maverick, 12 Jun 2014](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2014-06-12-inge-lotz-murder-nine-years-later-two-amateur-forensic-sleuths-once-again-point-fingers-at-fred-van-der-vyver)).

### Private money shapes outcomes on both sides

Private actors appear throughout, and not as heroes. Families who can pay bring in pathologists and fingerprint experts, and the trial becomes experts contradicting each other over one exhibit (Lotz; Rohde). A private detective obtained the confession in the 2018 Stella school-hostel murders, staged as suicides; at trial the accused said he had given the detective "the answers he was looking for", and was convicted anyway ([The Citizen, 17 Feb 2020](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/courts/stella-hostel-murders-xander-bylsma-found-guilty-of-strangling-ex-and-her-friend)). A wife hired private investigators to document her husband's affairs (Maqubela). A privately hired pathologist and former police generals found ballistic evidence in the Rhadebe case and were then disparaged in an internal police note. A lobby group's private prosecution unit funded the Coligny appeal and has been approached by police families. In the 1999 Betty Ketani case a typed confession was found under a carpet thirteen years later; its author was himself a private detective ([Engineering News, extract](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/cold-case-confession-2016-06-15)). Journalists did what police did not in Deokaran, the Murrays, Rhadebe and Ketani. Good police work also appears: a six-week turnaround on the Leigh Matthews kidnapping, and the undercover stings on Ndlovu and Panayiotou.

### Months if someone confesses, years if nobody does

From a hand-built sample, not systematic data: confessions and pleas finish in three to twelve months; a contested circumstantial case takes two to four years to verdict and up to six with appeals; cases built on a missing witness or weak forensics drift or end in acquittal; and cold cases break after five to seventeen years through a confession or a surrender, not a laboratory. Several "solved" cases keep an open core: accomplices never charged (Matthews), a motive never known (Ketani), a child never found and a buyer never identified (Joshlin Smith). These are the famous cases and over-represent convictions; the national figures say the typical ending is no arrest.

## The genre is crowded in Cape Town and empty on the Highveld

### What is worn

The post-1994 crime-fiction boom is dominated by Cape-based authors writing police or ex-police protagonists: Deon Meyer's Benny Griessel, Margie Orford's profiler Clare Hart, Mike Nicol, Roger Smith ([Wikipedia, Deon Meyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deon_Meyer); [Daily Maverick, 2 Mar 2012](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2012-03-02-murder-she-wrote-margie-orford-sas-crime-fiction-queen/)). Critics and practitioners identify the following as worn. The first is the **damaged alcoholic white detective**: Griessel, the suicidal Mat Joubert of the series *Cape Town*, the "broke, barely functioning alcoholic" of the film *Zulu*; Meyer has reportedly called Griessel "a bit of a cliché" (a search extract with an uncertain link). The second is **corruption as total explanation**: Leon de Kock writes that in Nicol and Smith every crime traces to ministerial corruption, "the tentacles spreading downwards like so many evil fingers", and objects that "it can't be quite as bad as Smith makes out" ([Daily Maverick / SLiP, 23 Jan 2012](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2012-01-23-sa-crime-thrillers-and-the-genre-snob-debate/)). The third is the **dead or abused woman or child as plot engine**, criticised from inside by Orford, who dropped her series because the profiler "was reproducing the male gaze that I was trying to interrogate". The fourth is "**cops versus evil**": Christopher McMichael, writing on the series *Donkerbos*, questions "whether South Africa needs more cops vs evil stories" and wants networks of power inside the state examined instead ([Africa Is a Country, extract](https://africasacountry.com/2023/01/the-limits-of-the-cop-drama)). The fifth and sixth are the **farm murder read as racial attack** and **occult or muti explanations**, which a Wits commentary says "often mask anxieties about race, power and precarity" ([EWN, extract](https://www.ewn.co.za/2025/09/15/satanic-panic-5-occult-crimes-that-gripped-south-africa-s-imagination)). On screen the export model is itself the cliché: an international co-production with an imported or internationally known lead, a traumatised profiler and a serial killer (*Reyka*, *Catch Me a Killer*, *Cape Town*, *Zulu*).

The sharpest objection goes to the premise. Orford: "I couldn't continue with the illusion that the police would help restore social balance. After the Marikana massacre, I thought that I can't go on pretending" ([Daily Maverick, 23 Nov 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-11-23-margie-orford-focuses-our-gaze-on-secrets-obsession-and-intergenerational-trauma-and-produces-an-exquisite-work-of-art/)). A detective who restores order in a mining town carries that specific credibility problem. True crime has its own default shape, "one killing cracks open a network", set by Mandy Wiener's *Killing Kebble*, and its own criticisms: grief turned into entertainment, nothing new, and uneven attention. Nechama Brodie notes that farm killings, under half a percent of homicides, "dominate the national narrative on violence" ([Daily Maverick, 25 Oct 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-10-25-nechama-brodie-challenges-myths-of-the-white-genocide-in-her-book-farm-killings-in-south-africa/)).

### What is praised, and what nobody has done

Praise goes to point of view from inside a community, to protagonists without institutional power, and to political economy over monsters. De Kock sets Sifiso Mzobe's *Young Blood* against the corruption thrillers because Mzobe "does not make large assumptions about state corruption"; what happens to his characters is a "crystallisation of township conditions". The series *Unseen*, about a house cleaner, was praised for depicting "people who are not valued in the community" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unseen_(TV_series))). The detective Ben Booysen's memoir of the Krugersdorp murders describes the killers "as criminals rather than through the supernatural aspects they believed in", records police botches, and ends with his retirement: "I had no retirement party or farewell function" ([Daily Maverick, 15 Jun 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-06-15-the-devilsdorp-files-an-unflinching-true-crime-account-of-the-appointment-murders/)). The same review supplies the only Highveld coal texture found in the genre: the smell of coal, black men in hostels, and "the rock drillers who were the poorest whites", with labour type a dividing line alongside race. No critic found says corruption should be left out; the objection is to corruption that removes local cause and individual choice.

The private investigator has a local lineage older than the boom. Scholarship places the first South African hard-boiled detectives with Black *Drum* writers of the 1950s; Arthur Maimane's narrator was a Black private detective in Johannesburg's underworld, a subversive figure under apartheid ([Il Tolomeo, extract](https://doi.org/10.30687/tol/2499-5975/2021/23/018)). James McClure's Kramer and Zondi novels show the sharper use of the cross-racial partnership: the Zulu sergeant does much of the detecting through language and domestic networks while the Afrikaner lieutenant holds the rank ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kramer_and_Zondi)). On the page, the working method for multilingual speech is English narration that keeps specific untranslated words; the films critics single out are shot largely in African languages or Afrikaans with subtitles.

On setting, **six searches found no crime novel, series or film set in Secunda, Witbank, Middelburg or Ermelo**. The nearest are a little-known coalfields fraud thriller by a retired mining lawyer, *Steal a Few Cents* ([NetGalley, extract](https://www.netgalley.co.uk/catalog/book/113041)); two thrillers set in isolated mining towns elsewhere; a Pretoria diamond-mine telenovela; and a Netflix family drama called *Magenta Coal* whose setting was not confirmed. Mining communities appear mostly in Marikana documentaries. The existing mining-town thrillers lean on "a town owned by one powerful figure", which is the nearest thing to a worn convention here. No South African work was found that adapts the *Red Harvest* structure of an outsider playing a company town's factions against each other, and no released detective game is set in a recognisable South African place. Absence from a handful of searches is not proof; Afrikaans-language publishing is the likeliest place for a missed example.

Prices are single cases; there is no court-verified taxi-hit tariff and nothing on the price of a corporate hit. The R2 million Deokaran figure is unverified. ## What this means for the Secunda story

Nothing in this section describes a real Secunda event. Each paragraph lays an invention over a documented pattern, and the story should use invented people, firms and offices wherever it attributes wrongdoing.

### The first killing: personal, read by the town as a hit, logged by police as a robbery

All three readings are supported, and the gap between them is the realistic part. Police label by what is missing: if a phone, a firearm or a vehicle is gone, the docket opens as murder and robbery, as it did for the Komatipoort councillor and the eMbalenhle councillor whose pistol was taken. The town labels by who the victim was: the public reading of the Murrays, Deokaran and Magaqa fixed at once on each victim's biggest known enemy. So a victim with a visible connection to the coal fraud will be called a hit in the taverns and on Facebook while the station calls it a house robbery, and both will be wrong in the way the record says people are usually wrong. The corrected motive should be smaller and closer.

The truth should then look like the mystery cases, not like the hit cases. A hit leaves a crew's traces: a stolen car with bad plates dumped out of town, a reconnaissance vehicle on someone's camera the day before, many rounds from a gun with a history, earlier threats, strangers. A personal killing leaves a household's traces: one weapon, possibly the family's own licensed gun or a knife; no approach vehicle; a gate, alarm or dogs that did not react; a survivor with light injuries; a phone switched off for two hours; an emergency call made late; a policy queried or a will altered beforehand. The story can let the personal killer borrow the look of a hit (the victim shot at the gate on arriving home, the classic location in 39% of hits) and then let the absence of a crew undo it. The killer should be someone respectable and near. If a helper was paid, the precedents are R5,000 to R60,000, a helper who is a relative or casual worker, and exposure from the helper's end.

Because the first reading suits everyone, the story should show who benefits from keeping it: a family spared scrutiny, a station spared an unsolvable hit, and the fraud's real participants, who are happy to be suspected of a killing they did not commit as long as no one looks at the books. The correction should not come from the laboratory.

### The second killing: a contract hit dressed as a robbery

The realistic disguise is a hijacking, a house break-in or a robbery at the gate, with things actually taken: the Komatipoort attackers took phones and a bakkie and assaulted the wife. The files warn that "a robbery cover is hard to sustain when the victim is shot many times with a rifle and keeps his wallet". The cover should therefore be competent, and the tells should be the documented ones. What was taken is wrong for a robbery: the Machaba attackers took a work laptop and investigation documents. The gun has been used before, in another province. The car was stolen months earlier elsewhere. The victim had been watched, had reported a threat or had survived an earlier incident that was shrugged off. And the timing fits the danger windows: just after a report was handed over, just before a hearing, just after someone got bail. A wrong-victim killing, as in the Armand Swart case, is documented and usable.

The crew should be small, hired through an intermediary, drawn from the taxi world or from men with police or security backgrounds, and from outside. Mkhondo, in the same district as Secunda, borders KwaZulu-Natal, and a suspect in its 2023 councillor killing came from near Durban. They do not know the victim or the client. The fee, on the court-reported range, is tens of thousands of rand split several ways, perhaps with a promised contract on top. The contrast with the first killing writes itself and is accurate: the first has too little organisation to be a hit, the second too much to be a robbery.

### The police ally: honest, overloaded and removable

A detective with 150 to 200 dockets, a share of a working vehicle, a DNA queue in which 1.5% of profiles arrive on time and a ballistics report that takes a year is the norm, so the ally's weakness needs no vice to explain it. The interesting pressure is the one the mining-towns report identified and these files confirm: from outside, overload and interference look the same. The ally's realistic risks are three: the suspects, colleagues who leak, and a superior who can reassign the docket, close it as an inquest or take the team off it on the eve of an arrest. Her realistic powers are also documented: sending exhibits to a second laboratory when the first report omits something; holding a suspect on an unrelated case so as not to alert the rest; getting the docket to an outside unit, which in Mpumalanga in 2026 could plausibly be a visiting PKTT detachment or the Secunda-based Murder and Robbery Unit; and protecting a chain of evidence. She should not be the one who delivers justice at the end. Orford's objection after Marikana applies, and Booysen's unceremonious retirement is the truer note.

### The private investigator: a file-builder with a citizen's powers

Real private investigators in these files do four things: obtain a confession the police did not (and have it attacked in court as coached), fund or find forensic evidence the police missed (and get smeared for it), document a spouse's movements, and build a file that is handed to police or to a private prosecution unit. They have no power to arrest beyond a citizen's, no lawful access to phone or bank records without consent or subpoena, and must be registered with the security regulator, as the earlier reports set out. The story's investigator should therefore work the evidence that actually solved these cases and that a private person can lawfully reach: security-company control-room logs, private cameras, vehicle-tracker reports held by owners and insurers, policy documents, the victim's duplicates (a message to a supervisor, a draft sent to a private address, a report already lodged with a committee), and people. Language is a working tool in the McClure sense: who will talk to whom, in which language, decides what can be learned. The question of who pays the investigator is worth settling early, since each documented client brings a bias: a family, an insurer, a political party, a lobby group.

Making the protagonist a private investigator already avoids the most worn figure in the genre, provided the character is not an ex-policeman with a bottle. The *Drum* tradition offers a better ancestor: improvised, unofficial authority in a place where the official kind is distrusted.

### The ending: the payer known, not charged

This is the documented outcome and can be written without cynicism if the mechanism is specific. The honest version has four parts. The crew, or part of it, is caught through the gun or the car and pleads; the plea agreement brings 15 to 22 years and does not name the client, and a judge says so. The one person who met both sides is dead, missing or has recanted, so what the investigator knows cannot be led as evidence. The victim's records prove the fraud and the motive, and what follows is what followed in life: a fraud charge, an asset-forfeiture order, a dismissal, a commission summons. Deokaran's case produced a R326 million forfeiture and no murder charge; Kebble's produced a corruption conviction of a police commissioner and nobody convicted of the killing. And time does the rest: a High Court date at Delmas or Middelburg two or three years out, postponed.

Three choices would keep the ending from repeating the genre's faults. The payer should be local and mid-sized, a person with something specific to lose from an audit, a sale or a report, and not a minister at the top of a pyramid; de Kock's objection is to exactly that pyramid, and the corrected motives in the real cases were always the smaller ones. The payer's protection should be ordinary and visible: a relative as intermediary, a friend in the station, cash, patience. And the cost of the knowledge should fall where it falls in the files: on the witness who must decide whether to accept protection that means losing her house and income, on the detective whose docket is taken away, and on the family, who are left counting days.

### What to ration or leave out

The research argues against a serial killer, a muti angle for colour, a murdered woman's body as spectacle, the hostel or informal settlement as scenery, a foreign crime boss, a single all-explaining corrupt official, and a quick forensic breakthrough. It argues for showing which deaths get a detective and which do not. In the same month as the story's two killings, the statistics say the district will see a tavern stabbing, a partner killing and a suspected thief beaten to death by a crowd, each of which will get less attention than either plotted death. The story is set in an election year in which political killings hit a record and Mpumalanga's murder count ticked up against the national trend, which gives the town's suspicion of a "hit" a real basis even when it is wrong.

## Conclusion

The two killings the story needs are mirror images in the real record, and the mirror is the useful discovery. South African practice sorts a shooting by what was taken, the public sorts it by whom the victim had angered, and neither test looks at the things that actually distinguish a household killing from a crew's work: the number of people involved, the history of the gun, the car, the time gaps, the paperwork. A plot built on those distinctions will be both fair to the reader and true to how cases here are in fact corrected, which is slowly, by records and by whoever in the chain is most frightened or worst paid.

The ending is where the research changes the brief most. "Known but not charged" is not a failure state to be softened; it is the place where the real cases have their drama, in the plea bargain that stops one rung short, the forfeiture order that stands in for a murder charge, the witness weighing protection against her livelihood, and the detective who did the work and lost the docket. No one has yet set any of this among the cooling towers, conveyors and numbered extensions of the Highveld coal towns, so the story has no local model to follow and none to imitate.
