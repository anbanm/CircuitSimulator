# Coal fraud thrives where murder dockets stall

Crime around Secunda is two different things a short drive apart: Secunda town records mostly theft and paper fraud, while eMbalenhle, by the police's own account the worst precinct in the Gert Sibande District, records roughly a murder a week, heavy assault and sexual violence, at a station whose commander said in 2025 that crime was "out of control". The murders that reach court here are overwhelmingly domestic killings, house-robbery murders and tavern shootings, solved by confession, flight or surrender and not by forensics; the one clear targeted killing, of an eMbalenhle ward councillor and a committee member in December 2023, had no reported arrests. The organised crime a story wants (coal swapping, weighbridge and laboratory fraud, hauliers' blockades, "business forum" extortion, hired killing of contractors and officials) is thoroughly documented in the Mpumalanga coal belt, but one district north and west, around Emalahleni, Middelburg, Ermelo, Carolina and the Eskom stations. None of it is documented at Sasol's Secunda plant or mines. Nationally, a murder docket has about a one-in-nine chance of producing an arrest, DNA and ballistics queues run to hundreds of thousands of exhibits and tens of thousands of cases, and in contract killings the gunmen are sometimes convicted while the person who paid almost never is. Since July 2025 the Madlanga Commission has added sworn but untested evidence that senior police were captured by organised crime, so the realistic question for any investigator in 2026 is which unit can safely receive evidence. About half of this report rests on search-result extracts only, and most of the organised-crime record consists of arrests and allegations with no known verdict; both limits are marked throughout and gathered in a section of their own.

## How to read this report: two grades of source and three grades of claim

Five research files underlie this report. Two of them, on the Secunda area and on mining towns outside the coal belt, rest entirely on **search-result extracts**: a headline, a URL and a short summary, with no page read end to end. The other three (the coal belt, illegal mining and investigation practice) add 61 pages that were **read in full**. In this report a citation followed by "full read" means the page was read in full and says what is stated. **Every citation without that tag is extract-only**, and its dates, figures and wording were not checked against the article or a court record. News24 pages could not be opened at all, and no court judgment was read for any case.

Claims also come in three grades. A **conviction** or **sentence** is stated as such. An **arrest** or **charge** means only that; where no outcome is given, none was found. An **allegation** covers claims by a party, a witness at a commission, an intelligence report or an anonymous source, and is labelled in the sentence. Private individuals who were only accused are described by role. Victims, convicted persons and office-holders are named where the sources name them. Background on the town itself (its layout, the plant, the mines, the municipality, the courts and stations) is in the earlier Secunda setting report and is only summarised here where a crime finding depends on it: Secunda town and eMbalenhle each have a police station, Evander has a station and a magistrate's court, Secunda has its own magistrate's court, and serious trials go to the Mpumalanga High Court sitting at Middelburg or on circuit at Delmas.

## Secunda is a theft town beside a murder township

Everything in this section is extract-only unless tagged.

### eMbalenhle leads the district; Secunda's volume is property crime

At a briefing on 29 November 2025 police said eMbalenhle leads crime across the Gert Sibande District and is among Mpumalanga's highest-crime stations, naming **murder, house robbery and gender-based violence** as its main problems ([Ridge Times, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/12/09/embalenhle-struggles-with-highest-crime-rate-in-district/)). Official station-by-category tables could not be retrieved, so the numbers come from third-party sites that repackage police data, and they disagree. One gives eMbalenhle **2,414 reported crimes** for April 2025 to March 2026, with assault with intent to do grievous bodily harm the largest category at 533 cases ([SafeSuburb](https://safesuburb.co.za/mpumalanga/gert-sibande/embalenhle/embalenhle/)). Another, on a calendar-2025 basis, gives 1,805 crimes including **47 murders** and 40 residential robberies ([SafetyBrief](https://www.safetybrief.co.za/province/mpumalanga/gert-sibande/embalenhle)). Treat 47 as indicative: it is about one murder a week in one precinct. Secunda's precinct recorded a similar total, **2,107**, but only 223 violent crimes against 639 property crimes, and its largest single category was general theft at 530 cases ([SafeSuburb](https://safesuburb.co.za/mpumalanga/gert-sibande/secunda/secunda/)). The police publish counts, not per-resident rates, so there is no official "crime rate" for either place.

Other markers point the same way. In January to March 2025 eMbalenhle was one of the six Mpumalanga stations with the most sexual-offence cases ([Lowvelder, 30 May 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2025/05/30/crime-statistics-mpumalanga-registers-a-sharp-increase-in-rape-incidents/)). In a single week in August 2026 the station opened 79 cases including murder, attempted murder and rape, and police singled out rapes of minors as the main worry ([Ridge Times, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-crime-news/2026/08/19/79-cases-reported-at-embalenhle-police-station-in-one-week-2/)). In September 2023 the provincial safety MEC said the Govan Mbeki stations were among the province's worst, cited gangsterism and the kidnapping and rape of children in eMbalenhle, blamed taverns, and said some officers were "in cahoots" with drug dealers, which is an allegation by a politician and not a finding ([Ridge Times, 12 Sept 2023](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2023/09/12/mec-tackles-police-at-evander-and-embalenhle/)). No source named local gangs, drug types or seizure cases. The working picture is weekend tavern violence and domestic violence, not organised gang war.

Three things stand out. First, **no extract describes a forensic breakthrough**. Cases closed because the accused confessed, pleaded, fled and was caught across a border, was arrested in transit or walked into a police station; ballistics appears only as a pending step. Second, delay is ordinary: a January 2022 killing in neighbouring Standerton drew a life sentence in the Secunda Regional Court in April 2026 ([Ridge Times, 4 Apr 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-news/2026/04/04/life-sentence-brings-justice-for-ellen-sikhosana/)), and the 2011 case took ten years. Third, the councillor case is the only one with the marks of a hit, and it went nowhere in public. Five men had fired more than 20 shots at his house and car in 2022, and the council agreed in September 2022 that he needed protection; the speaker later said she could not tell whether the motive was political or personal ([The Citizen / LNN](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/mpumalanga-councillor-ward-committee-member-killed-in-embalenhle/), full read). No source links the killing to tenders. No contract killing with a proven paymaster, no taxi-conflict killing and no tender-linked killing was found for Secunda, eMbalenhle or Evander in the period.

Robbery is real in the town. Armed robbers hit a Secunda shop in December 2021 and three were caught in Evander ([Ridge Times, 9 Dec 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2021/12/09/armed-robbers-hit-bala-bala-in-secunda-three-caught-at-evander/)); a suspect nicknamed the "panga man", linked to house robberies, got bail in April 2025 ([Ridge Times, 29 Apr 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/04/29/pangaman-out-on-bail/)); and in April 2026 a driver chased on the R29 between Leandra and Kinross fought off hijackers, one of whom was shot and found dead ([Ridge Times, 25 Apr 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2026/04/25/three-appear-in-court-for-attempted-murder-and-hijacking-2/)).

### eMbalenhle station: 150 dockets a detective and nine members arrested in a year

The eMbalenhle station is in documented crisis. An opposition oversight visit in October 2024 found **9 of 20 vehicles working and detectives averaging about 150 cases each** ([DA Mpumalanga, Oct 2024](https://mpumalanga.da.org.za/2024/10/lack-of-resources-at-embalenhle-police-station-negatively-affects-work-of-saps-members)); a later visit found no holding cells and too few detectives ([Mpumalanga News, 18 Apr 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/mpumalanga-news/news-headlines/local-news/2025/04/18/embalenhle-police-station-lacks-holding-cells-and-detectives/)). On 12 February 2025 the station commander told residents crime was out of control, and residents said officers tell complainants to trace suspects themselves ([Ridge Times, 23 Feb 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/02/23/embalenhle-police-commander-says-crime-is-out-of-control-2/)). At a legislature hearing in March 2026 residents raised unresolved dockets, missing case files and claims that officers work with criminals ([Ridge Times, 1 Apr 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2026/04/01/community-raises-safety-concerns-at-embalenhle-public-hearing-2/)). Those are residents' allegations.

The arrests of police are fact, though none has a verdict. **Nine members of the station were arrested during 2025** ([Ridge Times, 2 Dec 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/12/02/five-arrested-embalenhle-cops-make-bail/)). Three officers were charged in August 2025 with murder and defeating the ends of justice over the death of a motorist they had chased ([Ridge Times, 25 Aug 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-crime-news/2025/08/25/police-arrest-three-of-their-own-in-connection-with-the-murder-of-embalenhle-motorist/)). After a man died on 2 October 2025, allegedly assaulted and tortured during a drug search of his home, the police watchdog IPID arrested crime-prevention members on charges including murder, kidnapping and assault; the Evander court granted five officers **R2,000 bail each**, and two more were arrested in February 2026 ([IPID, 21 Nov 2025](https://www.ipid.gov.za/sites/default/files/2025-11/MP%20media%20statement%2021%20Nov%202025%20on%20Embalenhle%20arrest.pdf); [Ridge Times, 7 Feb 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2026/02/07/ipid-arrests-two-more-embalenhle-police-officers-in-mbatha-murder-case-3/)). In September 2026 a Flying Squad sergeant was arrested in Secunda, reportedly on duty and in uniform, and linked to a diesel-tanker hijacking in Bethal that May ([EWN, 4 Sept 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/09/04/cop-arrested-in-mpumalanga-for-alleged-links-to-diesel-truck-hijacking-syndicate)). Mob killings recur and residents tie them to police failure; after an 18-year-old was killed in Extension 15 in May 2022, two opposing groups picketed the station ([Ridge Times, 31 May 2022](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2022/05/31/mob-justice-murder-divided-embalenhle-community/)).

The local institutional map can be read off these cases: overloaded station detectives; a **Secunda Murder and Robbery Unit**, which arrested a constable in October 2025 ([Ridge Times, 7 Oct 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2025/10/07/saps-constable-gets-bail/)); a Secunda office of the Hawks, the police's priority-crime directorate, which handles commercial and syndicate cases; and IPID for deaths at police hands. Nothing was found on resourcing at the Secunda or Evander stations themselves.

### Plant crime is supplier fraud found by audit, plus gate-level collusion

The documented crime around the Sasol complex is quiet. In November 2021 the Hawks arrested a truck driver and a gate security guard at a company inside the complex over product worth about R100,000; the two had been in contact by cellphone and the guard was charged with conspiracy. Sasol later said the product was petrol, not chemicals, which police called "bizarre and curious" ([IOL, 15 Nov 2021](https://iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2021-11-15-security-guard-and-driver-in-court-for-allegedly-stealing-chemicals-from-sasol/)). In 2022 the directors of an equipment supplier were arrested after a Sasol internal audit found cheaper actuators supplied than those billed; they appeared in the Evander court and had reportedly repaid money ([IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/directors-arrested-for-allegedly-defrauding-sasol-by-supplying-cheap-goods-disguised-as-premium-cace4c83-7005-448b-bffc-a3207a0965ec)). Larger cases followed the same pattern of arrest without a reported outcome: four suspects accused of defrauding Sasol of R279 million with invalid guarantees ([News24, 23 Jul 2020](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/suspects-arrested-for-allegedly-defrauding-sasol-to-the-tune-of-r279-million-20200723)), and six people from an engineering firm arrested over empowerment "fronting" and tender fraud at Eskom and Sasol ([IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/saturday-star/news/six-suspects-arrested-in-r400-million-eskom-and-sasol-tender-fraud-scheme-8217f51b-a4d2-4cf9-92ed-3460e70ccf13)).

The municipality has its own case. A service provider invoiced Govan Mbeki Municipality in December 2022 for goods allegedly never delivered and was paid four days later without the prescribed process; the accused are the provider, an expelled official and a 65-year-old pensioner who used to work there, arrested by the Hawks' **Secunda Serious Commercial Crime Investigation unit** and appearing at Evander. The Hawks put the amount at R1.5 million and the municipality at R2.5 million ([Ridge Times](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/?p=407398), full read). The same Secunda Hawks unit made arrests in a roughly R3 million copper-cable case at Eskom's Tutuka station near Standerton, including an Eskom employee and a security supervisor ([IOL, 6 Mar 2025](https://iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2025-03-06-mpumalanga-security-supervisor-arrested-in-eskom-copper-cable-theft-scandal/)).

**No sabotage prosecution, coal or diesel syndicate case, or business-forum invasion at Sasol Secunda or its mines was found.** Sasol handles much internally, so absence from search extracts is weak evidence of absence, but the record gives a writer nothing local to cite. What the area does show is anger over who controls access to plant work: a protest in eMbalenhle against Sasol and labour brokers turned violent, with outside workers targeted and buses attacked ([The Citizen](https://citizen.co.za/news/news-cns/1811751/sasol-apparently-not-formally-informed-of-todays-mpumalanga-protest/)). The local scams are small advance-fee job frauds: eMbalenhle police warned in 2020 that "jobs are not for sale", and Secunda police in 2021 described a fake job advert that moved applicants to WhatsApp and demanded R2,000 for a "medical" ([Ridge Times, 26 May 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2021/05/26/secunda-police-warn-job-seekers-to-be-careful/)). Insider job-selling is a community belief with no prosecuted case behind it.

### Private response makes the first arrest in the suburbs

In July 2021 Secunda community-policing volunteers and a private tactical team chased a robbery vehicle through Evander and made the arrests ([Ridge Times, 23 Jul 2021](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/2021/07/23/secunda-cpf-arrests-robbers/)), and in Standerton police and a security company jointly arrested a suspected hijacker ([Ridge Times, 23 Jan 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/news-headlines/local-news/2024/01/23/police-and-kss-combine-forces-to-arrest-suspected-hijacker/)). The documented abuses are the security officer convicted of murder and the gate guard in the 2021 theft case. No list of local firms, complaints data or neighbourhood-watch arrest record was found.

## Illegal mining is a gang-run economy that reaches Evander only at the surface

### A pyramid with destitute diggers at the bottom and licensed dealers at the top

Researchers describe the illicit gold trade as a pyramid. About **30,000 illegal miners** (zama zamas) work in and around disused and active mines, organised by syndicates ([ISS Today, 19 Jun 2019](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/solving-south-africas-violent-and-costly-zama-zama-problem), full read). Above them are armed gangs, largely from Lesotho, that run shafts as extortion rackets; above those are buyers, and at the top are scrap-metal and second-hand gold dealers, jewellers, small refineries and exporters who launder the gold into legal supply ([GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/), full read; [Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa), full read). Because second-hand gold is tax-exempt, newly mined gold is melted to jewellery purity and passed off as recycled; two companies allegedly claimed R24.4 billion in fraudulent tax refunds between 2012 and 2020 (same Daily Maverick article, full read). Estimates of annual value run from R7 billion to more than R20 billion and measure different things, so they do not form a trend ([Daily Maverick, 7 Oct 2021](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2021-10-07-wild-north-west-six-killed-in-shootout-after-hundreds-of-zama-zamas-forced-to-surface-in-orkney/), full read).

The bottom of the pyramid is coerced as well as desperate. Men who surfaced at Stilfontein said they were offered casual work at roadside labour spots, including one in Middelburg in Mpumalanga, then forced underground at gunpoint; diggers pay to enter and to leave and buy food underground in gold, which traps them in debt. These are anonymous testimonies collected by one reporter ([GroundUp, 28 Nov 2024](https://groundup.org.za/article/trapped-between-the-law-and-zama-zama-gangs/), full read). Access to working mines is bought: security officials say a "line" of six or seven employees is paid off at about R65,000 to R70,000 a miner, and one mining house catches about 200 employees a quarter ([Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa), full read). That is an aggregate claim; the only named case found is one mine guard arrested in 2017.

### The violence is factional, and its attribution is nearly always disputed

The best-documented events are these. At **Stilfontein** in North West, police blocked shafts and supplies from August 2024; more than 1,000 men surfaced, a court-ordered rescue in January 2025 brought up 78 bodies, and later counts reach 87 to 93 ([ISS Today, 16 Jan 2026](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/stilfontein-mine-disaster-the-deadly-cost-of-information-failure), full read). Police told the Human Rights Commission that most who surfaced were trafficking victims and 27 were children (same source). At **Krugersdorp** in July 2022 eight women were raped at a mine dump; police arrested close to 100 suspected illegal miners, and three months later prosecutors **withdrew all charges against all 14 accused** for insufficient evidence ([The Citizen, 31 Oct 2022](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/krugersdorp-gang-rape-outcry-as-charges-dropped), full read). At **Riverlea** in Johannesburg five bodies followed a weekend of gun battles in July 2023; residents described shooting every night, peaking at month-end when men surface with gold and others wait to rob them ([Daily Maverick, 1 Aug 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-08-01-there-is-shooting-every-night-terrified-riverlea-residents-vow-to-continue-protests-until-police-act-against-zama-zamas/), full read). In 2026, mass shootings at the Jumpers settlement in Johannesburg in June (12 or 13 dead) and at a Wedela tavern near Carletonville in September (18 dead) were tentatively linked by police to mining turf wars; at Wedela the provincial commissioner said **robbery was being investigated but a turf war could not be ruled out** ([Daily Maverick, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-06-10-fingers-pointed-at-illegal-miners-as-police-hunt-cleveland-mass-shooters/), full read; [TimesLIVE, 27 Sept 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/2026-09-27-watch-bloodied-ground-scattered-food-tells-of-wedela-massacre-chaos/)).

The factions trace to Lesotho's accordion-music gangs, Terene and Seakhi, now aligned with mining gangs on the Reef ([Daily Maverick, 26 Mar 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-03-26-the-bloody-threads-that-bind-basotho-gangs-to-zama-zamas-and-famo-musicians-in-remote-mountain-outposts/), full read). Corruption cases exist but are few against the volume of allegation: four Barberton detectives arrested for allegedly stealing R1.8 million in a raid on an illegal-mining "paymaster" ([Business Report, 16 Nov 2024](https://businessreport.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2024-11-16-four-saps-sergeants-languish-behind-bars-after-they-allegedly-stole-r1-8-million-from-zama-zama-boss)), and four officers arrested after an alleged Stilfontein kingpin surfaced and was never booked ([TimesLIVE, 31 Jan 2025](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-01-31-four-cops-arrested-in-connection-with-release-of-alleged-illegal-mining-kingpin-tiger/)). Security officials allege that in North West "entire police stations have been corrupted" and that one mine security firm wrote its own dockets and kept copies in case they went missing; this is allegation, not a court finding ([Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa), full read). Towns answer with road blockades and, in Kagiso in August 2022, with vigilante raids that burned shacks and killed at least one suspected miner (same source).

The state's instrument is Operation Vala Umgodi, a mass-arrest operation that had produced more than 27,000 arrests by July 2025, many for immigration offences ([Mining Weekly, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/operation-vala-umgodi-must-escalate-the-fight-against-illegal-mining-2026-10-27)). The only conviction figure found is 46 found guilty by January 2025, and its original source was not seen. A typical penalty at the bottom is a small fine: a Stilfontein survivor got seven months or R7,000 ([GroundUp, 18 Jun 2025](https://groundup.org.za/article/these-human-bodies-are-like-pork-zama-zama-miner-tells-how-trapped-miners-in-stilfontein-ate-flesh-to-survive/), full read).

### Evander's record is surface digging, small operations and fines

Evander's gold mines make it the one place near Secunda with illegal mining, and the sourced record is thin and low-level. A 2017 feature, which could not be opened, reportedly called the area's disused gold mines "the next hot spot", recorded 197 Lesotho nationals arrested at Evander, eMbalenhle and Osizweni settlements, and quoted police saying there were no open shafts and that miners scratch at surface mounds; fines were R1,200 or two months, and "they pay the fines and come back" ([News24, 13 Aug 2017](https://www.news24.com/fin24/economy/disused-secunda-gold-mines-become-zama-zama-hot-spot-20170813-3)). In June 2018 police arrested 113 around Evander ([Mpumalanga News](https://citizen.co.za/mpumalanga-news/?p=338779)); in July 2019 twelve, in a joint operation by the Secunda cluster stations, crime intelligence and the Secunda dog unit, charged with illegal mining, trespass and immigration offences ([Ridge Times, 25 Jul 2019](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/143122/police-arrest-12-illegal-miners-evander/), full read); and on 5 April 2025 ten people digging near a sports field in eMbalenhle ([Lowvelder, 11 Apr 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2025/04/11/mpumalanga-illegal-mining-cops-crack-down-on-gold-mining-in-schoemanskloof/), full read).

Killing is documented but old. In November 2015 a 39-year-old man mining illegally at Winkelhaak Mine was shot dead by four men who got out of a white Golf; the police spokesman said "the regular shooting in the area must stop", and residents were said to be renting rooms to illegal miners ([Ridge Times, 25 Nov 2015](https://www.citizen.co.za/ridge-times/?p=47997), full read). A 2014 shooting there reportedly killed a teenage miner and wounded five ([Ridge Times, 2014](https://citizen.co.za/ridge-times/?p=16165)). **A deep-shaft siege at Evander would be invention**, borrowed from Stilfontein, Sabie or Barberton. The province's armed, gang-run gold mining is on the Lowveld escarpment: at Sabie in December 2024 police confirmed three dead and six captives rescued from a shaft where unnamed sources said more than 150 were held as forced labour ([Lowvelder, 4 Dec 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/lowvelder/news-headlines/local-news/2024/12/04/zama-zama-bosses-reportedly-keep-more-than-150-as-forced-labourers-in-illegal-sabie-mine/), full read).

## The coal belt's crime runs by the truckload, and the owners never reach the dock

### Swapped loads, forged weighbridge tickets and a laboratory precedent without convictions

The best-documented coal crime is small and repetitive. In November 2022 alone, two ash-haulage drivers were stopped leaving Kendal with stolen coal ([SA News](https://sanews.gov.za/node/60718)); a driver delivering to Camden was arrested after his load was swapped for sub-grade coal at "a known illegal coal yard", and he alleged a mine employee told him to offload at a yard in Middelburg first ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/coal-truck-driver-apprehended-at-eskoms-camden-station-a-week-after-sabotage-arrest-2022-11-23)); and a driver and his supervisor at a subcontracted haulier were arrested at Matla, the driver saying he exchanged the coal at a yard on the supervisor's instruction ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/a-coal-truck-driver-and-his-supervisor-arrested-for-coal-theft-at-matla-power-station/)). Several outlets noted the arrests were made by private or Eskom security, not police, and that the reports derive from Eskom statements. **No outcome was found for any of them.**

The weighbridge is the second pressure point. In September 2023 a driver and two clerks were arrested after the driver produced a ticket from Majuba's weighbridge for a truck that investigators established had never entered the station; the clerks had created a ticket for coal ordered but never received ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/a-coal-truck-driver-and-two-2-weighbridge-clerks-arrested-for-fraud-and-theft-of-coal/)). At Kusile eight weighbridge operators and a driver were arrested for allegedly processing loads never offloaded ([Sunday World](https://sundayworld.co.za/news/nine-suspects-arrested-for-eskom-coal-theft/)). A Camden case in August 2024 began with a routine quality inspection of deliveries ([SA News](https://www.sanews.gov.za/index.ph%70/node/70310)).

The yards are an economy of their own. Police said that in the year to September 2023, 748 coal yards were visited and 63 unregulated ones shut ([Daily Maverick, 21 Nov 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-11-21-r1-5-billion-multi-pronged-asset-seizure-order-sees-authorities-tighten-illegal-coal-mining-noose/), full read), and the head of crime intelligence described one 400-hectare farm holding 28 sub-yards rented to different people ([Daily Maverick, 12 Oct 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-10-12-sars-tip-offs-lead-to-successful-multi-province-coal-smugglers-search-and-seizure-operation/), full read). A tax-authority operation in October 2023 described the method plainly: power-station trucks diverted to yards, good coal replaced with low-grade product "often blended with scrap or other materials", the good coal sold or exported, with former Eskom employees among the suspects. No charges or court outcome were found (same source).

For laboratory fraud the precedent is the Brakfontein affair of 2015 to 2018. Eskom suspended a supplier's coal and two private laboratories after new tests flagged non-compliance, suspended two of its own coal scientists the next day, and reinstated supply within a week without a further test according to a later Treasury forensic report; a 2016 report called the coal "hopelessly non-compliant" ([Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/bd/companies/energy/2016-08-30-tegeta-coal-was-not-up-to-standard-report-finds/); [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2016-08-30-tegeta-coal-quality-hopelessly-noncompliant-says-report/); [National Treasury](https://www.treasury.gov.za/comm_media/press/2018/Final%20Report%20-%20Fundudzi%20-%20Eskom%2015112018.pdf)). It ended in reports and suspensions. **No prosecution of a bribed coal laboratory was found anywhere.**

### One sabotage conviction, with a commercial motive

The single heavy sentence in this field is for sabotage. In November 2022 a maintenance contractor's employee admitted removing a bearing-oil drain plug at Camden, tripping a unit; Eskom said his employer had been promised more repair work if the sabotage was done ([News24](https://www.news24.com/business/economy/maintenance-worker-arrested-for-sabotage-at-eskoms-camden-station-20221116)). On 26 January 2026 the Ermelo court sentenced him to two concurrent terms, the longer **20 years**, for damage put at R22.7 million ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/tampering-essential-infrastructure-met-severe-consequences-eskom/), full read). The proven motive is commercial: break the plant to win the repair.

### The "cartels" are intelligence, not evidence

The claim that four cartels run crime at Eskom comes from private intelligence reports commissioned under former chief executive André de Ruyter. Daily Maverick, which obtained them, quoted its own sources: "What we have here is not evidence. It's intelligence." The reports allege cash-initiated purchases, three quotes from one cartel, bribed guards signing for "ghost deliveries", breakdowns plotted to justify repairs, and sabotage of a coal rail line "to ensure the survival of their coal transport trucking companies". They also allege a political "territorial ruler" controlling a Mpumalanga hit squad, and name 13 provincial police officers as blocking prosecutions; the newspaper withheld the names and said it had found no documentary proof against the alleged ruler ([Daily Maverick, 27 Feb 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-02-27-introducing-the-four-crime-cartels-that-have-brought-eskom-and-south-africa-to-their-knees/), full read). News24 declined to publish the reports as "wild and uncorroborated" ([News24](https://www.news24.com/investigations/reporting-notes-why-news24-did-not-publish-george-fivazs-eskom-intelligence-reports-20230425)); the head of the Special Investigating Unit told Parliament their contents "cannot be ignored" ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/sius-mothibi-says-contents-of-private-eskom-investigation-cannot-be-ignored-2023-09-12)). Eskom's former operations chief told Middelburg businesspeople in May 2023 that "no concrete proof of cartel interference is available", asked "Have we seen anyone in an orange prison overall yet?", and added that "as soon as one cartel falls, another takes its place" ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/assassins-help-mpumalanga-coal-cartels-keep-control/), full read). The honest summary is that the single truckload is proven many times over and the layer that owns the yards, contracts and protection has never been tested in court.

### Business forums borrow a Treasury rule, and no Mpumalanga conviction was found

"Business forums" demand a share, typically **30%**, of contract value. The figure borrows a real Treasury rule on subcontracting 30% of state contracts above R30 million, and Treasury has called the demand illegal ([IOL](https://iol.co.za/business-report/economy/2023-06-29-companies-urged-not-to-make-construction-mafia-business-as-usual/)). The practice is traced to KwaZulu-Natal townships in 2014 to 2015 ([GI-TOC](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/GITOC-Extortion-or-Transformation-The-construction-mafia-in-South-Africa.pdf)) and had reached mining by 2022 ([News24](https://www.news24.com/fin24/Companies/extortion-mafia-hits-sas-r947-billion-mining-sector-20221209)). Between October 2022 and February 2023 business and police identified 143 suspects in incidents at construction sites, mines, bus routes and factories, 15 of the incidents in Mpumalanga and five involving murder; the builders' federation said "for one arrest there might be 20 incidents", and a security-company owner said forum members came to her office "demanding 30% of our business for not one drop of sweat" ([Daily Maverick, 2 Jul 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-07-02-murderous-construction-mafias-have-brought-many-companies-to-their-knees-with-saps-help-the-fightback-has-started), full read). Attempts are often launched under cover of a community protest, and some companies now accommodate forums ([S-RM, 30 Jun 2025](https://www.s-rminform.com/global-risk-hub/something-for-nothing-extortion-in-south-africas-mining-industry)).

The Mpumalanga cases are all arrests or charges. Five suspects were arrested at Ermelo in October 2024 for allegedly extorting almost R100,000 from two businesses at a mine ([Business Report](https://busrep.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/five-suspects-arrested-and-charged-with-extorting-almost-r100000-from-mining-businesses-in-mpumalanga-8f209b59-dc35-4467-a0cc-65fd0717e598)). At Carolina on 9 January 2025 seven men arrived at a mine with a purported court order to stop mining and demanded payment; police checked with the courts, found no such order, and charged fraud and extortion ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/police-arrest-suspects-extortion-carolina-mine-mpumalanga), full read). In 2019 two coal companies obtained an urgent interdict against a **truckers' association blockading a mine entrance**; the mine said it was losing R8 million a day ([HeraldLIVE](https://www.heraldlive.co.za/news/2019-11-04-mine-loses-r8m-a-day-due-to-protests/)).

The most instructive case shows a court failing in front of a powerful accused. In May 2026 a prominent Mpumalanga taxi boss and three others were charged with extortion and money laundering for allegedly taking more than R2.2 million in "protection fees" from a mining businessman between 2022 and 2025; he denies it and says the relationship was legitimate business. The prosecutor did not appear at the bail hearing; the chief magistrate held him in contempt and struck the case from the roll; it is alleged the prosecutor stayed away after threats to his life; and the case was re-enrolled in Delmas "due to safety and security concerns", with bail at R70,000 each. No trial date or verdict was found ([Daily Maverick, 28 May 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-28-joe-ferrari-sibanyoni-granted-bail-questions-remain-over-previous-prosecutor-magistrate/), full read). At the Madlanga Commission in September 2026 the same man conceded receiving R2.29 million from a road-upgrade subcontractor "for doing nothing" while denying extortion; that is untested commission evidence ([Business Day](https://www.businessday.co.za/news/law/2026-09-29-sibanyoni-admits-receiving-r229m-for-doing-nothing-denies-extortion/)).

### Contractors, officials and whistleblowers are shot, and arrests come late or not at all

Hired killing is a documented market. The Global Initiative Against Transnational Organized Crime recorded 141 assassinations in South Africa in 2022 and 131 in 2023; in 2023 about a third were organised-crime related, a third taxi related and a quarter political ([GI-TOC, 2024](https://globalinitiative.net/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Rumbi-Matamba-Chwayita-Thobela-The-politics-of-murder-Criminal-governance-and-targeted-killings-in-SA-GI-TOC-May-2024.pdf)).

The clearest coal-belt case is Mashoza Malaza, who ran a Belfast plant-hire firm that won a contract with a major coal miner in 2021. In February 2023 a gunman with a rifle shot him through his windscreen as he left his driveway with his two young daughters in the car; while he was in hospital his home was bombed with mining explosives. The docket was taken from Belfast police and given to Middelburg detectives. In hiding, he told a local paper he suspected politically connected businessmen and showed leaked messages, which is his allegation. On 2 February 2025 he was shot seven times in front of bystanders in Mhluzi, Middelburg. **No arrests were reported in any of the attacks** ([The Citizen, 7 Feb 2025](https://www.citizen.co.za/network-news/lnn/article/multi-million-rand-mpumalanga-mining-deal-middelburg-businessman-assassinated/), full read). The same papers list earlier Belfast attacks on businessmen from 2018 to 2020. In Middelburg the general manager of an inspection company was shot from a pursuing vehicle in March 2024, and in March 2026 the 62-year-old founder of a local **security company** was found in a field near a weighbridge on the N11, bound and shot four times, by his son, a constable stationed at Kinross; motive unknown, no arrests ([Middelburg Observer, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/middelburg-observer/news/2026/04/08/no-arrests-in-security-business-owners-murder/), full read). At Lydenburg in May 2025 seven men were arrested after an alleged tender-related plot in which armed men stormed a councillor's home and a businessman's, and a police officer who took the complaint was threatened ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/seven-arrested-in-lydenburg-over-alleged-tender-related-attack-on-businessman), full read).

Officials are targets too. A forensic investigator in the Mpumalanga Premier's integrity unit, who was probing officials with allegedly fraudulent qualifications, was shot in September 2023 while driving home to Pretoria; his laptop and investigation documents were taken. He lay in a coma, died in late 2024, and before dying named people he believed were involved. In May 2026 a Tshwane organised-crime unit arrested **two of his own colleagues** on murder and conspiracy charges; they are accused, not convicted. Sources differ on his rank, the number of shots (20 or 29) and the spelling of his name ([Mpumalanga News, 29 May 2026](https://www.citizen.co.za/mpumalanga-news/news-headlines/local-news/2026/05/29/second-mpumalanga-government-official-arrested-on-charges-of-murder/), full read; [The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/crime/two-government-officials-arrested-murder-forensic-investigator/), full read). At Mkhondo in January 2023 a councillor and two others were shot dead; police said the gunman came from KwaZulu-Natal, and a former mayor and a security-company owner were arrested on firearms charges ([IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/news/hitman-in-shocking-mkhondo-political-killing-is-from-kwazulu-natal-mpumalanga-police-say-d82222a5-cdd8-4636-9dde-1bb1023ea40c)). A serving provincial minister was charged with two murders in 2021; the charges were provisionally withdrawn in 2022 and he returned to the provincial cabinet ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandla_Msibi), full read).

The pattern across these cases is that gunmen and drivers are sometimes caught, the payer is not, gaps of two or three years between killing and arrest are normal, and arrests in sensitive cases come from units outside the area.

### Fuel, copper and scrap: guards and police recur among the accused

Fuel theft takes two forms on the Highveld: tapping the Transnet pipeline in the Delmas, Kendal and Emalahleni corridor ([News24, 1 Mar 2020](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/four-suspects-arrested-for-allegedly-stealing-diesel-from-mpumalanga-transnet-pipeline-20200301)) and hijacking tankers, as in the 2026 Bethal case. At Tutuka two Eskom employees and a supplier were charged in 2021 over fuel-oil theft Eskom put at about R100 million a month ([TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2021-11-05-two-eskom-employees-accused-of-theft-of-r100m-worth-of-fuel-oil-per-month-released-on-r5000-bail/)), and its general manager was reported to wear a bulletproof vest on site ([TimesLIVE](https://timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2022-11-24-power-station-boss-wears-bulletproof-vest-after-exposing-corruption-andre-de-ruyter)). Copper cases repeatedly name scrap dealers and the guards posted to protect the line: fourteen arrested in March 2022 included an Emalahleni scrap dealer and security personnel ([Eskom](https://www.eskom.co.za/fourteen-arrested-as-eskom-the-saps-and-the-hawks-serious-organised-crime-unit-swoop-on-a-cable-theft-syndicate-in-mpumalanga/)). In July 2023 a wave of truck burnings hit Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal, most of the trucks carrying coal and chrome; the police minister said evidence pointed to organised attacks ([gov.za](https://www.gov.za/news/media-statements/police-arrests-two-truck-drives-torching-trucks-13-jul-2023)). Outcomes were not found for any of these.

### Illegal coal mining is a business that lacks only the licence

The landmark case is at Carolina. Police raided an unlicensed open-cast mine in September 2023 and arrested the manager; prosecutors say about 270,000 tons, worth some R264 million, were mined over two years with no mining or environmental authorisation ([Daily Maverick, 22 Sept 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-09-22-mpumalanga-police-seize-mining-equipment-worth-r60m-during-crackdown-on-illegal-coal-mine/), full read). A **R1.5 billion preservation order** followed in November 2023 over the farm, stockpiles, machines and a separate wash plant, and R52.5 million was seized from ten Namibian bank accounts in January 2025; the case was transferred to the Mbombela High Court for trial in November 2025 ([Daily Maverick, 20 Jan 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-01-20-npa-seizes-r52-5m-in-illegal-mining-proceeds-in-namibia-in-time-to-tick-greylisting-box/), full read). Whether the trial began is unknown. Asset forfeiture and tax routes move faster than criminal trials. At the other end of the scale, 271 people were arrested around Ermelo's abandoned pits in October 2022 ([News24](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/mpumalanga-police-arrest-271-suspected-illegal-miners-20221015)).

The regulator's record is murky. The mining department's Mpumalanga office was closed from September 2018 for about a year amid corruption allegations, and the minister would not say on reopening whether the probe had produced any result ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/mantashe-mum-on-corruption-probe-after-mpumalanga-office-reopens-2019-08-06-1)). No arrest of a regional licensing official was found. In 2022 a provincial police spokesperson was quoted confirming a station commander "caught red-handed" taking a R500,000 bribe from an illegal coal-mining syndicate, while the Hawks denied arresting any police; the facts remain in conflict ([Business Report, 20 Sept 2022](https://businessreport.co.za/pretoria-news/news/2022-09-20-zama-zama-crimes-alleged-cover-up-cause-rift-among-mpumalanga-top-cops)).

### Environmental prosecutions land on municipalities and stall against industry

Criminal enforcement exists but is lopsided. Emalahleni municipality was fined **R650 million** in November 2025 after pleading guilty to water and environmental offences ([TimesLIVE](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-11-14-emalahleni-municipality-fined-for-water-mismanagement/)). Against Sasol the record is delay. A former process controller complained in May 2019 that chemicals were discharged from a plant unit into stormwater and on toward the Vaal system; the docket reached prosecutors in December 2021; Sasol was summoned in July 2022; the prosecutor **provisionally withdrew all charges on the morning of trial** in July 2025; and on 4 August 2026 investigators executed fresh warrants at Secunda, seizing electronic records and sampling the chemical sewer, while Sasol executives were addressing a parliamentary committee on site. Sasol denies wrongdoing and says its clean and dirty water circuits are separate ([Daily Maverick, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-08-04-npa-hits-sasol-with-fresh-warrants-seven-years-after-chemical-contamination-complaint/), full read). The whistleblower has described being afraid ([Mail & Guardian, 27 Mar 2021](https://mg.co.za/the-green-guardian/2021-03-27-it-s-absolutely-terrifying-to-be-a-whistleblower-in-sasol/)). Eskom was summoned in November 2020 over Kendal's emissions, including a count of **supplying false or misleading information** to an air-quality officer, after its own forensic investigation found exceedances misclassified in reports to regulators; the outcome was not found ([Daily Maverick, 29 Nov 2020](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2020-11-29-eskom-served-with-summons-for-criminal-prosecution-on-charges-of-air-pollution/), full read). Abandoned pits kill: nearly 800 abandoned mines were counted in Mpumalanga in 2019, and children have drowned in an unrehabilitated pit at Ermelo whose rehabilitation provision was only R600,000 ([Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/report/2022/07/05/forever-mines/perpetual-rights-risks-unrehabilitated-coal-mines-south-africa)).

### Mine-sale fraud has adjacent precedents but no proven overstated-reserves case

No South African case was found in which a buyer proved a seller overstated coal reserves, or in which a regulator disciplined the signing professional. The nearest precedents are the hollowing-out of the Optimum and Koornfontein rehabilitation trusts, over which a court moved the funds to safekeeping in 2018 and three people including a former senior mining official were arrested in 2022 ([Daily Maverick, 25 May 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-05-25-two-gupta-acolytes-and-ex-mineral-resources-top-official-arrested-over-r175bn-pillaging-of-mine-trust-funds/)); a 2004 dispute between two gold companies over inferred material counted as reserves ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001172724/000095012304013549/u48041a5exv99waw38.htm)); and stock-exchange censures of junior miners for disclosure failures ([Mining Weekly, 6 Mar 2026](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/jse-censures-mantengu-for-not-disclosing-2023-confidential-information-leak-2026-03-06)). The defensible frauds around a coal asset are therefore an understated rehabilitation liability and flattering quality results, not a faked borehole database.

## Other mining towns show five different patterns

This section is extract-only throughout, except where a fact also appears in the full reads cited above.

**The platinum belt's signature is the unsolved union killing.** About ten people, including two security guards and two police officers, were killed in the four days before police shot 34 strikers at Marikana on 16 August 2012 ([SA History Online](https://sahistory.org.za/article/marikana-massacre-16-august-2012)). As of May 2022 no police officer had been charged for the 16 August shootings ([Daily Maverick, 19 May 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-05-19-marikana-a-massacre-still-without-any-criminal-consequences/)), and families were still calling for accountability on the fourteenth anniversary ([EWN, 16 Aug 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/08/16/marikana-massacre-14-years-on-victims-families-still-wait-for-justice)). Targeted shootings of union figures followed for years, at taverns, shaft gates and homes: a union organiser shot at a Rustenburg tavern in May 2013, four regional leaders found shot within about two weeks in September 2017, each union blaming the other, and arrests rare ([Business Day, 27 Sept 2017](https://www.businesslive.co.za/bd/national/2017-09-27-marikana-death-and-fear-still-stalk-platinum-belt/)). The same landscape holds debt and sexual violence. Marikana miners borrowed from informal lenders who held their identity books or bank cards, and went home with nothing on payday after court-ordered wage deductions ([Daily Maverick, 19 Nov 2013](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2013-11-19-fraudulent-garnishee-orders-sas-poorest-of-the-poor/)); a 2015 household survey in Rustenburg found one woman in four had been raped ([MSF](https://www.msf.org/south-africa-sexual-violence-platinum-mining-belt-major-driver-hiv)). Royalties are a third strand: a Public Protector report found more than R255 million in irregular payments from one community's mining account ([TimesLIVE, 20 Jun 2017](https://www.timeslive.co.za/amp/news/2017-06-20-where-did-the-millions-go/)).

**Limpopo's eastern limb runs on chrome, blockades and community money.** Illegal chrome digging near Burgersfort has been open and mechanised since about 2016 ([Sunday Times, 26 Jun 2016](https://www.timeslive.co.za/sunday-times/news/2016-06-26-millions-stolen-in-illegal-chrome-rush/)). Residents camped outside a chrome plant for months in 2023 ([Daily Maverick, 19 May 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-05-19-fight-goes-on-vow-protesters-at-limpopo-chrome-mine)); fourteen trucks were burned in three days that July ([IOL, 11 Jul 2023](https://iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2023-07-11-look-three-trucks-burnt-in-limpopo-bringing-total-to-14-trucks-burnt-within-three-days/)); six people blockaded about 22 mine trucks at Driekop in May 2025, demanding payment before loading ([S-RM](https://www.s-rminform.com/global-risk-hub/something-for-nothing-extortion-in-south-africas-mining-industry)); eleven forum members were arrested at a Sekhukhune mine gate in November 2024 ([SA News](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/71976)); and in July 2026 two men, one an off-duty police officer, were shot dead in a dispute over a mine's ownership ([IOL, 22 Jul 2026](https://iol.co.za/news/crime-and-courts/2026-07-22-gauteng-police-officer-among-two-shot-dead-at-limpopo-mine-manhunt-launched/)). A figure of more than 70 illegal-mining deaths on one corridor rests on a single extract and cannot be interpreted.

**KwaZulu-Natal and the Wild Coast show three motives in one landscape.** Anti-mining activists were killed at Xolobeni in 2016 and Somkhele in 2020, with no arrests in any report found; in the Xolobeni case an investigation later reported an allegation that a senior Hawks officer tried to shut the inquiry down ([Daily Maverick, 9 May 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-05-09-murder-of-bazooka-radebe-in-xolobeni-hawks-general-accused-of-shutting-down-investigation)). At Richards Bay Minerals a general manager was shot dead on his way to work in May 2021, and by 2024 the company's managing director attributed the assassinations to procurement and supply-chain contracts ([Daily Maverick, 3 Mar 2024](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2024-03-03-cowboys-and-crooks-violent-mafias-are-holding-richards-bay-industry-to-ransom/)). Opposition to a mine, control of trust money and access to supplier contracts each produce killings, and investigators confuse them easily.

**The gold towns show what closure does.** After the Blyvooruitzicht mine was liquidated in 2013, residents lost services and illegal miners moved in ([Sunday Times, 29 Jan 2017](https://www.businesslive.co.za/bt/business-and-economy/2017-01-30-from-gold-town-to-ghost-town/)). In Welkom's townships every street lamp on one main road was cut down for scrap, the municipality was reported R3.5 billion in debt, and three mine managers were attacked, kidnapped or murdered within about a year ([TimesLIVE, 4 Dec 2017](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2017-12-04-mine-closures-r35bn-debt-crisis-puts--municipality-on-brink-of-collapse/); [Sunday Times, 18 Nov 2018](https://www.timeslive.co.za/sunday-times/news/2018-11-18-mines-wage-war-with-zama-zamas/)). In Khutsong six people were killed by a mob in November 2013 after residents said gangs were recruiting in schools ([Daily Maverick, 7 Nov 2013](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2013-11-07-anatomy-of-the-khutsong-horror-when-rampant-crime-met-mob-justice.md)). The murder rate in the Free State goldfields reached 55 per 100,000 in 2019 ([Daily Maverick, 7 Aug 2022](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2022-08-07-zama-zamas-just-one-part-of-a-multibillion-rand-organised-crime-economy-that-threatens-sa), full read).

**The Northern Cape is boom-town volume crime and quiet diamonds.** A provincial official tied rising crime to illegal mining at Kimberley and to the influx and taverns that came with mine expansion at Kuruman ([Diamond Fields Advertiser, 26 May 2023](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/diamond-fields-advertiser/20230526/281535115360849)). Illegal diamond digging on the Namaqualand coast is policed by repeated mass-arrest raids ([DFA, 16 Mar 2025](https://dfa.co.za/diamond-fields-advertiser/news/2025-03-16-police-crack-down-on-illegal-mining-in-kleinzee-six-foreign-nationals-detained)). No high-profile killings and no dealer prosecution surfaced.

One finding ties these together. In April to June 2024 all thirty of the country's highest-murder stations were in the Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal, Eastern Cape and Gauteng; none was in North West, Limpopo, the Free State or the Northern Cape ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/lifestyle/813606/south-africas-murder-nightmare/)). A mining town can look statistically ordinary while a handful of unsolved, contract-style killings decide who holds local power.

## A murder docket has a one-in-nine chance of an arrest

### Detection, caseload and the laboratory queue

South Africa recorded 24,692 murders in 2024/25, down from a peak of 27,621 the year before; Mpumalanga's count fell by double digits in two quarters of 2025 ([Daily Maverick, 6 Dec 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-06-decline-in-murders-brings-little-consolation-for-victims-families/), full read), though in April to June 2026 it was one of two provinces where murder rose ([eNCA](https://www.enca.com/news/murder-rate-down-between-april-and-june-2026)). The same article quotes an Institute for Security Studies analyst: police **murder detection stood at 11% in 2024** and the murder conviction rate at 13% over five years. "Detection" means at least one suspect identified and arrested. The much higher conviction rates sometimes quoted count only cases that reached court ([Daily Maverick, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-15-only-15-of-saps-dna-samples-are-processed-within-target-time/), full read). No Mpumalanga-specific rate was found.

The Auditor-General told Parliament in October 2026 that **some detectives handle 600 to 800 dockets**, that more than a third of the detectives it counted had five years' service or less, that forensic laboratories spent their full budget and met a third of their targets, and that DNA robots stood idle for want of tubes; it warned of withdrawals "because of unavailable evidence" ([Daily Maverick, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-02-800-dockets-for-one-detective-undermined-forensic-services-auditor-generals-critical-saps-findings/), full read). A police union calls 20 to 30 serious cases a manageable load ([IOL, 11 Jun 2026](https://iol.co.za/thepost/news/2026-06-11-overworked-and-underpaid-the-struggles-of-south-african-detectives/)). Only **1.5% of DNA profiles** generated in 2025/26 were finalised within the prescribed 30 days, with nearly a quarter of a million exhibits outside the deadline ([Daily Maverick, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-10-15-only-15-of-saps-dna-samples-are-processed-within-target-time/), full read). The head of police ballistics testified to a backlog of **41,846 cases**, and said it took more than a year to link five firearms to nearly 30 serious crimes ([Daily Maverick, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-10-29-saps-ballistics-backlog-imperils-criminal-cases-and-undermines-trust-in-police), full read). Toxicology, run by the health service, has its own backlog reported at about 39,000 cases ([Medical Brief](https://www.medicalbrief.co.za/?p=177816)).

Lost dockets are ambiguous in practice. The police told Parliament they do not record why courts dispose of matters and cannot link missing dockets to outcomes; lost dockets are reconstructed ([PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/40785/)). From outside, corruption and disorder look the same. Mpumalanga's worked example is the 2022 murder of Hillary Gardee near Mbombela: her family accused police of ignoring footage of the suspects' number plate, a political party hired private investigators, and in April 2023 charges against four accused were provisionally withdrawn because the state could not find three witnesses, one of whom police located hours later ([News24, 13 Apr 2023](https://www.news24.com/southafrica/news/hillary-gardee-murder-one-of-3-missing-witnesses-found-hours-after-case-withdrawn-20230413)). The family's claims are allegations. The consequence for any quick case in 2026 is that it moves on phones, vehicles, cameras, money and a cooperating insider, and it collapses on witnesses.

### Commercial crime: a 90% conviction rate on very few cases

Fraud runs through a crowded field: station and provincial detectives, the Hawks, the prosecution service's Specialised Commercial Crime Unit and its courts, the Asset Forfeiture Unit, the Financial Intelligence Centre, a prosecution-led corruption directorate created in 2019 and made permanent in 2024, and the Special Investigating Unit, which acts **only on a presidential proclamation** and pursues civil recovery in state bodies ([EWN, 17 Jul 2026](https://www.ewn.co.za/2026/07/17/explainer-understanding-the-roles-of-south-africas-key-crime-fighting-and-justice-agencies)). A purely private mining fraud cannot go to that unit. The prosecutors claim a 90.2% conviction rate in complex commercial matters, and it is true of the cases they take. But more than 128,000 commercial crimes were reported in 2023/24 against about 15,000 arrests, convictions per specialised court fell from about 76 a year in 2018 to about 15 in 2023, and convictions per specialised prosecutor fell from six a year to 1.5. The prosecution service itself blames "investigative delays and inconsistent docket quality" ([GroundUp](https://groundup.org.za/article/npa-reports-decline-in-corruption-money-laundering-convictions/), full read). The implication is that a well-documented private forensic file materially raises the chance a fraud is enrolled at all. Cases typically surface through an internal auditor, a whistleblower, a liquidator or an integrity-unit investigator, and that is also the list of people who have been killed.

### Police integrity after the Madlanga Commission

The police watchdog has about 170 investigators and a backlog above 14,000 cases; deaths at the hands of police reached 670 in 2024/25, the highest since records began in 1997 ([Daily Maverick, 26 Oct 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-10-26-alarming-surge-in-police-killings-highlight-leadership-chaos-and-deep-flaws-in-law-enforcement/), full read). Survey data cited by the Institute for Security Studies put public trust in the police at **27%** since 2021 ([ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/south-africa-must-rethink-its-community-safety-system), full read).

On 6 July 2025 KwaZulu-Natal's police commissioner alleged that the police minister had ordered a political-killings task team disbanded to shield politically connected members of a criminal syndicate. The minister denies it. A judicial commission under Justice Madlanga and a parliamentary committee have since heard sworn evidence, including an in-camera account of a "Big Five" cartel and claims of payments to senior officers; **all of this is testimony, not findings** ([Daily Maverick, 14 Dec 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-14-how-mkhwanazis-allegations-against-mchunu-stack-up-almost-six-months-later/), full read). One alleged cartel figure named in testimony, now dead, had a brother who served as a party treasurer in Mpumalanga ([Daily Maverick, 4 Sept 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-09-04-faces-of-the-cartel-emerge-in-the-madlanga-commissions-big-five-reveal/), full read). Arrests have followed, all of them charges: the national commissioner and twelve senior officials over a R360 million health contract (a later article says the commissioner was "subsequently cleared"), a crime-intelligence general and a Hawks provincial head over alleged unlawful possession of precious metals, and a suspended organised-crime sergeant at whose home police found ammunition, a stun grenade, **stolen dockets** and cash ([Daily Maverick, 12 May 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-12-top-cops-in-the-dock-heres-the-full-list-of-madlanga-related-arrests), full read). The chain the commission heard about began with an engineer shot 23 times in April 2024, apparently by mistake, after his firm flagged inflated tender prices; the alleged gunman was a serving police officer moonlighting as a hitman, and the trial is pending ([Daily Maverick, 14 Dec 2025](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2025-12-14-how-mkhwanazis-allegations-against-mchunu-stack-up-almost-six-months-later/), full read). A commission witness, a security-company owner and former police official, was shot dead with a rifle at his gate on 5 December 2025 after testifying about a death under torture; a former police special-forces member was arrested for it in March 2026 ([Daily Maverick, 12 May 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-05-12-top-cops-in-the-dock-heres-the-full-list-of-madlanga-related-arrests), full read).

### Private investigators and private security have a citizen's powers

Private investigation is regulated as a security service: investigator and firm must both be registered with the industry regulator, and hiring an unregistered one is an offence ([ProtectionWeb](https://www.protectionweb.co.za/opinion-and-analysis/private-investigations-regulated-as-security-service/)). A party to a conversation may record it; planting a recorder in a room one is not in is unlawful interception ([Cliffe Dekker Hofmeyr, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2026/South-Africa/Dispute-Resolution/dispute-resolution-alert-24-february-Big-brother-in-the-boardroom-Is-bugging-the-office-lawful-under-RICA)). A university study records that courts accept the working model: the investigator builds a file and hands it to police, who make the arrest ([UCT](https://open.uct.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/7c6fb767-0b4a-46fa-bf3e-a39a37a73ff2/content)). A civil-rights group's private prosecution unit works the same seam, pressing prosecutors and seeking leave to prosecute privately when they decline ([The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/explainer-how-south-africas-first-private-prosecutions-unit-will-work-72389)).

Security officers have no police powers and may only make a citizen's arrest ([PMG](https://pmg.org.za/committee-question/39494/)). In practice they are first on many scenes, and one study found scene contamination more likely from small, less-trained firms than large ones ([UKZN](https://researchspace.ukzn.ac.za/items/9a9ac843-6081-4e88-90d9-aefab169948d)). The industry is also a vehicle for crime. The Institute for Security Studies reports more than 7,000 new security companies registered since 2020, criminal figures acquiring firms for legitimacy, tenders, intimidation and **legal firearms**, a task team linking security-company firearms to crimes, and 138 firearms a month lost by or stolen from security companies against about 55 from the police ([ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/criminal-links-of-south-africa-s-private-security-companies-also-require-scrutiny), full read).

### Contract killing: tens of thousands of rand, and the payer stays hidden

Assassination research describes a market rooted in the minibus-taxi industry, which "provides a recruitment pool of hitmen for hire", alongside gangs and serving or former police and soldiers; taxi bosses in Gauteng often hire gunmen from KwaZulu-Natal ([ISS/ENACT](https://issafrica.org/enactafrica/enact-observer/tracking-the-trajectory-of-targeted-killings-in-south-africa), full read). Police statistics have no category for targeted killings. Fees stated in court in recent rural cases are **R75,000**, allegedly paid by a traditional leader for a councillor's killing ([TimesLIVE, 1 Sept 2025](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-09-01-court-hears-how-kzn-chief-paid-hitman-r75000-to-kill-da-councillor/)), and **R60,000**, allegedly promised by a victim's wife; in that case the gunman was sentenced to 30 years and the intermediary is to testify for the state ([The Citizen](https://www.citizen.co.za/news/south-africa/courts/hitman-jailed-30-years-murder-plot-allegedly-orchestrated-victims-wife/)). The first is an allegation; the second is a conviction of the gunman only.

The benchmark case is Babita Deokaran, a Gauteng health official shot outside her home in August 2021 after flagging suspicious hospital payments. Six men, all linked to the taxi industry, pleaded guilty in August 2023 and received 6 to 22 years; the shooter and whoever ordered the killing remained at large ([Daily Maverick, 22 Aug 2023](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2023-08-22-babita-deokaran-killers-plead-guilty-and-get-jail-terms-but-the-shooter-and-assassination-mastermind-remain-at-large/)). In April 2026 Daily Maverick counted at least ten professionals killed in three years with work-related motives suspected and **no mastermind charged**. The pattern it describes: killings in public or semi-public places, nothing taken, signs of prior surveillance, a coordinated escape and inside information. A researcher said "often, the hitmen themselves don't know who the mastermind is", and that in business disputes "it is easier and quicker to find and commission a hitman than to go through formal legal processes". One victim's family said police did not contact them for six weeks ([Daily Maverick, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2026-04-15-10-professionals-assassinated-in-three-years-zero-masterminds-arrested/), full read). A municipal forensic auditor shot in June 2025, days after reporting on a R1.8 billion tender, was reportedly tracked from the moment he left home ([Human Rights Watch, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/02/24/south-africa-whistleblowers-under-attack)).

### Informal justice kills hundreds; journalists are threatened, not killed

Police motive data attribute 299 murders in the most recent release to vigilantism or mob justice, **32 of them in Mpumalanga**; sources disagree on whether that covers a quarter or a year ([TimesLIVE, 25 Sept 2026](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-09-25-mob-justice-under-spotlight-after-man-is-killed-over-kempton-park-murders/)). Community policing forums are described as often most dysfunctional in the poorest, worst-affected areas, and some as captured by political or criminal interests ([ISS Today](https://issafrica.org/iss-today/south-africa-must-rethink-its-community-safety-system), full read). A person named locally as a suspect can be killed before any arrest.

South Africa ranked 21st of 180 on the 2026 press-freedom index ([RSF](https://rsf.org/en/country/south-africa)). The documented threat to reporters is intimidation, online smears, phoned threats and obstruction, for example an editors' forum condemning a businessman's threats against a reporter seeking comment ([TimesLIVE, 20 May 2025](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2025-05-20-sanef-condemns-threats-to-journalists-who-work-professionally-and-ethically/)). **No case of a journalist or community broadcaster killed over a local corruption story in Mpumalanga or a mining town was found for 2018 to 2026.**

## The main gaps and unverified points

**Source depth.** The Secunda-area file and the other-mining-towns file contain no full reads at all. Every local statistic, every local murder outcome except the councillor case, every plant-fraud case except the wastewater matter, and the whole of the platinum, chrome, KwaZulu-Natal, gold-town and Northern Cape material rest on extracts. No court judgment was read; "sentenced" means a newspaper or the prosecution service said so.

**Local statistics.** No official per-category counts were obtained for Secunda, eMbalenhle, Evander, Kinross, Bethal or Leandra. The two repackaged totals for eMbalenhle disagree (2,414 and 1,805), and the 47-murder figure comes from the less transparent of them. Whether Trichardt and Kinross have their own stations is unconfirmed. There are no figures at all for Bethal or Leandra.

**A local claim contradicted by a full read.** One extract attributed to a Ridge Times page a report that six illegal miners were killed in a clash between rival groups at Evander. That page, read in full by another researcher, is the July 2019 report of twelve arrests and contains no such killing. The six-deaths claim is unverified and is not used above. No Evander illegal-mining incident dated 2022 to 2026 was found other than the April 2025 eMbalenhle arrests, and the main Evander source (2017) could not be opened.

**Outcomes missing.** No verdict was found for: any of the eMbalenhle police-accused cases; the Secunda constable's double-murder charge; the Flying Squad sergeant; the R279 million and R400 million Sasol-related frauds or the actuator case; any coal-swap, weighbridge or fuel-oil arrest; the Kusile prosecutions; the Kendal emissions prosecution; the Carolina illegal-mining trial; the taxi-boss extortion case; the Premier's-office murder charges; the Lydenburg plot; or the Gardee prosecution. Whether Sasol's environmental charges were reinstated after September 2026 is unknown.

**Things looked for and not found.** No sabotage, coal or diesel syndicate, forum extortion or contract killing tied to Sasol Secunda or its mines. No Secunda-area loan-shark, job-selling, pyramid or share-scheme case. No named local gangs or drug cases. No prosecution of a coal laboratory, of a licensing official for bribery, or of empowerment fronting in a coal right. No disciplinary case against a professional who signed a resource report. No Mpumalanga extortion conviction. No official conviction rate for illegal-mining arrests. No statistics on registered private investigators or on enforcement against them. Nothing on traditional courts or street committees in Mpumalanga.

**Conflicts and date hazards.** The date of the Middelburg contractor's killing is 2 February 2025 in the article read in full; a separate extract pointed to March 2024, which is the date of a different Middelburg shooting. The Premier's-office victim's rank, the shot count and the spelling of his name differ between sources. Detective headcounts conflict (22,413 in one parliamentary reply; about 17,600 in another report; the Auditor-General's 3,294 is evidently a subset). Stilfontein's death toll is a range from 78 to 93 or more. The vigilante-murder figure of 299 is described as quarterly by most sources and annual by one, and a separate count gives 431 "over the past year". The first eMbalenhle officers' case has four accused in the watchdog's statement and five in the bail report.

**Statute law is unverified.** The researchers could not confirm from a source the detail of several rules a writer will want: the duty on people in authority to report corruption and fraud above R100,000 to the Hawks, and whether a proposed cut to R30,000 has been enacted; the bail test for premeditated murder; the terms of citizen's arrest and force in arrest; how police obtain phone and bank records by subpoena; the plea-agreement and accomplice-witness provisions; trespass and data-protection limits on investigators. These are stated here only as matters to check with a lawyer, not as findings. The role of the local criminal record centre at a scene, the mortuary's turnaround and witness-protection numbers are likewise unsourced.

**Whole subjects not sourced.** How police use cell-tower data, number-plate cameras, vehicle trackers and firearm records in a murder case; hit prices from the main assassination research (the reports could not be opened); the structure of chrome-theft syndicates; pension or retrenchment-payout fraud in mining towns; the final reports of the Madlanga Commission and the Human Rights Commission's illegal-mining inquiry.

## What the findings imply for the story

Nothing below describes a real Secunda event. Every element is an invention laid over a documented pattern, and wherever the story attributes wrongdoing it should use invented firms, forums, lenders, laboratories and people, not the real companies, associations and individuals in the sources.

### A personal murder first read as a robbery

This is the most strongly supported element. Secunda's documented murders are personal: a husband, a boyfriend, a house guest. The 2026 wife-killing shows a killer cleaning the scene and concealing the body, and police catching him because he ran. House robbery is common enough that it is the natural first reading of a body in a suburban house, and the 2019 pensioner case is exactly that crime. Real dockets carry both labels at once: the councillor's killing was opened as murder and armed robbery because his pistol was taken, and at Wedela police investigated robbery while saying a turf war could not be excluded. The misreading would not be corrected by the laboratory, given the 1.5% on-time DNA figure and the year-long ballistics links. It would be corrected, if at all, by what the local record shows closes cases: a confession, a flight, a phone, a camera, a witness who stays available. The earlier setting report's point that such a death can open as an inquest docket and wait on a post-mortem still fits. The story should not show a forensic breakthrough solving it quickly.

### A coal-quality fraud at an invented family colliery

The mechanics are all precedented, and all at Eskom, not Sasol: load swapping at a yard, weighbridge tickets for coal never delivered, sampling and laboratory results that flatter the product, scientists suspended for objecting. The setting report notes that Sasol's own quality problem is stone in the feed and that it has bought outside coal since late 2021, which gives an invented supplier a real market. Detection should come from a mundane mismatch, as in the real cases: a gate log against a weighbridge record, a routine quality inspection, an internal audit like the one that caught the actuator supplier, or a tax anomaly. The realistic path is then the Hawks' commercial-crime unit in Secunda, a first appearance at Evander or Secunda, and years of postponement; the Special Investigating Unit is not available for a private company. Three limits apply. No laboratory has been prosecuted, so a bribed lab is plausible invention without a case behind it. No overstated-reserves case exists, so if the colliery is being sold the defensible lies concern its rehabilitation liability and its product quality. And nothing documented shows such a fraud at or against Sasol, so the buyer's staff must be invented and not implied to be real.

### An armed-response firm

The record supports the firm in three roles at once. It is first on the scene and makes the arrest in the suburbs, with only a citizen's powers, and its control-room logs, cameras and vehicle tracks exist before any police docket does. It is a possible contaminant of the scene, more so if small. And nationally the industry is a documented route for criminal figures to acquire legitimacy, contracts and licensed guns, and a source of lost firearms. Guards appear as insiders in the 2021 Sasol theft and the Tutuka cable case, and a Secunda guard was convicted of murder. Security-company owners also appear as victims: the Middelburg founder found bound and shot in 2026, and the commission witness killed at his gate. A forum demanding 30% of a security firm's business is documented in a first-person quote. The firm in the story can therefore be honest, compromised or targeted without straining the evidence, but no misconduct by a real Secunda firm was found and none should be suggested.

### A haulier-led business forum

A forum led by truckers is well grounded: a truckers' association blockaded a coal mine in 2019 at a claimed cost of R8 million a day, trucks were held at a Limpopo loading point until payment, coal trucks were burned in 2023, a taxi boss stands charged over "protection fees" from a mine operator, and the intelligence reports allege rail sabotage to keep coal on trucks. The forum should present itself as it does in life, as local empowerment owed under a mine's social obligations, so that the same meeting reads as grievance and shakedown; a forged court order is a documented tool. A small family colliery that trucks its coal is exactly the kind of operation exposed to this. Two cautions: no forum extortion is documented at Secunda, where the only recorded event is a lawful march for procurement access, and no Mpumalanga forum case has ended in conviction. If the story shows the forum's leader in court, the Kwaggafontein sequence, with an absent prosecutor and a case moved to another town for safety, is the documented picture.

### An informal lender

Lenders who hold borrowers' bank cards, grant cards and identity books are documented across Mpumalanga and were raided by the Hawks in Mbombela in 2024; the credit regulator says the practice is illegal ([Mpumalanga News, 21 May 2024](https://www.citizen.co.za/mpumalanga-news/news-headlines/local-news/2024/05/21/update-hawks-raid-alleged-mbombela-loan-sharks-offices-confiscate-id-books-and-bank-cards/)). The Marikana material shows what it does to a mining workforce: wages gone on payday and debt as a credible motive for desperate acts. No Secunda or eMbalenhle case was found, so the lender is transplanted, plausibly. The lender is a natural keeper of other people's cards, pay dates and secrets, and month-end is a documented pressure point. Nothing in the research links a lender to a killing, so that connection is the story's own.

### An overloaded detective

The eMbalenhle figures (about 150 dockets each, fewer than half the vehicles running, no holding cells) and the national ones (600 to 800 dockets for some, an 11% detection rate) make an honest detective who cannot work a case properly the realistic default. Residents told to trace suspects themselves, a family not contacted for six weeks, and a witness the state "cannot find" who turns up within hours are all documented. The more interesting question the record raises is whether the detective's failures are overload or interference, which cannot be told apart from outside; missing dockets are reconstructed and never counted. Resourcing at the Secunda station itself is unknown, so placing the heaviest load at eMbalenhle and something lighter but thinner in Secunda town is an inference. A Murder and Robbery Unit based in Secunda exists and has arrested police.

### A later contract killing

A second killing done for money fits the regional record, but one district away: the nearest documented hits on business figures are in Belfast and Middelburg, and the eMbalenhle councillor case is the only local shooting with that shape. The research gives the texture. The killing is public, nothing is taken, the victim was watched, the gunmen are outsiders, often from KwaZulu-Natal, drawn from the taxi world or with police or military backgrounds. A fee of R60,000 to R75,000 is consistent across two unrelated court reports; nothing supports a figure for a corporate hit. The first people caught are the spotters and drivers, they plead, and the intermediary is the only door to the payer. Arrests come years later and from an outside unit. Realism therefore argues against a final arrest of the payer by the local station, and for an ending in which the shooter's layer is identified and the person who paid is known to the protagonist but not, or not yet, to a court. The contrast with the first murder is usable: a robbery scene implies things taken, while a hit is marked by nothing being taken.

### What to keep out, or treat as invention

A deep-shaft illegal-mining siege at Evander, a named cartel operating at Sasol, a journalist or radio presenter murdered over a tender story, a bribed licensing official shown as proven, and a laboratory convicted of falsifying results all go beyond the record. Each can appear as rumour, fear or allegation inside the story, which is how most of them exist in the sources.

## Conclusion

The research changes the expected picture in one main way. The dramatic crimes of the coal belt and the daily crimes of Secunda are not the same crimes and are not in the same place. Secunda's own record is domestic murder, opportunistic robbery, audit-detected supplier fraud and a township police station under arrest by its own watchdog. The trucks, yards, forums and gunmen belong to the Witbank and Middelburg world, and they enter a Secunda story through a haulage contract, which is the one real commercial link between a small colliery and that world.

The second lesson is about what is known at all. In this field the proven facts are small (one truckload, one forged ticket, one drain plug, six men in a getaway car) and everything above them is allegation that has sat for years: intelligence reports no court has tested, commission testimony without findings, prosecutions withdrawn on the morning of trial. A story that is faithful to this does not end with the structure exposed. It ends with one layer proved, the next layer named but untouched, and the investigator deciding who can be trusted to hold the file.
